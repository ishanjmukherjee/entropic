Daddy pumped Sinclair at Carver's Ferry,
Green tin dinosaur up on a pole,
Two men from the Authority came sighting
A tripod up and down the river road.
They shot the grade at Kessler's Feed and Seed,
And Mama's kitchen floor at eight-eighteen,
They gave her forty dollars for the walnut
And a pamphlet with the valley painted green.

Mark it down at eight-twenty,
Paint it orange on the wall,
Everything below the line
Goes to the river, house and all.

In March a crew from Knoxville dug the churchyard,
Three men to a grave and a truck to a row,
Grandma Ada rode up to the ridge in pine
With a number on the lid where her name should go.
They set her by the new road with the others
On a stone the government had paid,
They'd cut it ADDIE where it should say ADA,
And Daddy took his hat off just the same.

Mark it down at eight-twenty,
Paint it orange on the wall,
Everything below the line
Goes to the river, house and all.

Daddy drained the last Ethyl in the Ford truck,
The church bell went to Dalton for ninety and a ham,
Now the bulb above our sink up on the ridge
Comes down a wire from the Elk Fork Dam.
Mama'd leave it burning after midnight,
Wouldn't let him reach and pull the chain,
She'd sit there with her coffee and the radio
Like she was waiting on the Ferry road to drain.

Mark it down at eight-twenty,
Paint it orange on the wall,
Everything below the line
Goes to the river, house and all.

In the dry fall of eighty-eight they drew it down
Till the old road surfaced like a seam,
I walked out past the stumps in Daddy's boots
And found the pump island, cracked and clean.
The dinosaur was gone but there's the pole,
And a grape Nehi bottle in the clay,
I stood up on the step to Mama's kitchen
Till the rain come in on Thursday and it all went back away.

Mark me down at eight-twenty,
Not up on the ridge at all,
Everything below the line
Goes to the river, house and all.
