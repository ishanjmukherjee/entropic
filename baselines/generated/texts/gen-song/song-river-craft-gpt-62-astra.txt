At Nora's till on Quarry Street
a man unrolled a county map.
His pencil took the river bend
and laid it flat across her lap.
She marked the spot above her head
with chalk they used for price of eggs.

How high's the water, Nora Bell?
She holds her hand out flat.
How high's the water, Nora Bell?
No higher up than that.

My dad drove mixer seventeen
from Quarry Street to shutter three.
He'd bring his lunch tin home at six
with one wet boot on either side.
A dime from Nora's stuck for good
in concrete on his left boot heel.

How high's the water, Nora Bell?
She holds her hand out flat.
How high's the water, Nora Bell?
No higher up than that.

The day they shut the bottom gates
we took the till to higher ground.
One drawer slid out on Quarry Hill;
we knelt to gather up the change.
Below the road a Coca-Cola
button floated off its nail.

How high's the water, Nora Bell?
She holds her hand out flat.
How high's the water, Nora Bell?
No higher up than that.

Now Nora rents out boats by day
and keeps the till beneath the oars.
She sends me out when school lets out
to sound the channel with a pole.
At sixteen feet the tip slips in
the iron socket of her sign.

How high's the water, Nora Bell?
She holds her hand out flat.
How high's the water, Nora Bell?
No higher up than that.
