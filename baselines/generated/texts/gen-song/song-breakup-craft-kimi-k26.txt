Parked the Nissan by the loading dock
where we used to watch the FedEx trucks
back in at four a.m. The smell
of diesel and your father's cigarettes.
You wore my hoodie with the torn cuff.
I never fixed it. That was the year
we measured everything in Waffle House
checks and half-empty tanks of gas.

Still got your locker key from the Y
on Claremont, the one with the rusted hinge.
Number 847. I should mail it.

The chorus earns its repetition.

It's just the freeway now, the same five signs,
the same Chevron where you bought those tapes.
I play them sometimes. Sides B and C
warped past the words I knew by heart.

Your mother called in March. She'd found
a picture from the aquarium trip—
you touching glass, the octopus
reaching back, its one smart eye.

The chorus earns it differently now.

The new job's in a building with no view.
Lunch at twelve. I set a phone alarm,
the same chime you hated, birds in spring.
I never changed it. That is the sound
of someone walking to a meeting,
nothing more, nothing less than walking.

The chorus comes back empty, full, your call.

Last Tuesday, rain, I drove past our exit
just to see the closed Cineplex,
the SOLD sign at the Kroger lot.
The Nissan died at the light. I sat.
A bus sprayed water on my door.
I counted Mississippis. Seventeen.
Someone honked. The world went on
knowing nothing, which was fair.

The chorus earns it final, meaning all the ways
a thing can last past wanting,
past the person it belonged to,
past the belonging itself,
just this shape the mouth makes twice,
just the making, just the twice.
