They Call It Progress Now

Oh, the river ran wild through the valley below,
Where my grandfather fished in the long ago,
Where the willows hung low and the water ran free,
That old river meant everything to me.

But they dammed the water, they dammed the flow,
They buried the valley down deep below,
They said the world was changing, and I suppose it's true,
But I still hear that river calling, cool and blue.

There were trout in the pools and herons on the sand,
Ferry boats once crossed, now there's dry dead land,
The town by the water with its church and its mill,
They moved it up the mountainside, up on the hill.

Now they say the lights will burn from the power they make,
And the crops will grow greener for the farmers' sake,
But no turbine's turning, no bright city glow,
Can bring back the water that I used to know.

So I climb to the ridge when the evening falls,
Where the old river once ran, now a concrete wall,
And I dream of the current, the rush and the roar,
And a valley that don't sing like it did before.

Yes, they dammed the water, they dammed the stream,
And I'm left with the ghost of a long-lost dream,
They can stop the river, but the memory follows through,
And I still hear that water calling, cool and blue.
