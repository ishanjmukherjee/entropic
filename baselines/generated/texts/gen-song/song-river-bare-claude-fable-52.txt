My grandfather's house stood down by the bend,
Where the cottonwoods leaned on the shore,
And the river ran silver from mountain to sea,
As it had ten thousand years before.

Then the men came with papers, the men came with plans,
With their transits and stakes painted red,
Said, "The cities need power, the farms need the rain,
And the river must work now," they said.

Oh, run, river, run, while the running is free,
For they're pouring the wall in the glen,
And the water will rise over all that we knew,
And you'll not see the ocean again.

They bought up the orchards for pennies an acre,
They moved all the graves on the hill,
And the church bell they carried away on a truck,
But I swear I can hear it there still.

The salmon came up in the fall of the year,
As their mothers had taught them to do,
And they beat on the concrete with silver and blood,
But the wall wouldn't let them come through.

Oh, run, river, run, while the running is free,
For they're pouring the wall in the glen,
And the water will rise over all that we knew,
And you'll not see the ocean again.

Now the lake lies as flat as a coin in the sun,
And the speedboats cut circles above
The schoolhouse, the mill, and the old swimming hole,
And the porch where my mother found love.

But the mountains are patient, and concrete will crack,
And the snow melts the same every spring,
And a river remembers the way to the sea,
Long after the dam builders' names mean a thing.

So sleep, river, sleep, in your bed made of stone,
Though they've bridled and broken you then,
For the rain keeps on falling, the wall's growing old,
And you'll run to the ocean again.
Yes, you'll run to the ocean again.
