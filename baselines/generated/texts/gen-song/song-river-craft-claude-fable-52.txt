A man from the Corps in a gray Plymouth coupe
drove up Mill Street in March, 'fifty-three,
set a camera on legs in the road by our gate
and a slate chalked with Tract Forty-Three.
Mama spit on her thumb and went after my hair,
Daddy came from the pumps with a rag,
and the man lined us up on the steps by our height
and put his head in a black cloth bag.

Hold still, hold still,
keep your eyes here on my hand.
Don't nobody blink or wander
off the spot right where you stand.
Hold still, hold still,
on the count of three we're through,
and you all can go on back
to what you were fixing to do.

By October they'd sawed every sycamore off
at the height of a milking stool,
and the Baptists had carried their dead up the hill
in the bus from the Marlow school.
Mama backed me up straight to the kitchen-door jamb
where she'd penciled me, year over year,
laid a Ball jar lid flat on the top of my head
and took the pencil from back of her ear.

Daddy pried that jamb loose with a tire iron
and it rode to Aunt Verna's on top.
Come May we stood up on the bluff with the town
and the Lions Club handed out pop.

and the Sennett came up to the wall,
and it turned around twice like a dog on a rug,
and Daddy said, not loud at all:

I've a girl of my own now, she's ten come the fall,
and a jon boat I rent by the day
from a dock where they sell you a map of the lake
with the old roads drawn in, dotted gray.
I shut off the Evinrude out past the point
where the Humminbird read sixty-one,
and she stood in the bow while I wound on the film
with her hand up to block out the sun.
