I remember the river when she ran wild and free,
Cutting through the canyon, bringing life to the sea,
The salmon came riding on the silver crest,
And the cottonwoods whispered on her western breast.

She carried the lovers on her moonlit tide,
She carried the farmers where the fields were wide,
She carried the children on the summer days,
And she carried the old ones to their final rest.

Now the concrete is rising where she used to roll,
They're sealing up her canyons, they're taming her soul,
She'll sleep in the stillness of the reservoir,
And forget how she thundered when the spring rains roared.

Goodbye to the rapids, goodbye to the foam,
Goodbye to the winding that carried us home,
The water's still rising and the valley is gone,
And the river lies silent while the concrete lives on.

They say we need power, they say we need grain,
To light up the cities through the windowpane,
But I stand on the shoreline where the water grows deep,
And I weep for the river that's fallen asleep.
