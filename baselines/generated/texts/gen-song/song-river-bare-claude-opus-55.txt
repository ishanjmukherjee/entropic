My granddad fished the Cedar Bend
When the willows touched the shore,
He'd sing to me of the river's song
Before the water rose no more.
We'd watch the herons lift at dawn
Where the shallows caught the light,
And the river ran down to the sea
Through the long and starry night.

Oh, the river ran so wild and free,
Through the valley, through the pines,
Now she sleeps behind a wall of stone
And the old songs lose their lines.
They've bound her up with concrete chains,
Drowned the fields we knew before,
And the river that once sang to me
Don't sing to me no more.

The men came down in '62
With their papers and their plans,
Said, "Progress waits on the other side,
Sign your name and sell your land."
They gave us coin for the bottom fields
And the churchyard on the hill,
Now my grandmother's resting stone
Lies beneath the water still.

The town of Ashford's gone below,
Just a steeple in the drought,
When the summer's long and the lake runs low
You can see the bell tower out.
The salmon beat against the wall
Where they used to journey home,
And the eagles circle overhead
Over waters still as stone.

They say the lights burn bright in town,
That the cities hum with power,
But who will light the darkened homes
Of the ones who lost their hour?
And who will tell the river's tale
When the old folks all are gone?
I'll carry it in this old guitar
And I'll pass the singing on.

Someday the concrete's bound to crack,
And the stone will turn to sand,
And the river will find her ancient path
Back across the drownéd land.
The willows will return again,
And the herons on the shore,
And my children's children's children
Will hear her sing once more.

Oh, the river ran so wild and free,
Through the valley, through the pines,
Though she sleeps behind a wall of stone,
She's just waiting for her time.
They've bound her up with concrete chains,
But a river's made to roam,
And someday she'll break those chains of stone
And she'll carry us all home.
