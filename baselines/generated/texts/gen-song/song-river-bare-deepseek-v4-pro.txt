I was born in the high cold spring, a trickle and a sigh
Tumbled down the granite face, beneath a watching sky
Shone upon by seven stars, I carved my crooked way
A silver thread across the green, at the closing of the day.

I knew the otter and the trout, the kingfisher's quick dart
I wore the ancient standing stones a canyon and a heart
I sang the only song I knew, a wild and running sound
To the willow and the cottonwood and the holy, thirsting ground.

But now there's a hush where the rapids rushed, a stillness in the air
A wall of stone and steel and bone has stopped me in my prayer
My voice is a whisper in the pipes, my body deep and cold
A captive in a quiet lake, a story to be told.

They broke the bedrock for my bones, they measured every breath
They spoke of light and turning wheels, and a life that cheated death
I heard the hammer and the drill, a beating in the stone
A symphony of industry that chilled me to the bone.

Now the drowned tree lifts a branch, a ghost beneath the glass
Where the deer once bent her gentle head, the silent boats now pass
The old ford sleeps in emerald dark, the leaping fish are gone
And the only tide that moves me now is the pull before the dawn.
