Quarter past ten, got a text: “Come down.”
June double-parked with the windows wound down.
One heel on, one heel in my hand,
Hopped three steps to the beat of a van
Selling soft-serve late with its jingle off-key.
June said, “Look who decided to grace us.” Me.
Passed me the aux, let the whole block hear
The song we swore we hated all senior year.

Red light, hands up, bass in the seat,
Green light, whole skyline slides to the beat.
Got no guest list, got no grand design,
Just a pocketful of quarters and a little more time.

Left the car at the lot where her uncle worked nights,
Took the stairs toward a string of blue lights.
Club so small when the DJ spun vinyl,
His elbow kept bumping a framed liquor license.
June caught a groove with a cook off shift,
Apron string hanging from his back left hip.
I sat one out till the snare went crack—
Then my coat hit the chair, couldn’t call me back.

Red light, hands up, bass in the seat,
Green light, whole skyline slides to the beat.
Got no guest list, got no grand design,
Just a pocketful of quarters and a little more time.

Four in the morning, two stools at Sal’s,
June drew a crown on my head on the check.
Fed my last quarter to the jukebox slot;
Our song came on, we denied it was a bop.
Outside, first bus gave a sigh at the curb,
Driver mouthed along, knew every last word.
Sun coming up on the walk to the ride,
One heel in each hand this time.
