THE WESTERN WINDOW

[The palace kitchen, just before dawn. QUEEN MARA stands at a table, cutting an apple into very thin slices. Across from her, NELL, the kitchen girl, tries to thread a needle in the poor light.]

NELL: If you cut them any thinner, Majesty, there’ll only be one side.

MARA: My husband likes them that way.

NELL: Shall I put his plate in the warmer?

MARA: No. Last time they curled up like toenails.

[NELL laughs, then looks to see whether she ought to have. Three knocks at the yard door.]

MARA: That’ll be him. He never could come home through a front door.

[NELL opens it. A MESSENGER stands outside, one boot on, one foot wrapped in a flour sack.]

MESSENGER: I was told the queen might be here.

MARA: You were told correctly.

[He takes off his cap. Inside it is a folded paper.]

MESSENGER: The army at Bellwater has surrendered. The king agreed to terms yesterday, shortly after noon.

MARA: Agreed to what terms?

MESSENGER: The wounded could go home. The others were to be held until a settlement.

MARA: And my husband is being held where?

[The MESSENGER puts the paper on the table.]

MESSENGER: He died yesterday evening, Majesty.

[The pot on the stove boils over. NELL moves it.]

MARA: You might have said that first.

MESSENGER: Yes.

MARA: Is that the order they told you to say it in?

MESSENGER: No. I thought if I could get the first part out, I could get the rest out after.

MARA: Nell, the gentleman needs a shoe.

NELL: I can see that.

[Neither moves.]

MARA: Were you there when he died?

MESSENGER: I was outside. They’d put him in the tollkeeper’s house. The doctor came out and asked if anybody had a pencil. I gave him mine. Then he went back in. That’s as near as I was.

MARA: Thank you for not going inside.

MESSENGER: Majesty?

MARA: By the time this story reaches the council chamber, six men will have been holding his hand.

[She unfolds the paper.]

MARA: This is a bill for six hundredweight of onions.

MESSENGER: Other side.

[She turns it over. She knows the writing. She sits.]

MARA: “Let the hospital carts cross without inspection.” He’s signed it twice.

MESSENGER: His hand slipped on the first one. There are forty carts at the western gate. The watch won’t let them through on a dead king’s order.

MARA: How did the watch learn he was dead before I did?

MESSENGER: They asked me why I hadn’t brought him home. I’ve got a boy on the watch. I couldn’t look him in the eye and say his king was coming.

MARA: But you could let me put an apple on a plate.

MESSENGER: I’m sorry.

[NELL takes the plate away. MARA catches her wrist.]

MARA: Leave it. Please.

[NELL sets it down.]

MARA: Who’s in the carts?

MESSENGER: Ours. Theirs. Some folk who lived by the river. One woman with a perfectly good leg who won’t leave her sister.

MARA: Nell, fetch the blue seal from my desk.

[NELL starts out.]

MARA: No. Wait. How long does it take you to get upstairs and back?

NELL: Depends who wants the seal.

MARA: I do.

NELL: Five minutes. If the prince hears me on the landing, half an hour. He’ll want to know if his father’s brought the little dog.

[MARA looks toward the ceiling.]

MARA: I’d forgotten about the dog.

[Outside, a single cartwheel squeals. The MESSENGER turns toward the sound.]

MESSENGER: They’ll have started moving up. You can see the gate from that window.

[MARA opens the western window. Cold air lifts the paper off the table. NELL pins it down with the apple plate.]

MARA: Gatekeeper! It’s Mara. Can you hear me?

[A faint answering call.]

MARA: Yes, in the kitchen. Open both gates. All the way, mind. They’ve people lying down in there.

[A beat. Then the long scrape of the gates.]

MARA: Nell, find this man two shoes. He shouldn’t have to keep the bad one just because it stayed on.

MESSENGER: What shall I tell them about the king?

MARA: Tell them what you told me. Only start with him.

[She takes a slice of apple and goes upstairs.]
