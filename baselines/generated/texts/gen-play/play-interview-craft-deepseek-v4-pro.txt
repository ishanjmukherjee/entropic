MS. ALBRIGHT
Do sit down.

DIANE
Thank you.

(Diane sits. The only chair faces a wide steel desk. On the desk: a telephone, a blank notepad, and a glass paperweight, perfectly round. Ms. Albright stands. She holds a single sheet of paper.)

MS. ALBRIGHT
You found us all right.

DIANE
Number 42.

MS. ALBRIGHT
The lift’s out, I’m afraid.

DIANE
I don’t mind stairs.

MS. ALBRIGHT
No.

(She looks at the paper.)

You’ve been at… Harper & Squire.

DIANE
Three years this March.

MS. ALBRIGHT
And before that, the Whitford.

DIANE
Intern. Then associate.

MS. ALBRIGHT
Not a long time. At the Whitford.

DIANE
Long enough.

MS. ALBRIGHT
Long enough for what?

(Diane doesn’t answer. Ms. Albright places the paper on the desk but does not sit.)

They give you a good send-off?

DIANE
Sorry?

MS. ALBRIGHT
A card. Drinks. The usual.

DIANE
It was fine.

MS. ALBRIGHT
Only “fine”?

DIANE
Very generous.

MS. ALBRIGHT
But you left.

DIANE
I did.

MS. ALBRIGHT
Why?

DIANE
(Pause.)
I’m interested in growth.

MS. ALBRIGHT
We’re a small firm.

DIANE
Growth doesn’t have to mean size.

MS. ALBRIGHT
What does it mean?

DIANE
Depth. Range. This role—

MS. ALBRIGHT
The role. Yes. Client-facing. High net worth. Discretion is paramount.

DIANE
Of course.

MS. ALBRIGHT
Discretion about clients, certainly. But also about… previous roles.

DIANE
I understand.

MS. ALBRIGHT
Do you? Whitford is a village. We are a village. People talk. I hear someone did something rather… remarkable. At Harper & Squire. A resignation that wasn’t quite a resignation.

DIANE
I gave notice.

MS. ALBRIGHT
They were surprised.

DIANE
Notice often surprises people.

MS. ALBRIGHT
(Moves to the window.)
The view from here is terrible. Brick wall. No one sees in. I like that.

(Diane uncrosses her legs, then recrosses them.)

DIANE
What did people say?

MS. ALBRIGHT
That you were very calm. Collected your things in a cardboard box. A plant.

DIANE
A peace lily.

MS. ALBRIGHT
And then you walked down all four flights. And you stood in the lobby.

DIANE
For a moment.

MS. ALBRIGHT
For seven minutes. By the security desk. Then you just… left.

DIANE
It was raining.

MS. ALBRIGHT
So I heard. No coat.

DIANE
I gave my umbrella to the receptionist.

MS. ALBRIGHT
(Nods slowly.)
Someone said you were waiting for someone.

DIANE
People say a lot of things.

MS. ALBRIGHT
Yes. They do. They say Spencer Hale was let go the same afternoon.

(Diane’s hand moves to the paperweight. She doesn’t lift it. Just touches the cool glass.)

DIANE
Spencer.

MS. ALBRIGHT
Your manager.

DIANE
For a time.

MS. ALBRIGHT
And the reason you left?

DIANE
No. The reason I left is what I said. Growth.

MS. ALBRIGHT
Spencer Hale is no longer working in this city. In this sector. Did you know that?

DIANE
I don’t keep track.

(She slowly spins the paperweight on its base.)

MS. ALBRIGHT
You’d be working closely with Gordon Meade. He’s our senior partner. A very… particular man.

DIANE
I’m adaptable.

MS. ALBRIGHT
He’s old-fashioned.

DIANE
How old-fashioned?

MS. ALBRIGHT
He has a way of putting his hand on the back of a chair where you’re sitting. Or your shoulder. Nothing that crosses a line. Just… places it.

(Diane’s fingers stop the paperweight’s spin.)

DIANE
Does he?

MS. ALBRIGHT
Does that bother you?

DIANE
Should it?

MS. ALBRIGHT
I’m asking.

DIANE
I’m very good with difficult people.

MS. ALBRIGHT
Because you manage them?

DIANE
I like to think I… help them manage themselves.

(A pause. Ms. Albright comes back to the desk. She looks down at the paperweight, then at Diane’s hand, still resting on it.)

MS. ALBRIGHT
You took a client list with you. When you left Harper’s.

DIANE
I did not.

MS. ALBRIGHT
No. But someone did. A very specific list. Mid-tier accounts. Funnelled to a competitor. It ruined Spencer Hale.

DIANE
Did it.

MS. ALBRIGHT
Not the money. The embarrassment. His phone stopped ringing. His wife left, they say. It was thorough.

DIANE
Sounds like a systems breach.

MS. ALBRIGHT
It was surgical.

(Diane lifts the glass weight. Holds it in her palm, testing the heft.)

DIANE
This is beautiful. Where did you get it?

MS. ALBRIGHT
Murano. A gift from Gordon.

DIANE
(Examining it to the light.)
It has a flaw. Inside. Do you see it? A small crack, right in the centre.

MS. ALBRIGHT
I know.

DIANE
And you keep it.

MS. ALBRIGHT
I like to see where the pressure is.

(Diane sets it down gently, precisely. She stands.)

DIANE
Tell Gordon Meade I look forward to meeting him.

MS. ALBRIGHT
I haven’t offered you the position.

DIANE
You haven’t. But you will.

MS. ALBRIGHT
Why?

(Diane picks up her handbag. She walks to the door. There she stops, holding the frame. She turns, the door half-open.)

DIANE
Because Spencer Hale had a particular way of touching a chair. And now he doesn’t.

(She lets the door click shut behind her. Ms. Albright stands motionless. She picks up the paperweight. Holds it flat on her palm, staring into it.)
