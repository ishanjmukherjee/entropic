A simple chapel anteroom. Two folding chairs. MARCUS, 45, sits in one, staring at his phone. DEREK, 48, enters, loosening his tie. He stops when he sees his brother.

DEREK: Didn't think you'd make it.

MARCUS: (not looking up) Flight got in this morning.

DEREK: That's not what I meant.

MARCUS finally pockets the phone. Silence.

DEREK: You look tired.

MARCUS: You look old.

DEREK: (almost smiling) Same Marcus. Some things don't change.

MARCUS: Some things do. You wear suits now.

DEREK: Funeral, Marcus.

MARCUS: You wore them to the hospital too. Every photo Mom sent—

DEREK: Don't.

MARCUS: —you in a suit. Standing there like—

DEREK: I said don't.

MARCUS rises, moves to the window.

MARCUS: Forty-three people out there. I counted.

DEREK: You always did count.

MARCUS: Old ladies from his factory job. Neighbors. Where's everyone from his life?

DEREK: We're his life.

MARCUS: (bitter laugh) "We."

DEREK sits heavily in the second chair.

DEREK: You could have come sooner.

MARCUS: You said that on the phone. October. "Come sooner."

DEREK: You didn't come at all.

MARCUS: I sent checks.

DEREK: Checks.

MARCUS: Large checks.

DEREK: (standing) HE WANTED YOU, NOT YOUR MONEY. He asked for you. "Where's Marcus?" Every goddamn day, Marcus. "Where's my boy?"

MARCUS: (quietly) I was busy.

DEREK: Busy.

MARCUS: Company going public. Eighty-hour weeks.

DEREK: Eighty-hour weeks. (almost spit) I changed his diapers, Marcus. I fed him spoonfuls of applesauce while he forgot my name. Where were your eighty hours then?

MARCUS: YOU CHOSE TO STAY.

DEREK: Someone had to!

MARCUS: AND I CHOSE TO GO. We both made choices, Derek. You don't get to be the martyr just because mine paid better.

A long silence. DEREK moves toward the door, stops.

DEREK: He kept your first chess trophy on the mantle. The little plastic one. Not the state championship—that went in a box. The cheap one. You were seven. He said that was the day he knew you'd be brilliant.

MARCUS: Derek—

DEREK: Don't. Don't do that voice. That "let's be civilized" voice you use when clients cry. I heard it for two years on the phone. "Let's be practical, Derek." "Have you considered a nursing home, Derek." Practical. (beat) He hid your letters, you know. Under his pillow. The three you sent. Wore them soft as cloth.

MARCUS turns fully to the window. His shoulders move slightly.

MARCUS: (measured) I have a flight.

DEREK: Of course.

MARCUS: Tonight. Singapore. Deal was supposed to close—

DEREK: Go, then.

MARCUS: The lawyers will handle the estate. I'll take nothing. Whatever he left, it's yours.

DEREK: He left chess sets. Fourteen of them. Wooden ones he carved himself after he retired. You remember he used to carve?

MARCUS: No.

DEREK: No. You wouldn't. (moving toward him) He left you the whale.

MARCUS: (still not turning) What?

DEREK: The scrimshaw. The one Grandfather gave him. (beat) He never let anyone touch it. "This goes to Marcus," he said. Two weeks ago, lucid hour. "The whale goes to Marcus."

MARCUS finally turns. His face is wet.

MARCUS: I don't deserve—

DEREK: No. You don't.

MARCUS: Then why?

DEREK: Because he decided you did. (moving to the door) That's all I know.

He exits. MARCUS stands alone. He touches one of the folding chairs, sits where DEREK sat. He holds his face in his hands. After a moment, he reaches into his pocket, pulls out his phone, looks at it. He sets it on the chair beside him, screen-down. He does not move. Blackout.
