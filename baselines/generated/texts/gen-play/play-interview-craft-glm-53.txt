(A office. Two chairs, one behind a desk. On the desk, a glass paperweight. VICTOR sits. MARSH enters with a folder.)

VICTOR
Sit.

MARSH
Thank you for—

VICTOR
Sit, sit.

(MARSH sits. He puts the folder on his knee.)

VICTOR
You came far?

MARSH
Not really. Forty minutes.

VICTOR
Train?

MARSH
Car.

VICTOR
Parking's a nightmare.

MARSH
I found a spot.

VICTOR
Lucky.

(He picks up the paperweight. Turns it. Sets it down.)

MARSH
Should I start with—

VICTOR
You put parking on your form.

MARSH
Sorry?

VICTOR
Under skills. You listed parking.

MARSH
I listed—

VICTOR
"Negotiation. Parking."

MARSH
It was a typing error.

VICTOR
For what?

MARSH
"Parchment." It was a— I do calligraphy. It autocorrected.

VICTOR
Calligraphy.

MARSH
It's a hobby.

VICTOR
We had a calligrapher here once. Wrote the noticeboard. Birthdays. People loved it.

MARSH
Right.

VICTOR
She's gone.

(He slides the paperweight to the edge of the desk. MARSH watches it.)

MARSH
Shall I tell you about my background?

VICTOR
In a minute. Do you know what we do here?

MARSH
Logistics.

VICTOR
Broadly.

MARSH
Movement of goods.

VICTOR
Broadly, yes. You've read the website.

MARSH
I read the website.

VICTOR
The website's a lie. Not a lie. A version.

MARSH
Of what?

VICTOR
Of the thing.

MARSH
Which is?

VICTOR
You'd be on phones. Mostly.

MARSH
The ad said planning.

VICTOR
Planning supports phones.

MARSH
I left a job for this interview.

VICTOR
People do.

MARSH
A good job.

VICTOR
Then why leave it?

MARSH
I want growth.

VICTOR
Growth.

MARSH
Vertical movement.

VICTOR
You want my chair.

MARSH
I want a path to—

VICTOR
Everyone wants the path. Nobody wants the road.

MARSH
I'm not sure I follow.

VICTOR
The road is the phones.

(He stands, walks to the window, comes back. Doesn't sit. Picks up the folder from MARSH's knee, opens it.)

VICTOR
This is thin.

MARSH
It's focused.

VICTOR
It's thin. Three jobs. Nine years.

MARSH
Loyalty.

VICTOR
Or fear.

MARSH
Or loyalty.

VICTOR
Nine years at Dane's. What did they make?

MARSH
Containers.

VICTOR
You made containers.

MARSH
I coordinated the—

VICTOR
Containers for what?

MARSH
Shipping.

VICTOR
Of?

MARSH
That's client information.

VICTOR
I'm asking about boxes, not state secrets.

MARSH
I signed a confidentiality—

VICTOR
Boxes.

MARSH
Various. Goods.

VICTOR
What fell out of one once. That's the question. Nine years, something fell out. Or went missing. Or a man named—

MARSH
Don't.

VICTOR
Kowalski.

MARSH
That is not—

VICTOR
It's on the internet. One page. You can't keep a Kowalski off the internet.

MARSH
If you've read the page, you've read the finding. No fault. No charges.

VICTOR
I read what you said to the tribunal.

MARSH
I said what happened.

VICTOR
You said a sentence that took four minutes.

MARSH
I was asked to be precise.

VICTOR
Nobody's that precise.

MARSH
Nobody's asked to be.

(He takes the paperweight. Just takes it. VICTOR watches. MARSH holds it up to the light.)

MARSH
There's a flaw. Inside. Near the top.

VICTOR
There isn't.

MARSH
There is. You have to want to see it.

(He sets it back. It rolls. Neither man moves to catch it. It stops, half off the desk.)

VICTOR
Take the role.

MARSH
The phones.

VICTOR
Six months. Then we talk about the road.

MARSH
The path.

VICTOR
Same thing.

MARSH
It's not, though. That's rather the point of this.

VICTOR
You want the job or not?

MARSH
What happened to her? The calligrapher.

VICTOR
She grew.

MARSH
Vertically?

VICTOR
She left.

(He pushes the paperweight back onto the desk with one finger. MARSH stands, takes his folder, and puts it back on his knee. Doesn't offer it.)

MARSH
Is Dane's on your client list?

VICTOR
Why?

MARSH
Because if I sit in that chair and they call in, someone named Marsh answers, that's a conversation. And conversations like that are never precise.

VICTOR
Six months on phones first. You'd never take the calls.

MARSH
You said mostly phones.

VICTOR
I said the website was a version.

(Silence. MARSH sets his folder on the desk, on top of the paperweight.)

MARSH
I'll take the six months.

VICTOR
Good.

MARSH
On one condition.

VICTOR
There are no conditions.

MARSH
Then I'll take it without one.

(He leaves the folder. VICTOR waits until the door closes, then opens the folder. It is empty except for one sheet. He reads it. He looks at the paperweight. He puts the sheet in his pocket, picks up the paperweight, and drops it neatly into the bin.)

(Lights down.)
