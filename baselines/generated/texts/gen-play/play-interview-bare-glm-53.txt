A stage play in one scene

SETTING: A small, cramped office. A metal desk cluttered with papers. Two chairs. MR. DUMFRIES, 50s, sits behind the desk in a ill-fitting suit. A résumé sits in front of him. BARBARA, 40s, enters, clutching her handbag like a shield.

DUMFRIES: Come in! Come in. Sit down, sit down. You're Barbara... (checks paper) ...Crane?

BARBARA: That's right. Thank you for seeing me.

DUMFRIES: Absolutely. Absolutely. Now, before we begin, I have to ask — do you have any experience being the Assistant Regional Manager?

BARBARA: Well, the posting said Assistant to the Regional Manager.

DUMFRIES: (leaning forward) No. No, it didn't.

BARBARA: I brought a copy of the posting.

DUMFRIES: (waving hand) Details, details. The point is, this is a very important role. You'd be managing inventory, client relations, and, on occasion, my calendar.

BARBARA: (hesitant) Which... which one is the priority?

DUMFRIES: The calendar. Obviously. Without the calendar, there IS no inventory. Think about it.

(Barbara clearly does not think about it. A pause.)

BARBARA: I... see. Well, as it happens, I have twelve years of experience in—

DUMFRIES: Twelve! Ha! I had a fellow in here yesterday with eleven years. Eleven! Can you imagine? What was he doing with that extra year? Slackin g off, probably.

BARBARA: I... worked that year?

DUMFRIES: (suspicious) Did you. Did you though.

BARBARA: Yes. At Hendersen & Lowe. I ran their entire logistics department, actually. Oversaw a staff of fifteen, increased efficiency by twenty percent—

DUMFRIES: Efficiency. (he says it like a curse word) You know what happens to efficient workplaces, Ms. Crane? People get comfortable. Then they get sloppy. Then they unionize. I prefer a workplace with a certain... tension.

BARBARA: Tension.

DUMFRIES: Keeps people sharp! Like a knife! A tense knife!

BARBARA: Sir, that's not — knives aren't tense, they're just — sharp.

DUMFRIES: (pointing at her) See, this is the kind of attitude I'm talking about. Correcting management. In the first ten minutes. Noted. (writes on the résumé)

BARBARA: Noted where? You're writing on my résumé.

DUMFRIES: I take very thorough notes. Now. Salary expectations. The posting says forty thousand, I believe?

BARBARA: It says sixty-five.

DUMFRIES: Every price is negotiable, Ms. Crane. I once bought a boat for half the asking price. Sank, but that's not the point. The point is negotiating. Speaking of which — how do you feel about dogs?

BARBARA: ...Dogs?

DUMFRIES: In the office. My dog, specifically. Jeremy. He's a St. Bernard. He has anxiety. He needs to be near me at all times, and he does sometimes — not always, but sometimes — he expresses his anxiety. On the carpet.

BARBARA: He... expresses...

DUMFRIES: It's mostly the waiting room. Now, one final question, and be honest, because this is the most important one. (long pause; he folds his hands) If a train leaves London at nine a.m. travelling at sixty miles per hour, and another leaves Leeds at ten, and the job depends on where they meet — where do they meet?

BARBARA: (beat) That depends on the distance between London and Leeds, the—

DUMFRIES: WRONG. They meet in Birmingham. Everyone knows that. It's basic geography.

BARBARA: It's basic arithmetic, but not basic geography, and without the distance and—

DUMFRIES: (standing, extending hand) Barbara. I like you. You've got moxie. When can you start?

BARBARA: (standing slowly) I... don't think I'll be starting.

DUMFRIES: Monday it is! Nine sharp. Bring a mop. For Jeremy.

(Barbara turns and walks out. Dumfries sits back down, satisfied, picks up her résumé, and drops it into the bin.)

DUMFRIES: Best interview all week.

(Lights down.)

END
