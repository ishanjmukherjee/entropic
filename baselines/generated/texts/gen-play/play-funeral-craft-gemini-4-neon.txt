(A small side room. A folding table, two chairs. PAUL stands by the window in a dark coat. MARK enters, carrying two paper cups. He sets them down, then drops a heavy bunch of keys on a brass fob onto the table.)

MARK
Milk, no sugar.

PAUL
Still black.

MARK
Thought you’d gone off it.

PAUL
No.

MARK
Carol’s asking where you’re parked.

PAUL
Down by the bridge.

MARK
You’ll get a ticket there.

PAUL
It’s a Sunday.

MARK
Not on the yellow, it isn’t. They’ve got a van now. Cameras on the roof.

PAUL
(Turns from the window.)
Good turnout.

MARK
Half of them came for the sandwiches.

PAUL
The vicar knew him, then.

MARK
Met him Tuesday.

PAUL
Right.

MARK
I gave him the dates. Told him about the navy. He got the ship wrong, did you notice?

PAUL
I wasn’t listening.

MARK
Well, you wouldn’t.

(PAUL walks to the table. He looks at the keys, doesn't touch them.)

PAUL
Quick, was it?

MARK
The service?

PAUL
The end.

MARK
(Sits.)
Roof’s gone on the back extension.

PAUL
Right.

MARK
Not the whole thing. Just the felt. Water’s coming in over the boiler.

PAUL
You should get someone in.

MARK
I had a bloke out. Wants four grand.

PAUL
That’s steep.

MARK
That’s what they charge now. You wouldn’t know, renting.

PAUL
I bought a place. Two years ago.

MARK
(Pause.)
Did you.

PAUL
Near the station.

MARK
Good for you.

PAUL
He didn’t say?

MARK
Who?

PAUL
I sent a card. With the address on it.

MARK
He didn’t open the post. Not since Easter. There’s a stack of it in the hall, up to your knee.

(MARK nudges the keys an inch toward PAUL.)

PAUL
Was he awake? At the end.

MARK
On and off.

PAUL
Did he know who—

MARK
The solicitor’s in on Thursday.

PAUL
I’m back on the train tonight.

MARK
Thursday morning, ten o’clock. It’s just signatures.

PAUL
Post them to me.

MARK
She wants us both there.

PAUL
Tell her I’m in Leeds.

MARK
You’re not in Leeds, you’re standing in front of me.

PAUL
(Picks up a paper cup, sets it down.)
She’s a nurse, was she? The woman in the green hat.

MARK
From the home.

PAUL
She looked upset.

MARK
She’s like that. Cries at the telly.

PAUL
He liked her, then.

MARK
He threw a slipper at her on Monday.

PAUL
(Smiles, briefly.)
Sounds like him.

MARK
Didn’t miss, either.

(A beat. PAUL sits opposite MARK.)

PAUL
Did he have his glasses on?

MARK
What?

PAUL
In the bed. He hated not having them.

MARK
I don’t know, Paul. I was sorting the car.

PAUL
You weren’t there?

MARK
I was in the car park. Getting a ticket. You have to put the reg in now, the machine wouldn’t take my card.

PAUL
Right.

MARK
I was gone ten minutes.

PAUL
I’m not—

MARK
I was there every bloody day for six months.

PAUL
I know you were.

MARK
Don’t sit there and ask me about his glasses.

(Silence. MARK rubs his face. He looks at the keys.)

MARK
The bank’s been ringing.

PAUL
About what?

MARK
The yard. He never cleared the lease.

PAUL
I thought he sold the yard.

MARK
He sold the vans. Kept the lease. God knows why.

PAUL
How much?

MARK
Enough.

(PAUL looks at the keys. MARK watches him.)

PAUL
He had a box. In the wardrobe.

MARK
It’s all still there. I haven’t touched a thing.

PAUL
Green tin.

MARK
You want to go up there? After this?

PAUL
I’ve got the six-twenty.

MARK
There’s an eight-fifteen.

PAUL
I’ve got work tomorrow, Mark.

MARK
I’ve got work tomorrow.

PAUL
(Stands.)
I should go say hello to Carol.

MARK
She’s not going anywhere.

PAUL
Did he ask?

MARK
What?

PAUL
Before Monday. Before the slipper.

(MARK looks at him for a long moment.)

MARK
He asked if the dog was fed.

PAUL
We haven’t had a dog since—

MARK
I know when we had a dog.

(PAUL buttons his coat.)

MARK
Take them.

PAUL
I can’t do Thursday.

MARK
I’m not asking about Thursday. Just go up there. Get your green tin. Have a look at the boiler.

PAUL
I don’t know anything about boilers.

MARK
Neither do I.

(MARK stands. He walks to the door, stops with his hand on the handle.)

MARK
They’re bringing the tea out.

PAUL
I’ll be there in a minute.

MARK
He didn’t say your name, Paul. If that’s what you’re waiting for.

(MARK opens the door, leaves it ajar, and goes out. PAUL stands by the table. He looks at the keys. He reaches out, rests his fingertips on the brass fob, and leaves them there.)
