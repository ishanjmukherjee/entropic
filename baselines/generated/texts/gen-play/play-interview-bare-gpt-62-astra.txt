THE OTHER SIDE OF THE DESK

[A municipal building. An interview room overlooking a bus depot. Two chairs, a desk, a jug of water. MS. VALE sits behind the desk. ELI stands beside the other chair, holding a paper cup.]

VALE: Please, sit.

ELI: I did. The chair went down.

VALE: It adjusts.

ELI: Not back up, apparently.

[He sits. His chin is level with the desk. VALE considers this, then lowers her own chair to match.]

VALE: There. Now neither of us can reach the water.

ELI: I brought some.

VALE: Good. Resourceful.

[She makes a note.]

ELI: Was that part of it?

VALE: No. The chair is genuinely broken.

[She lifts a folder off the desk and sets it on her knees.]

VALE: Why do you want to work in Passenger Complaints?

ELI: My mother drove the number six for twenty-eight years. When she retired, people came to our house with cakes. Six cakes. You don’t think of a bus driver as someone who might need a cake, but six separate people did.

VALE: And that made you want to work in complaints?

ELI: Well, nobody comes to your house if you work in complaints. That part appeals to me.

[She laughs. He relaxes.]

VALE: We’ll do a practical exercise. I’m a passenger. You’re on the desk. Ready?

ELI: Yes.

VALE: Yesterday the four-fifteen drove straight past my stop. I was standing there with my arm out. What are you going to do about it?

ELI: I’m sorry. Which stop was that?

VALE: Mercer Street, outside the baths.

ELI: The four-fifteen doesn’t stop there anymore.

VALE: There’s a sign.

ELI: There is. They haven’t taken it down.

VALE: Then how was I supposed to know?

ELI: You weren’t. That’s our fault.

[VALE looks at the folder.]

VALE: You’re meant to offer me a complaint form.

ELI: Would you like a complaint form?

VALE: No, I’d like to get to work.

ELI: If you walk through the baths car park, there’s a gate onto Bell Lane. The bus stops just there.

VALE: They lock that gate at three.

ELI: Do they?

VALE: Yes. I had to climb it.

[A pause.]

ELI: Was this yesterday?

VALE: We’re doing the exercise.

ELI: Only, you’ve got green paint on your cuff.

[She turns her cuff under.]

VALE: I missed the beginning of my daughter’s concert. She plays the bassoon. There’s very little bassoon in the second half.

ELI: I’m sorry.

VALE: Thank you. Now. Suppose the passenger becomes abusive.

ELI: Did you?

VALE: I beg your pardon?

ELI: Become abusive.

VALE: I described the sign in terms I wouldn’t ordinarily use.

ELI: To whom?

VALE: The man on the telephone.

ELI: My brother.

VALE: What?

ELI: Yesterday? After four? That’s our Kevin. Did he say, “I appreciate your journey”?

VALE: Repeatedly.

ELI: He wrote that himself. They put it in the handbook.

[Outside, a bus releases its brakes.]

VALE: Is everyone in your family employed by the depot?

ELI: Not me. How abusive?

VALE: I asked whether he’d ever actually been on a bus.

ELI: Kevin can’t go on buses. He gets sick.

VALE: I didn’t know that.

ELI: Why would you? You rang about a sign.

[VALE closes the folder.]

VALE: I think we should stop.

ELI: I’d rather finish. I took the morning off.

VALE: I don’t think I can score this fairly.

ELI: Then let me have another passenger.

VALE: I haven’t got another passenger.

ELI: Be one of the cake people. Something’s happened to your cake.

[Despite herself, she thinks.]

VALE: The driver braked suddenly. My sponge slid off my lap.

ELI: Was anybody hurt?

VALE: No.

ELI: Good. Was it for an occasion?

VALE: My daughter’s birthday.

ELI: Is she little enough that sticking the candles in the new top will work?

VALE: She’s thirty-one.

ELI: Then she’s old enough to do it herself.

[VALE opens the folder. She makes a note.]

ELI: What did you put?

VALE: “Does not offer form.”

[She tears the complaint form from the back of the folder and turns it over.]

VALE: What’s Kevin’s surname?

ELI: Same as mine.

VALE: Right. Of course.

[She begins to write.]

ELI: He likes “Dear Kevin.” “Dear Sir” generally means he’s about to have a bad afternoon.

[She crosses something out. ELI waits. Without looking up, she holds out her hand. After a moment he understands and gives her his water.]

[Blackout.]
