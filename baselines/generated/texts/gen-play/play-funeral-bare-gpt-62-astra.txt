[The chapel after the service. LEON, fifty, is stacking the printed orders of service on a chair. His younger brother KIT stands at the lectern with a cassette recorder. From it comes the sound of a man clearing his throat.]

LEON: Turn that off.

KIT: I'm trying.

LEON: The square one.

[The sound stops.]

KIT: I know what stop looks like.

LEON: You were pushing eject.

KIT: Has anybody ever told you how wonderful it is to have you nearby?

[LEON evens up the stack against his knee.]

KIT: They want us at the restaurant.

LEON: Then go. Tell them I'm getting the flowers.

KIT: Are you?

LEON: Eventually somebody will have to.

[KIT takes out the cassette. A length of brown tape hangs from it.]

KIT: Oh, hell.

LEON: Give it here.

[KIT gives it to him. LEON takes a pencil from his breast pocket and winds the tape back in.]

KIT: You still carry a pencil.

LEON: Evidently.

KIT: I thought he sounded good. Once the coughing stopped.

LEON: That was 1998. That's how he sounded in 1998.

KIT: I wasn't going to play the last voicemail he left me. It's four minutes of him trying to get a parking app to work.

LEON: He rang you about the parking app?

KIT: Every Thursday. From the renal clinic. He'd read me the numbers and I'd pay from my phone.

LEON: I gave him parking money.

KIT: Well. The old crook.

[LEON nearly smiles. He puts the cassette on the chair.]

LEON: You could've mentioned that in there.

KIT: What?

LEON: Thursdays. Something that happened this century. Listening to you, anybody would've thought he died the summer we built that boat.

KIT: You said you didn't want to speak.

LEON: I didn't.

KIT: I asked you for something to say.

LEON: And I sent you something.

KIT: You sent me his medication list.

LEON: On the back, Kit.

[KIT takes a folded sheet from inside his jacket. He opens it and turns it over.]

KIT: Oh.

[A door closes somewhere outside.]

KIT: “Dad liked the woman at the bakery because she cut his bread the long way.” Why did she cut it the long way?

LEON: He liked a long piece of toast.

KIT: “He learned the names of all six dogs on the landing but none of the owners.” That's good. Leon, I'm sorry. I saw amlodipine and thought you'd sent the wrong thing.

LEON: I wondered why you said you'd run it past the minister.

[KIT sits. He reads the rest in silence. LEON picks a white carnation out of a wreath. It comes away on a wire.]

KIT: Do you want me to read this at the restaurant?

LEON: No.

KIT: I could.

LEON: Just keep it.

[KIT folds the sheet, this time with the writing out.]

KIT: At the end of the recording, did you hear him say, “Mind your fingers, Lenny”?

LEON: He said, “Mind your fingers, Leonard.”

KIT: Nobody ever called you Leonard except when you were in trouble.

LEON: You were holding the hammer.

KIT: Christ, yes.

LEON: You brought it down on my thumb. He drove us both to Casualty. You threw up in the car and somehow you got the front seat on the way home.

KIT: I don't remember that part.

LEON: No. It was a pretty good arrangement for you.

[KIT looks at him. LEON puts the carnation in his buttonhole. The wire is too long; it sticks out below his lapel.]

KIT: I didn't know how to come back once I'd stayed away that long.

LEON: There's a direct train.

KIT: I know there's a direct train.

[LEON bends the wire up under his lapel.]

LEON: I used to work out which one you could still catch. On Christmas Eve. Even after we'd eaten.

KIT: What time was the last one?

LEON: Nine fourteen.

[KIT returns the cassette to the machine. He depresses two buttons together.]

LEON: What are you doing?

KIT: Tell me about the toast again.

LEON: You'll tape over the boat.

KIT: Other side. Look.

[LEON checks. KIT sets the recorder between them.]

LEON: Well. It wouldn't fit in the toaster, obviously. So every morning he had to put the grill on. And every morning the smoke alarm went off. And the dog next door—

KIT: Which dog?

LEON: Pamela.

[They both laugh. The machine turns.]

LEON: Leave that bit in.
