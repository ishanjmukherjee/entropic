ARCHIVIST
(quiet)
Another letter for you.

QUEEN
From the front?

ARCHIVIST
Yes.

QUEEN
Read it.

ARCHIVIST
He wrote it himself.

QUEEN
So you’ve read it.

ARCHIVIST
I bring the letters. I don’t—

QUEEN
(holding out her hand)
Then bring it.

(ARCHIVIST does not move. QUEEN lets her hand fall.)

QUEEN
You always read them first. You always summarize. Today you stand there like a stone.

ARCHIVIST
It’s his hand. The seal was his.

QUEEN
Is it victory?

ARCHIVIST
It’s his hand.

QUEEN
Is it defeat?

ARCHIVIST
A single rider made it through the pass. One horse, lamed at the gate. No others.

QUEEN
Then read it.

ARCHIVIST
The light is bad in here.

QUEEN
You’ve read it. Speak it.

(ARCHIVIST looks at the sealed scroll in her grip. She runs a thumb across the wax.)

ARCHIVIST
The pass was held. The pass was held and then lost and then held again. He writes the numbers very clearly. The numbers are—

QUEEN
I didn’t ask for numbers.

ARCHIVIST
You asked for the letter.

QUEEN
I asked you to read it.

ARCHIVIST
I’m reading.

QUEEN
You’re not reading. You’re protecting me from something. Stop protecting me.

ARCHIVIST
The seventh day. The line broke on the left flank. He rallied them. He writes, “I rallied them.”

QUEEN
Good.

ARCHIVIST
Wait.

QUEEN
That’s good. That’s what he does.

ARCHIVIST
There’s a second page.

QUEEN
Read the second page.

(ARCHIVIST does not move.)

QUEEN
You’ve already read the second page.

ARCHIVIST
I did my duty. I open all correspondence.

QUEEN
Then do your duty now.

(ARCHIVIST lifts the scroll. She does not look at it.)

ARCHIVIST
“By the time you receive this, the eighth day will have ended. The ninth will begin without me.”

(QUEEN stands. She walks to the window. Her back is to the ARCHIVIST.)

QUEEN
There’s more.

ARCHIVIST
Yes.

QUEEN
Read it.

ARCHIVIST
It’s personal.

QUEEN
Everything that comes to this room is mine. Read it.

ARCHIVIST
“Tell my wife—”

QUEEN
Read it.

ARCHIVIST
“Tell my wife the child should be named for her mother. Not mine.”

(QUEEN turns.)

QUEEN
Give it to me.

ARCHIVIST
No.

QUEEN
Excuse me?

ARCHIVIST
You don’t want to hold this.

QUEEN
You don’t tell me what I want.

ARCHIVIST
The paper is—there’s—blood. On the second page. His blood. Dried. I thought you should know before you touched it.

QUEEN
You thought.

ARCHIVIST
I thought.

QUEEN
You stand there with a letter from my husband, a letter written in his own blood—

ARCHIVIST
Not the ink. The blood is beside the ink. On the paper. He bled while writing. That’s all I meant.

(Beat.)

QUEEN
(quietly)
Bring it here.

(ARCHIVIST crosses the room. She holds out the scroll. QUEEN reaches for it. ARCHIVIST does not release it. They both hold the scroll between them.)

ARCHIVIST
The numbers were six thousand dead. He writes that he is sorry for the numbers.

QUEEN
Six thousand. He would apologize for the weather.

ARCHIVIST
He writes that the child should not learn his name first. The child should learn yours.

QUEEN
Let go of the letter.

ARCHIVIST
You’ll notice the blood.

QUEEN
I want to notice it.

ARCHIVIST
You’ll look for what it means.

QUEEN
It means he was alive when he wrote it.

(ARCHIVIST releases the scroll. QUEEN holds it. She does not look at it. She looks at the ARCHIVIST.)

QUEEN
When the rider came. You spoke to her.

ARCHIVIST
Yes.

QUEEN
What did she say. Not what he wrote. What she saw.

ARCHIVIST
She saw him fall.

(Beat.)

QUEEN
Fall.

ARCHIVIST
Fall. She was clear. She used that word.

QUEEN
Not “killed.”

ARCHIVIST
She said fall.

QUEEN
A man can fall and rise.

ARCHIVIST
She saw him fall and saw no rising.

QUEEN
But she didn’t stay.

ARCHIVIST
The line had broken. She was sent. She did her duty.

QUEEN
She did her duty.

(QUEEN looks down at the scroll. She traces the wax seal with one finger. She does not break it.)

QUEEN
You kept it sealed.

ARCHIVIST
It’s sealed.

QUEEN
I could decide not to open it.

ARCHIVIST
You could.

QUEEN
A letter unopened is every outcome at once.

ARCHIVIST
That’s no way to rule.

QUEEN
That’s the only way.

(QUEEN holds the scroll out.)

QUEEN
Keep it.

(ARCHIVIST stares at her.)

ARCHIVIST
Keep it.

QUEEN
In the archive. With the others.

ARCHIVIST
You don’t want—

QUEEN
File it. Under his name. Date it for today.

ARCHIVIST
It’s a letter from the front. It belongs in the war records.

QUEEN
It belongs where I say it belongs.

ARCHIVIST
If you don’t read it—

QUEEN
(cutting across)
I’ve read it. You read it to me. The pass was held and lost and held. Six thousand. He rallied them. He sends his love to the child and asks for a name that isn’t his. That’s the letter.

ARCHIVIST
There might be more.

QUEEN
Is there more?

ARCHIVIST
I don’t know. I never read the second page.

(Beat. QUEEN stares at her.)

QUEEN
You said—

ARCHIVIST
I said there was blood. I saw the blood. I turned the page, I saw the blood, I sealed it again and came here.

QUEEN
You were protecting me.

ARCHIVIST
I was protecting myself.

(They look at each other. The scroll is in the space between them, held by neither.)

QUEEN
(quiet)
You’re afraid of what he wrote to me.

ARCHIVIST
I’m afraid of what you’ll become when you read it.
