(A church hall kitchen. A trestle table, trays of sandwiches, a tea urn. RAY, in a dark overcoat too short in the sleeves, stretches cling film over a tray. NEIL stands in the doorway, car keys in his hand.)

NEIL
They're asking for more tea.

RAY
They can ask.

NEIL
I can do it.

RAY
You have to hold the switch down. It's got a knack.

NEIL
Right.

(He doesn't move.)

Good turnout.

RAY
He owed most of them money.

NEIL
The woman in the hat —

RAY
Maureen.

NEIL
She called me Ray.

RAY
What did you say?

NEIL
I said thank you for coming.

(RAY tears the cling film.)

NEIL
Is that his coat?

RAY
It's cold in that church.

NEIL
It's not cold in here.

RAY
Egg or ham. There's about forty left. You could take some for the drive.

NEIL
I'm not driving tonight.

RAY
Aren't you.

NEIL
I've got the room till Thursday.

RAY
What's Thursday?

NEIL
Nothing. It was cheaper by the —

RAY
There's a man coming Wednesday. To the house.

NEIL
What man?

RAY
He measures.

NEIL
That's quick.

RAY
I had the gutters done in March. You'd not know that.

NEIL
I wasn't saying —

RAY
New boiler the year before. Four thousand, that was.

NEIL
Ray. I haven't asked for anything.

RAY
No. You haven't asked.

(NEIL puts his keys down on the table. Picks them up again.)

NEIL
Was he in the front room? At the end.

RAY
They bring a bed.

NEIL
Could he talk?

RAY
He could talk.

NEIL
What about?

RAY
The bins. Had I put the bins out. He thought the nurse was stealing teaspoons.

NEIL
Was she?

RAY
We're down two.

(NEIL laughs. RAY doesn't.)

NEIL
Anything else?

RAY
Like what?

NEIL
I don't know. People say things.

RAY
Do they.

NEIL
I rang. In the summer.

RAY
I know you rang.

NEIL
He told you?

RAY
I was stood there.

NEIL
He didn't say a lot.

RAY
No.

NEIL
After, though. When he'd put it down. Did he —

RAY
There's a form. The solicitor's. It only wants a signature, it's nothing. I've got it in the car.

NEIL
What does it say?

RAY
It says who was there.

NEIL
Who was there.

RAY
It's a page. Page and a half.

NEIL
And the man on Wednesday, he needs it.

RAY
He doesn't need it. It's tidier.

NEIL
For who?

RAY
You want ham? You always had ham.

NEIL
I'll read it.

RAY
There's nothing to read.

NEIL
Then I'll be quick.

(Pause. RAY presses the cling film flat with both hands.)

NEIL
It doesn't fit you.

RAY
It fits.

NEIL
The sleeves.

RAY
He was shorter than he looked.

NEIL
He had it on at Mum's. Same coat.

RAY
You weren't at Mum's.

NEIL
I was at the back.

(RAY looks at him for the first time.)

RAY
You weren't.

NEIL
By the door. He had that on, and you had the grey suit, and you held his elbow going down the steps.

RAY
You could have come to the house.

NEIL
Could I?

(RAY turns back to the tray.)

NEIL
Is there anything in the pockets?

RAY
I haven't looked.

NEIL
You've had it on all day.

RAY
I haven't looked.

NEIL
Not even your hands? In that church?

RAY
I had the order of service.

NEIL
Ray.

RAY
What?

NEIL
Can I —

RAY
What?

(NEIL shakes his head.)

VOICE (off)
Ray? Is there any tea, love?

RAY
Coming, Maureen.

(He doesn't go. He unbuttons the coat, slowly, takes it off, and stands holding it. Then he lays it on the table among the trays, collar toward NEIL.)

RAY
I'll get the form.

NEIL
Now?

RAY
It's only in the car.

NEIL
Ray, if he said something —

RAY
Hold the switch down. Count to ten or it trips.

(RAY exits. NEIL looks at the coat. He reaches for the near pocket and stops, his hand flat on the cloth.)

VOICE (off)
Ray?

(NEIL crosses to the urn and holds the switch down. He stands there, arm out, looking back at the coat. The urn begins to tick.)

NEIL
One. Two.

(Lights down.)
