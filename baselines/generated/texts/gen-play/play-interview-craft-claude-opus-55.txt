(An office. A desk, two chairs. A glass of water on the desk, untouched. ROSS reads a CV. CLAIRE sits very upright, a fountain pen in her hand.)

ROSS
Sorry. Printer.

CLAIRE
That's fine.

ROSS
It does this. Prints page two first.

CLAIRE
I can tell you what's on page one.

ROSS
No, no.

(He turns the pages over. Turns them back.)

ROSS
You were at Halbrook.

CLAIRE
Four years.

ROSS
I know people at Halbrook.

CLAIRE
Everyone knows people at Halbrook.

ROSS
Do you know Ian Feld?

CLAIRE
Is he still there?

ROSS
You'd know.

CLAIRE
Would I?

(Pause. He gestures at her pen.)

ROSS
May I? Mine's —

CLAIRE
Of course.

(She hands it over. He uncaps it, writes something, looks at the nib.)

ROSS
Heavy.

CLAIRE
You get used to it.

ROSS
So. Why here?

CLAIRE
Your growth figures.

ROSS
Those are public.

CLAIRE
That's why I could read them.

ROSS
I meant why this role.

CLAIRE
It's a good role.

ROSS
It's a junior role.

CLAIRE
Is it?

ROSS
It's on the advert.

CLAIRE
Adverts.

(He clicks the cap on and off.)

ROSS
You've managed teams.

CLAIRE
Small ones.

ROSS
Eleven people.

CLAIRE
You did read page one.

ROSS
This role reports to me.

CLAIRE
Yes.

ROSS
Directly.

CLAIRE
That's what I understood.

ROSS
And you'd be comfortable with that.

CLAIRE
Who does your role report to?

ROSS
That's not —

CLAIRE
Just for the structure.

ROSS
Patricia.

CLAIRE
Patricia Unwin.

ROSS
You know her.

CLAIRE
I know the name.

ROSS
From where?

CLAIRE
Is that water for me?

(Pause.)

ROSS
Yes.

(She doesn't take it.)

ROSS
Where do you see yourself in five years?

CLAIRE
Do people still answer that?

ROSS
They try.

CLAIRE
Where did you see yourself, five years ago?

ROSS
Here, probably.

CLAIRE
Well then.

ROSS
That's not an answer.

CLAIRE
It's a good outcome.

(He writes something. Presses too hard. Looks at the nib.)

ROSS
Did Patricia ask you to apply?

CLAIRE
I'm sorry?

ROSS
It's a simple —

CLAIRE
I saw the advert.

ROSS
On a Tuesday. It went up on a Tuesday and came down on the Wednesday. We had four applications.

CLAIRE
I'm quick.

ROSS
You're very quick.

CLAIRE
Is that a weakness? You'll ask me for a weakness.

ROSS
I wasn't going to.

CLAIRE
Impatience.

ROSS
I didn't ask.

CLAIRE
I'm giving it to you anyway.

(Silence. He puts the pen down on his side of the desk. She looks at it.)

ROSS
Ian Feld said you left Halbrook quite suddenly.

CLAIRE
So you did speak to him.

ROSS
He rang me.

CLAIRE
Before or after you saw my name?

ROSS
Does it matter?

CLAIRE
To Ian, probably.

ROSS
He said you're very good at —

CLAIRE
I am.

ROSS
— at rooms. At getting into rooms.

CLAIRE
That's kind of him.

ROSS
I don't think he meant it kindly.

CLAIRE
Then it's kind of you to pass it on.

(She reaches for the water. Stops. Leaves it.)

CLAIRE
Can I ask you something?

ROSS
That's usually at the end.

CLAIRE
Is this the end?

(Pause.)

ROSS
How long have you known Patricia?

CLAIRE
You keep coming back to her.

ROSS
You keep not answering.

CLAIRE
You keep not asking me about the job.

ROSS
What would you like to know about the job?

CLAIRE
Whose desk it is.

ROSS
Sorry?

CLAIRE
The one I'd sit at.

ROSS
It's empty.

CLAIRE
Since when?

ROSS
It's just empty.

CLAIRE
And yours?

ROSS
What about mine?

CLAIRE
Is it the one by the window?

(Long pause. He picks up the pen again, turns it over.)

ROSS
This is a good pen.

CLAIRE
Thank you.

ROSS
Someone give it to you?

CLAIRE
Why?

ROSS
People don't buy themselves pens like this.

CLAIRE
Some people do.

ROSS
Not at your level.

CLAIRE
Which level is that?

(He doesn't answer. He stands. She doesn't.)

ROSS
I think we've covered everything.

CLAIRE
We've covered Ian.

ROSS
We'll be in touch.

CLAIRE
Both of you?

ROSS
I'm sorry?

CLAIRE
You and Patricia. Will you both be in touch, or —

ROSS
I'll be in touch.

CLAIRE
Good.

(She stands. Holds out her hand. He goes to shake it. She turns her palm up. A beat. He looks at the pen in his hand.)

ROSS
Oh.

(He doesn't move. Neither does she.)

ROSS
Do you want the water? You never touched the water.

(She keeps her hand out.)

(Blackout.)
