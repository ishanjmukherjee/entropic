A chamber in the castle. Night. A single candle burns on a table beside a chessboard, the game half-played. QUEEN ISOLDE sits alone, a shawl about her shoulders, studying the board. A knock. She does not look up.

ISOLDE: Come.

Enter CORRAN, a young messenger, perhaps seventeen. He is mud to the thigh. He kneels at once, too fast, and nearly falls.

CORRAN: Majesty. I come from the field at Ashwater, with word from—

ISOLDE: When did you last eat?

CORRAN: (thrown) Majesty?

ISOLDE: It's a simple question. Even soldiers can manage it.

CORRAN: Yesterday. Before the dawn. I think.

ISOLDE: (She rises, takes bread and a cup from a sideboard, and sets them on the table.) Sit.

CORRAN: I can't sit in your presence, Majesty.

ISOLDE: You can do whatever I tell you. That's what a presence is. Sit.

He sits on the very edge of the chair. He does not touch the bread.

ISOLDE: You rode through the night.

CORRAN: Yes, Majesty.

ISOLDE: And you killed a horse doing it.

CORRAN: (ashamed) Two.

ISOLDE: Then the news is not good. Nobody kills two horses to tell a woman she's won. (Beat.) Eat something first. Whatever you've carried this far can wait for one mouthful.

He tears off a piece of bread and chews it as if it were sand. She watches him. She returns to her chair across the chessboard.

ISOLDE: Now. You have a speech prepared. They always give you a speech.

CORRAN: Lord Marshal Hendry bade me say—

ISOLDE: I don't want Hendry's words. Hendry would describe the end of the world as a regrettable shift in position. Tell me what you saw.

Pause. CORRAN looks at his hands.

CORRAN: We met them at the ford, as His Majesty planned. In the morning it went well. We held the bank. Then their horse came round through the marsh. Nobody thought horses could cross the marsh. They came out of the reeds behind us, and the line just... it came apart, Majesty. Like wet paper.

ISOLDE: The King?

CORRAN: The King lives.

She closes her eyes, briefly. It is not quite relief.

ISOLDE: Go on.

CORRAN: His Majesty was cut off by the river. There was only the old bridge, the narrow one, and their horse were coming down on it. Someone had to hold the bridge so the King's guard could get him across.

ISOLDE: (very still) Someone.

CORRAN: The Prince asked for it, Majesty. I heard him ask. He took forty men onto the bridge.

ISOLDE: And the King said yes.

CORRAN: (He hesitates.) The King said—the King said it was well thought of.

Long silence. ISOLDE reaches out and moves a piece on the chessboard: a white knight, set forward. Then she lays it on its side.

ISOLDE: How long did they hold?

CORRAN: Long enough. The King crossed. And the guard. And some of us after.

ISOLDE: You were on the bridge.

CORRAN: At the far end. He sent me back, Majesty. He said, "Corran, you're the lightest rider we have. Go and tell my mother." (His voice breaks.) I didn't want to go. I want you to know I didn't want to go.

ISOLDE: I know.

CORRAN: He said to tell you—

ISOLDE: (sharp) Don't. (Softer.) Not yet. Not tonight. Tomorrow I will ask you, and you will tell me exactly, every word. Tonight I don't have room for it.

CORRAN: Yes, Majesty.

ISOLDE: How old are you, Corran?

CORRAN: Seventeen at harvest.

ISOLDE: He'd have been nineteen. (Beat.) Where is the King now?

CORRAN: Riding for Harrowgate, with what's left of the guard. Two days behind me. Perhaps three.

ISOLDE: And the enemy?

CORRAN: Behind him.

ISOLDE stands. She pulls the shawl off her shoulders and drops it on the chair. Something in her has changed. She is no longer a mother waiting for news.

ISOLDE: Then here is what will happen. You will go down to the kitchens and eat a proper meal, and then you'll sleep in a real bed. In the morning you'll find Captain Ruel at the gatehouse and tell him what you told me—all of it. The marsh. The horse. He'll want to know how many.

CORRAN: And the King, Majesty?

ISOLDE: The King will be welcomed home. The gates will be open and the walls will be manned, and when he rides in he will find his castle ready and his queen on the steps. (She looks at the fallen knight.) And he will have to look at me.

CORRAN rises, bows, and goes to the door. He stops.

CORRAN: Majesty. He held a long time. I wanted you to know that, at least.

ISOLDE: (without turning) Of course he did. He was mine.

CORRAN exits. ISOLDE stands alone. She picks up the fallen knight, holds it a moment, and closes her fist around it. She blows out the candle.

Darkness.

END OF SCENE
