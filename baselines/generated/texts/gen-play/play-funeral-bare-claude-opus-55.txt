A back porch, late afternoon. A screen door leads into a house where muffled voices and the clink of dishes suggest a reception winding down. A cooler sits by the steps. Two folding chairs. WALT, fifties, tie loosened, sits holding a paper plate he hasn't touched. The screen door creaks. DANNY, late forties, in a suit that fits too well for this town, steps out. He stops when he sees WALT.

DANNY: I can go back in.

WALT: It's a porch. It's not mine.

DANNY: It's a little yours.

(DANNY sits in the other chair, leaving space between them. Silence.)

DANNY: Mrs. Kowalski asked if I was the one in Denver.

WALT: What'd you say?

DANNY: I said Portland. She said, "Well, he talked about Denver."

WALT: He got them mixed up.

DANNY: For eleven years?

WALT: He wasn't a geography man.

(A beat. DANNY almost laughs, doesn't.)

DANNY: You did a good job. The service. The flowers.

WALT: Carol did the flowers.

DANNY: The eulogy, then.

WALT: You didn't have to say that.

DANNY: I know I didn't have to.

(WALT sets the plate down on the cooler.)

WALT: You could've done it. The eulogy. I'd have let you.

DANNY: No, you wouldn't.

WALT: No. I wouldn't.

(DANNY reaches into his jacket and takes out a pair of drugstore reading glasses, one arm bent.)

DANNY: These were in the truck. Glove box. I went out to sit in it before. I don't know why.

WALT: He had fifteen pairs of those. He'd lose them on his own head.

DANNY: The arm's bent.

WALT: He sat on them at the VFW. Said they worked fine.

DANNY: They don't sit straight.

WALT: He said the world didn't either.

(DANNY turns the glasses over in his hands.)

DANNY: Did he ask for me? At the end?

(WALT takes a long time.)

WALT: He asked for a lot of things. He asked for Mom. He asked if the Packers won. He asked me to check the furnace three times a day.

DANNY: That's not an answer.

WALT: He asked if you'd called.

DANNY: And?

WALT: And I said you were on your way.

DANNY: I wasn't.

WALT: I know you weren't.

DANNY: You told me it wasn't serious. On the phone, in March, you said—

WALT: I said the doctors said—

DANNY: You said don't bother coming, Walt. Those were the words. "Don't bother."

WALT: (standing) Because you'd come for a weekend, and you'd fix things, and you'd leave, and I'd be the one he'd look at like I let you go. Again.

(Pause. The dishes inside go quiet, then resume.)

DANNY: You think I left because of you?

WALT: I think you left and I stayed and somebody had to drive him to dialysis.

DANNY: I sent money.

WALT: He didn't want money.

DANNY: He took it.

WALT: He took it because it was the only thing you sent.

(DANNY stands. They face each other. For a moment it might become something physical. Then DANNY holds out the glasses.)

DANNY: Here.

WALT: Keep them.

DANNY: I don't want them.

WALT: Then why'd you take them out of the truck?

(DANNY has no answer. He lowers his hand.)

DANNY: He called me once. Two years ago. Middle of the night. He thought I was you. He said, "Walter, I'm sorry I was hard on you. You're the good one." And I didn't correct him.

(WALT sits down slowly.)

WALT: Why not?

DANNY: Because he sounded happy. And I thought, that's the best I'm going to get. Being you for four minutes.

(Long silence. WALT picks up the paper plate, looks at it, sets it back down.)

WALT: He told me once you were the brave one. For going.

DANNY: He never said that.

WALT: He was drunk. Christmas. He said it to the dog, technically. But I was in the room.

(DANNY sits. Something releases in his shoulders.)

DANNY: So he got us mixed up too.

WALT: He wasn't a geography man.

(DANNY laughs, finally, a short broken sound. WALT doesn't laugh, but he doesn't look away. DANNY unfolds the glasses and, after a moment, puts them on. They sit crooked on his face.)

DANNY: How do I look?

WALT: Like him. Unfortunately.

DANNY: They don't sit straight.

WALT: No.

(WALT opens the cooler, takes out two cans of beer, hands one across the gap between the chairs. DANNY takes it. Neither opens his. They sit, looking out at the yard as the light goes.)

Lights fade.

END OF SCENE
