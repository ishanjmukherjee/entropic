THE WEIGHT OF THE RIVER

A Scene

CHARACTERS:
QUEEN ISOLDA, fifties, twenty years on the throne
A MESSENGER, young, mud-spattered, a soldier's bearing gone ragged
LADY MARENNE, the Queen's attendant

SETTING: A private chamber in the palace, late at night. A single brazier burns. A table holds maps weighted down with stones, a cup, a half-eaten plate. QUEEN ISOLDA stands over the maps. LADY MARENNE waits near the door.

(A knock. MARENNE opens the door a crack, listens, turns.)

MARENNE: A rider, Majesty. From the Vessen crossing.

ISOLDA: (not looking up) At this hour. Then he rode through the night, and no one rides through the night with good news. Let him in.

(The MESSENGER enters. He kneels, unsteady. He is holding a leather dispatch case and does not offer it.)

ISOLDA: You're bleeding on my floor.

MESSENGER: It isn't mine, Majesty. Most of it.

ISOLDA: Stand up. Marenne, give him water.

(MARENNE pours. The MESSENGER drinks too fast, coughs.)

ISOLDA: Now. You have a case in your hand and you haven't given it to me. So either it's empty or you've been told to say it aloud first.

MESSENGER: Lord Harrow said the letter would tell you the numbers. He said I was to tell you the rest.

ISOLDA: Harrow wrote it? Not the Marshal?

(Silence.)

ISOLDA: I see. Go on.

MESSENGER: The Marshal fell in the first hour, Majesty. At the ford. The river was higher than the scouts reported. There'd been rain in the hills, three days of it, and nobody on our side of the water knew. The vanguard went in to their chests. The horses wouldn't hold.

ISOLDA: And the enemy was waiting on the far bank.

MESSENGER: No, Majesty. That's the thing. They were behind us.

(ISOLDA finally looks up from the maps.)

ISOLDA: Behind.

MESSENGER: They'd crossed at Dunmere two nights before. The bridge there was meant to be burned.

ISOLDA: I gave that order myself. I put my seal on it.

MESSENGER: Yes, Majesty. The order arrived. The garrison at Dunmere had already opened the gates.

(A pause. ISOLDA moves one of the stones on the map, very precisely, an inch to the left.)

ISOLDA: Dunmere is held by my cousin.

MESSENGER: Yes, Majesty.

ISOLDA: Say it plainly. I'll not have it hinted at.

MESSENGER: The Duke's banners were in their line. I saw them myself. The white heron, beside the enemy's colours.

MARENNE: Majesty, perhaps you should sit down.

ISOLDA: I have been sitting for twenty years, Marenne. It appears that was the difficulty. (to the MESSENGER) How many came back across?

MESSENGER: Lord Harrow holds the ridge above Vessen with perhaps two thousand. He can keep it four days. Five if the rain comes back.

ISOLDA: Two thousand. I sent nine.

MESSENGER: Yes, Majesty.

(ISOLDA holds out her hand. He gives her the case. She does not open it.)

ISOLDA: There's something more. You've been holding your breath since you came in.

MESSENGER: The Prince was with the vanguard.

(MARENNE's hand goes to her mouth. ISOLDA is entirely still.)

ISOLDA: He was to stay with the reserve. That was agreed.

MESSENGER: He rode forward when the Marshal's standard went down. He took it up himself. The men rallied to it. That's the only reason any of us reached the ridge.

ISOLDA: I didn't ask what he did. I asked nothing yet. Is he living?

MESSENGER: I don't know, Majesty.

ISOLDA: You don't know.

MESSENGER: He was taken, not killed. I saw them pull him from the water. They were careful with him. They knew who he was.

ISOLDA: Of course they knew. My cousin dandled him on his knee. (She sets the case on the table.) What is your name?

MESSENGER: Tobin, Majesty. Tobin Ashe.

ISOLDA: How old are you, Tobin Ashe?

MESSENGER: Nineteen.

ISOLDA: His age. (beat) You'll eat and sleep. In the morning you'll ride back to Lord Harrow with my answer.

MESSENGER: What answer, Majesty?

(ISOLDA takes the stone that marks Dunmere and closes her fist around it.)

ISOLDA: That he is to hold four days. And that the Duke will send terms before then, with my son's name written into them. (to MARENNE) Wake the council. All of them. And send for whoever carried my seal to Dunmere.

MARENNE: And if the terms come, Majesty?

ISOLDA: Then I'll learn what a crown weighs against a boy. (She opens her hand and looks at the stone.) I had always supposed I knew.

(She sets it back on the map. Lights fade on the brazier.)

END OF SCENE
