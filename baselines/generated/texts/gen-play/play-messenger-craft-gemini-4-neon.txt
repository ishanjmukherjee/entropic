(The QUEEN sits at a table. A small silver bell sits beside her elbow. The MESSENGER stands near the door, mud up to his knees.)

QUEEN
You’ve brought the road in with you.

MESSENGER
I didn’t stop at the—

QUEEN
I can see you didn’t stop. Don’t stand on the rug.

(He steps back onto the stone.)

MESSENGER
The Captain said you’d want it straight.

QUEEN
The Captain says a great many things. Did he give you water?

MESSENGER
No, Majesty.

QUEEN
He’s getting careless. Sit down. Not there.

MESSENGER
I’d rather stand.

QUEEN
You’re shaking.

MESSENGER
It’s the rain.

QUEEN
It hasn’t rained here since Tuesday.

MESSENGER
It rained at the river.

(She looks at the silver bell, then back at him.)

QUEEN
The river’s high, then.

MESSENGER
High enough.

QUEEN
Good. It’ll slow them down.

MESSENGER
It slowed us down.

QUEEN
You’re very young to be riding for the General.

MESSENGER
I’m twenty-four.

QUEEN
You look sixteen. Did you tell the Captain?

MESSENGER
Tell him what?

QUEEN
Whatever you’ve come to tell me.

MESSENGER
He asked for the bag. I said it was for your hands.

QUEEN
Where is the bag?

MESSENGER
I lost the horse three miles back.

QUEEN
And the bag?

MESSENGER
With the horse.

(She reaches out and rests a finger on the rim of the bell.)

QUEEN
You walked three miles.

MESSENGER
Ran some of it.

QUEEN
And you didn’t tell the Captain.

MESSENGER
No.

QUEEN
Why?

MESSENGER
Because he’s got a loud voice.

QUEEN
He does. He’s also got forty men in the courtyard.

MESSENGER
I saw them. They’re sharpening pikes.

QUEEN
They’re always sharpening pikes. It keeps them from drinking.

MESSENGER
They won’t need them.

QUEEN
No?

MESSENGER
Not for a day or two.

(She pulls her hand back from the bell.)

QUEEN
Tell me about the bridge.

MESSENGER
Which one?

QUEEN
Don’t be clever.

MESSENGER
The stone one’s still there. They’re using it.

QUEEN
And the General?

MESSENGER
He sent his regards.

QUEEN
He’s never sent his regards in his life.

MESSENGER
He was in a hurry.

QUEEN
South or east?

MESSENGER
He didn’t say. He just told me to ride until the horse dropped and then keep going.

QUEEN
You’re a very obedient boy.

MESSENGER
I’m twenty-four.

QUEEN
You said. What’s your name?

MESSENGER
Thomas.

QUEEN
Thomas. If I ring this bell, the Captain comes in.

MESSENGER
Yes.

QUEEN
And he takes you to the kitchens. You get a hot meal. Dry boots. A bed in the barracks.

MESSENGER
I don’t want a bed in the barracks.

QUEEN
You can barely stand.

MESSENGER
My sister lives in the lower town. Near the tannery.

QUEEN
The lower town’s a swamp.

MESSENGER
She’s got a cart.

QUEEN
Has she.

MESSENGER
No horse, though. They took the mule in April.

QUEEN
The army needed mules.

MESSENGER
They don’t need them anymore.

(She stares at him. He doesn’t look away.)

QUEEN
The South Gate is closed at dusk.

MESSENGER
It’s not dusk yet.

QUEEN
It’s closed to carts.

MESSENGER
Unless there’s a paper.

QUEEN
Why would I give you a paper?

MESSENGER
Because I didn’t tell the Captain.

QUEEN
You think he won’t find out?

MESSENGER
He’ll find out tomorrow. Maybe tonight, if the riders from the ridge get through.

QUEEN
The ridge held?

MESSENGER
I didn’t say it held. I said riders might get through.

QUEEN
If I ring this bell—

MESSENGER
Then I’ll tell him.

QUEEN
You’ll tell him what? You don’t have a dispatch. You don’t have a seal. You’re a boy in a wet coat.

MESSENGER
I’ve got the General’s ring.

(He doesn’t reach for his pocket.)

QUEEN
He took it off?

MESSENGER
I took it off.

QUEEN
You’re a thief as well.

MESSENGER
He wasn’t using it.

QUEEN
Give it to me.

MESSENGER
I’ll leave it on the table. On my way out.

QUEEN
One horse?

MESSENGER
Two. The cart’s heavy.

QUEEN
I’m not giving you two horses.

MESSENGER
Then I’ll take one. And a paper with the red wax, not the black. The gatekeeper can’t read, he only knows the colour.

QUEEN
You know a lot about the South Gate.

MESSENGER
I’ve been thinking about it since the river.

QUEEN
If I write this paper, you don’t stop at the tannery. You don’t stop anywhere.

MESSENGER
I’m not leaving her.

QUEEN
I don’t care who you leave. You’re through the gate before the bell rings for vespers.

MESSENGER
That’s an hour.

QUEEN
Then you’d better run.

(She doesn’t move to write. She picks up the silver bell by the handle, holding it just above the wood, keeping the clapper still.)

QUEEN
Who else knows?

MESSENGER
Nobody.

QUEEN
You’re lying.

MESSENGER
Everybody who knows is still at the river.

QUEEN
Give me the ring.

(He reaches into his coat, pulls out a muddy ring, and sets it on the edge of the table. He steps back.)

QUEEN
You’re sure he’s dead?

MESSENGER
I didn’t say he was dead.

(She looks at the ring. Her hand shifts, and the bell gives a tiny, dull clink. Neither of them moves.)
