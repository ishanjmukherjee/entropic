(Night. A table, one candle, a plate of food half eaten. The QUEEN sits. The MESSENGER stands inside the door, mud to the thigh, holding out a leather dispatch case. She does not take it.)

QUEEN
You came by the kitchen stair.

MESSENGER
The gate was shut, Majesty.

QUEEN
The gate is shut every night. People knock.

MESSENGER
I didn't like to wake the house.

QUEEN
No. (She eats.) Who saw you?

MESSENGER
A girl with a bucket.

QUEEN
Did she see that?

MESSENGER
It was under my coat.

QUEEN
Put it back under your coat.

(He crosses and sets it on the table. She moves her plate away from it.)

MESSENGER
It's from the Lord Marshal. His own seal.

QUEEN
I can see whose seal. Where is his rider?

MESSENGER
I'm his rider.

QUEEN
His rider is a man called Orrin with a red beard. He stands just there and he drips.

MESSENGER
Orrin's horse went lame.

QUEEN
Where?

MESSENGER
At the river.

QUEEN
Which side?

(Pause.)

MESSENGER
I've not eaten since Tuesday, Majesty.

QUEEN
(Pushing the plate toward him.) Which side of the river?

MESSENGER
The far side.

QUEEN
So there is still a far side.

MESSENGER
There was on Tuesday.

QUEEN
Eat.

(He eats, standing, with his hands.)

QUEEN
What are you, when you aren't his rider?

MESSENGER
I see to the horses.

QUEEN
A groom.

MESSENGER
Farrier's boy.

QUEEN
And the Lord Marshal looked about him, at all his captains, and gave his seal to the farrier's boy.

MESSENGER
There wasn't a great crowd to choose from.

(She looks at the case. She does not touch it.)

QUEEN
Is it long? The letter.

MESSENGER
I've not read it.

QUEEN
I didn't ask if you'd read it. You saw him write it.

MESSENGER
I saw it written.

QUEEN
That is not what I —

MESSENGER
It's short.

(She gets up, goes to the window, comes back.)

QUEEN
The council sits at nine. At ten the Tessaly envoy signs. Six weeks he's been deciding, and tonight he has the pen in his hand. Do you know what an envoy does when a short letter arrives at breakfast?

MESSENGER
No, Majesty.

QUEEN
No. How was the road?

MESSENGER
Bad.

QUEEN
Slow, then.

MESSENGER
I made good time.

QUEEN
You made poor time. The road was bad. A man on that road might not be seen here before noon.

MESSENGER
I'm here now.

QUEEN
A girl with a bucket says so.

(He stops eating.)

MESSENGER
I'd want a horse.

QUEEN
You have a horse.

MESSENGER
It's got the army's brand on it. I'd want one without. And a paper.

QUEEN
What kind of paper?

MESSENGER
One that says I can go south.

QUEEN
The war is north.

MESSENGER
Yes, Majesty.

(She picks up the case at last. She turns the seal to the candle.)

QUEEN
There's a thumb in this wax. A small one.

MESSENGER
The wax was hot.

QUEEN
Show me your hand.

(He wipes it down his coat.)

MESSENGER
If you open it you'll know.

QUEEN
If I open it I'll have opened it.

(She puts the case down on his side of the table. She takes paper from the drawer and writes. He watches the pen.)

MESSENGER
He said you'd not take it. He said to tell you —

QUEEN
How do you spell your name?

MESSENGER
I don't.

QUEEN
Then you're "the bearer." (She writes. She presses her own ring into the wax, slowly, and holds the paper up. He reaches. She keeps hold.) The grey mare in the end stall. You sleep in the straw and nobody sees you. At noon you knock at the gate, in front of everyone, with that in your hand. Then south.

MESSENGER
Noon.

QUEEN
You can count to noon.

MESSENGER
Yes, Majesty.

(She lets go. He folds the paper inside his shirt. He takes the case from the table and goes to the door. He stops.)

QUEEN
Well?

MESSENGER
Orrin's beard wasn't red, Majesty. It was only the mud up there. It's red mud.

(He bends, sets the case on the floor just inside the door, and goes. His steps on the stair, then nothing.)

(The QUEEN does not move. Then she takes the knife from the plate, wipes it on her sleeve, and crosses to the case. She stands over it. Below, a horse is led out across the yard.)

(She looks at the window. She looks at the case.)

(The candle.)
