A bare office. A desk, two chairs. Fluorescent hum. MARLENE, 50s, poised, sits behind the desk. GARY, 30s, enters, chatty, carrying a manila folder.

GARY
(extending hand)
Gary Flemming. Thank you for this opportunity, Ms.—

MARLENE
(not shaking it)
Sit.

GARY sits. The chair collapses. He sprawls.

GARY
—Hawthorne!

He laughs, alone. MARLENE does not move. GARY rights the chair; one leg splinters. He sits cautiously, listing.

MARLENE
Your résumé says "adaptable."

GARY
(still finding balance)
I—yes. Absolutely.

MARLENE
(looking at paper)
Five jobs in three years.

GARY
Growth phases.

MARLENE
You were fired from all five.

GARY
(correcting)
"Farewelled." Different energy.

Silence. MARLENE opens the manila folder GARY brought. It's empty.

MARLENE
Your portfolio.

GARY
Yes! Portfolio. The—the folder is the portfolio. Conceptually. Empty space, potential, you know? As an event coordinator—

MARLENE
This is a tax firm.

GARY
(beat)
Events have tax implications.

He pulls a crumpled receipt from his pocket.

GARY
(cont'd)
Charitable gala. 2019. I coordinated. Attendees paid taxes. Indirectly.

MARLENE
(closing empty folder)
Where do you see yourself in five years?

GARY
(brightening; this he knows)
Here! Growing with Hawthorne & Associates. Learning from you. Maybe your job, if we're being—no, not your job, obviously you're here, I meant—adjacent to your job. A lateral you. Your—

The broken chair leg snaps. GARY crashes again, this time hitting a wastebasket. Coffee cups scatter.

MARLENE stands, moves to window, her back to him.

MARLENE
Do you know what we do here, Mr. Flemming?

GARY, on floor, gathering cups.

GARY
Tax preparation. For individuals and small businesses. You— (quoting) —"take the anxiety out of inheritance."

MARLENE
(turning)
My father founded this firm. I have buried two comptrollers. Fired six. I have not replaced the chair in this office since 1987.

GARY
(still on floor)
Loyalty. I respect that.

MARLENE
It is an interview chair.

GARY stops. Looks at chair.

GARY
Oh.

MARLENE
Everyone sits in it.

GARY
And... how many...

MARLENE
You are the fourteenth to break it.

GARY
I'm not the—

He sees her face. Stands slowly, wincing.

GARY
I am the fourteenth.

MARLENE
The last one was a CPA. Ivy League. cried for twenty minutes.

GARY
(quietly)
I won't cry.

MARLENE
(almost to herself)
I thought the CPA might work out.

She returns to desk. Sits. GARY remains standing.

MARLENE
Please. Sit.

They look at the broken chair together.

GARY
I think I'll stand.

MARLENE
Suit yourself.

She opens a drawer, removes a letter, slides it across.

MARLENE
Early retirement. Effective Friday. I'm giving this to myself.

GARY
(picking up letter)
You're... retiring?

MARLENE
Forty-three years. My father died in this office. Tuesday afternoon. Heart attack. Client meeting. I was nineteen, temping across town. No one told me until Thursday.

GARY doesn't know where to put the letter. Holds it.

MARLENE
(cont'd)
I always thought I'd be—what's the word—driven out? No. (searching) —Replaced. Naturally. Someone better, climbing up. Instead I look around and everyone is falling. Into chairs. Into wastebaskets. Into—

She gestures at nothing.

MARLENE
You're not qualified for this job.

GARY
(honest)
Not even slightly.

MARLENE
You're not even the worst candidate I've seen this month.

GARY
Is that...?

MARLENE
It is not a compliment.

GARY
(looking at broken chair)
Can I—should I—?

MARLENE
Leave it. It stays broken. That's the system.

GARY places the retirement letter carefully on the desk. MARLENE watches.

MARLENE
What will you do? After this.

GARY
Probably cry. In my car. Professionally, I mean.

MARLENE
(almost smile)
I meant today. Generally.

GARY
Water my neighbor's plants while he's in Phoenix. He has a fern. It's dying. I overwater. (beat) Then I'll update my résumé. Change "adaptable" to something else. "Resilient." "Chair-resistant."

MARLENE
Don't.

GARY
?

MARLENE
Keep "adaptable." It irritates me less, knowing.

She stands. Extends her hand. GARY shakes it, surprised.

MARLENE
Goodbye, Mr. Flemming.

GARY
(at door, turning)
The retirement. Is it—are you going somewhere?

MARLENE
(sitting back down, alone with broken chair)
Phoenix, actually. My brother. Has a fern.

Blackout.

GARY's voice, distant:

GARY
(V.O.)
It's probably overwatered.

Silence.

Fluorescent hum. Alone.

End of scene.
