A one-act scene

SETTING: The queen's chamber. Night. A single candle burns on a small table. QUEEN MARGARET sits motionless in a chair, still dressed in formal mourning black, though the mourning is old and worn soft. A distant sound of wind. After a moment, hurried footsteps. A knock.

QUEEN: (without turning) Enter.

THE MESSENGER enters. He is young, mud-splattered, exhausted. He drops to one knee immediately, head bowed, holding a sealed letter before him in both hands like an offering.

MESSENGER: Majesty.

QUEEN: (finally turning) You've ridden hard. Your horse may drop.

MESSENGER: He has dropped, Majesty. At the gate. I ran the last mile.

QUEEN: (a long pause) Men only run to me when something has ended. Stand.

He rises but keeps his eyes down.

QUEEN: Look at me. If you carry what I think you carry, you will owe me the courtesy of your eyes.

The MESSENGER raises his head. His face is drawn.

QUEEN: There. Now I know. (She does not take the letter.) My son. Or my husband. Which is it you cannot say?

MESSENGER: Majesty, the letter is from Lord Cmdr. Vales—

QUEEN: The letter is a coward. Paper does not have to say the words with its own mouth. I am asking you. Which of my men is dead?

MESSENGER: (barely audible) Both, Majesty.

Silence. The QUEEN does not move. The candle flame trembles.

QUEEN: Both. (She stands slowly.) Both in one day. They were generous to me. Twelve years I have taken bad news in pieces, and now they give me the whole loaf at once. Sit down, boy.

MESSENGER: Majesty, I cannot—

QUEEN: You ran a mile. Sit. (He sits heavily on a stool.) Now. Tell me how. Not the letter's way — with soft phrases written by a secretary to spare me. Tell me the true way, the soldier's way. You were there.

MESSENGER: (haltingly) I was... I carried the standard, Majesty. Near the river. The King led the left flank himself. They broke our center at midday — the traitor Gorrin turned his cavalry, we didn't see it coming — and the King, he... he saw the rout beginning. He rode into it. To hold the line. He held it, Majesty. Three hours. They say the enemy lost heart just looking at him.

QUEEN: And the Prince?

MESSENGER: The Prince was with the reserve. When the King fell, word reached him, and he... (He stops.)

QUEEN: Go on.

MESSENGER: He charged the hill where they'd taken the King's body. He meant to bring it home. Men followed him because he was the King's son, and he fought like — Majesty, I have seen battles, I have never seen anything like it. He reached the hill. (A breath.) He did not reach back down.

QUEEN: (quietly) Was he brave?

MESSENGER: (with sudden force) Majesty, when they tell the story of this day in fifty years, your son's name will be in it. I swear it on my mother.

QUEEN: (after a long silence) Twenty years old.

MESSENGER: Yes, Majesty.

QUEEN: I forbade him to go. He knelt where you knelt, in that very spot, and he said: "Mother, a Prince who does not bleed for the kingdom has no right to inherit it." I struck him. (beat) The last thing I ever did to my son was strike him across the face.

MESSENGER: Majesty — he spoke of you. Before the charge. He said if he fell, we should tell you he had no regrets. He said... he said the blow was the truest thing I ever taught him. That is what he said, word for word. I memorized it, in case I lived.

The QUEEN turns away. Her hand grips the back of the chair. When she speaks, her voice is steadier than it should be.

QUEEN: What remains? Of the army. Numbers, boy.

MESSENGER: Perhaps four thousand, Majesty. They've fallen back to Corwell under General Brath. The passes are held. The kingdom stands.

QUEEN: The kingdom stands. (She takes the letter at last, weighs it in her hand without opening it.) You know what this is? Four thousand men are waiting tonight to hear whether they have a queen or a grave. And Gorrin the traitor is waiting too, to see if this breaks me.

She sets the letter on the table, unopened, beside the candle.

QUEEN: It will not break me. I have spent twelve years learning to hold my face like a wall. Now we find out what the wall was for. (She straightens.) What is your name?

MESSENGER: Aldric, Majesty.

QUEEN: Aldric. You will eat, and sleep — a real bed, in this castle — and in the morning you will carry my words to Corwell. Can you ride again so soon?

MESSENGER: (rising) I will ride tonight if you command it.

QUEEN: No. Tomorrow. Dead men can afford to wait one night; the living cannot. (She moves toward the door, then pauses.) Aldric. You ran a mile to bring a woman the worst news of her life, and you looked me in the eye when you gave it. That is worth more to me than a thousand soft letters. The kingdom will remember your name too.

She exits. The MESSENGER stands alone, looking at the unopened letter and the burning candle. Blackout.
