(A funeral parlour. A coffin on trestles. LEN is at the head of it. MICHAEL stops at the foot.)

LEN
They gave him a parting.

MICHAEL
Did they.

LEN
Come round here. You can see the line.

MICHAEL
I can see from here.

LEN
That's the lid you're looking at.

MICHAEL
I know what I'm looking at.

LEN
It's on hinges now. Used to be they lifted it right off.

MICHAEL
Are they waiting?

LEN
They can wait. There's a lady out there eating a pear.

MICHAEL
That's Auntie Val.

LEN
I know my own aunt.

MICHAEL
Then why's she a lady?

LEN
Well, look who's come to sort us out.

MICHAEL
I said I'd come.

LEN
You said you'd see.

MICHAEL
And here I am.

LEN
At the wrong end.

(MICHAEL moves up one side of the coffin.)

LEN
Other side. You'll get my shadow.

MICHAEL
Leave off a minute.

LEN
See what I mean about his hair?

MICHAEL
It's fine.

LEN
It's not his parting.

MICHAEL
Run your hand through it then.

LEN
You do it.

MICHAEL
I didn't say there was anything wrong with it.

LEN
You've still got that nail.

MICHAEL
What nail?

LEN
Thumb. The ridge in it.

MICHAEL
They don't grow out, Len.

LEN
Funny what does and what doesn't.

MICHAEL
Have you got enough men?

LEN
Men?

MICHAEL
To carry.

LEN
There's a thing on wheels.

MICHAEL
I saw Colin outside. He said six.

LEN
Six to the wheels. Then nobody. Then six again.

MICHAEL
So you've got six.

LEN
Colin can count to six. That's not the same thing.

MICHAEL
Which side are you on?

LEN
Why?

MICHAEL
You've got a bad shoulder.

LEN
Have I?

MICHAEL
I can see it from here.

LEN
You can see a lot from where you are.

MICHAEL
I can go that side.

LEN
In those shoes?

MICHAEL
They're black.

LEN
They're very black. You could tip him up and show him his parting.

(MICHAEL steps back.)

LEN
Where are you going?

MICHAEL
Give you your light.

LEN
I haven't got a bad shoulder.

MICHAEL
All right.

LEN
It's the elbow. I can take weight. I can't straighten it after. That's all.

MICHAEL
Colin can take the foot. He's tall.

LEN
You worked it all out in the car?

MICHAEL
I came on the bus.

LEN
You could've rung.

MICHAEL
You'd have been busy.

LEN
I drove past the stop.

MICHAEL
What time?

LEN
What does it matter what time?

MICHAEL
I'd have been there or I wouldn't.

LEN
I went past twice.

MICHAEL
Was it raining?

LEN
What?

MICHAEL
I'm trying to work out if it was me. There was a fellow there before me.

LEN
Nobody said I saw you.

MICHAEL
No.

LEN
You can't see much going past.

MICHAEL
No.

LEN
He hasn't got his glasses.

MICHAEL
He wouldn't. Lying down.

LEN
They asked me and I said no. Then Val said yes. Then they'd taken them away.

MICHAEL
You were right.

LEN
You haven't seen him without.

MICHAEL
I'm seeing him now.

LEN
I mean lately.

MICHAEL
I know what you mean.

LEN
There was a fortnight he didn't put them on once.

MICHAEL
What did you do with them?

LEN
What do you mean what did I do? They're by the bed.

MICHAEL
I could get them.

LEN
On the bus?

MICHAEL
If you wanted them for after.

LEN
What after? We're not opening him up again.

MICHAEL
No. Of course.

LEN
You can have them if that's what you're at.

MICHAEL
What would I do with his glasses?

LEN
Put them by your bed. See how you get on.

(MICHAEL puts his hand under the coffin handle.)

LEN
Not yet.

MICHAEL
I'm seeing how high.

LEN
It comes up.

MICHAEL
I know.

LEN
Don't pull on it. They hear that out there, they'll come in.

MICHAEL
When do you want them?

LEN
She hasn't finished her pear.

MICHAEL
Len.

LEN
What?

MICHAEL
Move over a bit.

(LEN moves. MICHAEL puts his hand to their father's hair.)

LEN
Which way are you going to do it?
