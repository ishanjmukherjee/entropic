(A municipal interview room. A brass handbell on the table. ELLIS sits. JO stands.)

ELLIS
You can sit.

JO
Is that part of it?

ELLIS
Sitting?

JO
Choosing when.

ELLIS
Not for this grade.

(JO sits.)

ELLIS
You found us all right.

JO
You still have to come through Rates.

ELLIS
You've been before.

JO
Everybody's been through Rates.

ELLIS
Not everybody says still.

JO
Is that the bell from the counter?

ELLIS
We'll come to the counter.

JO
Somebody's polished it.

ELLIS
Tuesday to Saturday. Eight fifteen, not eight thirty.

JO
What's the quarter hour for?

ELLIS
Getting ready to open.

JO
Paid?

ELLIS
It says from eight fifteen.

JO
On the door it says from eight thirty.

ELLIS
The door isn't applying.

JO
Good. It'd get it.

ELLIS
Why would it get it?

JO
Already knows everybody.

(ELLIS turns the bell a little.)

ELLIS
Say I'm a woman at the counter.

JO
All right.

ELLIS
My appointment was ten. It's twenty past. I've a child in a buggy.

JO
What's your name?

ELLIS
The child is crying.

JO
I can hear. What's your name?

ELLIS
I don't care for that.

JO
Which bit?

ELLIS
I can hear.

JO
Then don't tell me the child's crying.

ELLIS
It's a circumstance.

JO
It's not a name.

ELLIS
Mrs Vale.

JO
Which office are you here for, Mrs Vale?

ELLIS
I was told to wait.

JO
By who?

ELLIS
Whom.

JO
That's an unusual name.

ELLIS
Would you make a joke at the counter?

JO
Depends if it worked in here.

ELLIS
It didn't.

JO
Then no.

ELLIS
She won't leave. There's a queue behind her. You have that.

JO
The bell.

ELLIS
That's what it's there for.

JO
Who comes?

ELLIS
You're at the counter. What do you do?

JO
I want to know who comes.

ELLIS
Someone from the back.

JO
You?

ELLIS
Not necessarily.

JO
Because you've got a very particular woman in mind.

ELLIS
I've got four more people to see.

JO
Have any of them got my name?

ELLIS
It's a common name.

JO
You picked Vale.

ELLIS
I had to pick something.

JO
You didn't have to give her the buggy.

ELLIS
You're welcome to pick another circumstance.

JO
Same woman. No buggy. She's got both hands free. Does that change what you do?

ELLIS
It's what you'd do.

JO
She's not even at the counter yet. She's only coming through Rates.

ELLIS
There's no line of sight from the counter into Rates.

JO
There. That's the first useful thing.

ELLIS
Useful for the job?

JO
I can do Saturdays.

ELLIS
That wasn't in doubt.

JO
You haven't asked for a reference.

ELLIS
Would you like me to?

JO
I'd like the same questions the others get.

ELLIS
None of the others have asked to see me in particular.

JO
It was a box on the form.

ELLIS
It said particular requirements.

JO
And here you are.

ELLIS
Yes. Here I am. Shall we do the part where you take the bell?

(JO picks it up, holding the clapper.)

ELLIS
Not much use like that.

JO
It's heavier than I thought.

ELLIS
They're waiting.

JO
Let them.

ELLIS
Mrs Vale is leaning over now. She's trying to see who's in the back.

JO
You said twenty past.

ELLIS
Yes.

JO
At twenty past I was at school.

ELLIS
In this exercise you're employed here.

JO
Was it twenty past?

ELLIS
I can give you till the end of the minute.

JO
She'd have had to get me by half three.

ELLIS
You're holding it wrong.

JO
Show me.

ELLIS
There's nothing to show.

JO
Then how can I be holding it wrong?

(ELLIS puts a hand over JO's hand.)

ELLIS
You don't have to shake it. Just let that drop.

JO
Did you?

ELLIS
Your thumb. Move your thumb.

JO
You've got hold of it now.

(From outside, two knocks.)

ELLIS
We're not finished.

JO
They can't hear you through that.

(Neither lets go. The bell rings once.)
