Title: The Weight of Tidings

Characters:

- QUEEN ELARA (40s): A ruler holding the last thread of hope.
- LIAM (20s): A messenger, dusty, broken, and barely standing.

Setting:
The Queen’s private solar. Late evening. A single candle burns on a small table, illuminating maps that have been rolled and re-rolled too many times. The rest of the stage is in deep shadow.

(At rise, QUEEN ELARA sits rigidly in a wooden chair. She does not look at the maps. She stares at the door. A heavy knock, three distinct thuds, echoes. She closes her eyes for a moment, then opens them.)

QUEEN ELARA
Enter.

(The door creaks open. LIAM stands in the doorway, silhouetted. He is caked in mud and what looks like dried blood. He takes one staggering step forward, then another. He falls to one knee not out of protocol, but because his legs give way.)

QUEEN ELARA (A statement, not a question.)
You are not Sir Declan.

LIAM
(Head bowed, voice a dry rasp.)
No, Your Majesty. Sir Declan is dead. I am Liam, his squire.

(A long silence. The Queen’s face is a mask of stone, but her hands, resting in her lap, tighten until the knuckles are white.)

QUEEN ELARA
Then Rise, Squire Liam. You did not come all this way to kneel. A man does not outride death to deliver a courtesy.

(LIAM struggles to his feet. He sways but remains upright. He cannot meet her eyes.)

LIAM
I rode through the night. Through the marshes. I… I had to be the one. The last command he gave me. He said, “Tell her yourself. Let her see a face that loved him.”

QUEEN ELARA
(Her voice catches, just for a syllable.)
Loved…

(She stands. She moves to a small cabinet and pours dark liquid from a decanter into a pewter cup. She crosses the room and presses the cup into his shaking hands.)

QUEEN ELARA
Drink. Then speak.

(He drinks greedily, a trickle of wine escaping down his chin and cutting a path through the grime. He coughs, then looks at her with a terrifying directness, the look of a man who has walked through a nightmare and can no longer be polite about its existence.)

LIAM
We were broken at the Serpent’s Pass. Not defeated. Broken.

QUEEN ELARA
(The word is a fragile, brittle thing.)
Broken.

LIAM
They had the high ground, and a sorcerer we didn’t know about. The sky… it turned green. The rocks melted. Men became shadows burned into the stone. And still, the King held the line. He held it with his voice, and when his voice failed, with his sword, and when his sword shattered, with a standard torn from the dead.

(The Queen turns away, walking towards the maps she knows by heart. She places a hand flat on the parchment, her back to him. It’s a gesture of both control and immense, silent agony.)

QUEEN ELARA
Give me the last of it. No poetry. No mercy. Just the fact.

LIAM
(Straightens his spine. His voice becomes steady, the voice of a chronicler, hollow and precise.)
I was by him. The retreat was sounded, but he wouldn’t take it. A final charge, to buy time for the archers to escape. He took an arrow in the thigh. He pulled it out. Then a spear found him, here.

(He touches his own shoulder, just below the collarbone.)

LIAM
He went to his knees, but he was still swinging. Laughing. I saw it. He was laughing at the madness of it. I tried to get to him. Sir Declan held me back. That’s when the axe-man came. The King… his head was never bowed. Not once.

(A sharp, dry sob escapes the Queen. It’s the only one. She masters it instantly, strangling the sound in her throat. She doesn’t turn around.)

QUEEN ELARA
And his body?

LIAM
I… we could not recover it. The line broke completely. It was a rout. Sir Declan was cut down pulling me away. The last I saw, the King’s standard was still standing, driven into the ground beside him, but the enemy swarmed over it. Like ants.

(Liam’s voice finally breaks.)

LIAM
I have failed you in the telling, Your Majesty. I have brought you only chaos and a ghost. I did not save him. I did not save his body. I am a poor squire, and a poorer messenger. I should have died there.

(A silence so profound it seems to swallow all the oxygen in the room. Queen Elara turns. Her eyes are wet, but her face is a terrible, regal calm. She walks back to him, closer than is strictly proper, and looks up into his devastated young face.)

QUEEN ELARA
Do not ever say that. Do not wish for your death. You brought me the single thing that can keep me alive tonight.

LIAM
(Confused, hopeless.)
What? What did I bring?

QUEEN ELARA
A witness. You have let me see his final moments through your living eyes. You showed me that the sun did not set. That he, that my love, chose the manner of his own ending. Laughing. Defiant. You have not brought me chaos, Squire Liam. You have brought me certainties. He is not missing. He is not a prisoner enduring torment. He is dead. And in truth, I have known it for an hour. I felt it. A severing.

(She presses a hand to her own heart.)

QUEEN ELARA
Here. A cord, pulled and snapped. So you see, you are not a messenger. You are a witness. The only one he trusted enough to send. That gift… that is not small. That is what there is now. Now.

(She takes the empty cup from his hand, her fingers briefly touching his, a gesture of profound, unroyal intimacy. She places it on the table, next to the useless maps. She inhales deeply, and when she speaks again, her voice is iron.)

QUEEN ELARA
You will not speak of this to anyone. Inside these walls, the King holds the Serpent’s Pass. He holds it until my son, until the Prince, is crowned. Do you understand? The morning must believe him a living god for one more night, so the news of his mortal death does not shatter this kingdom before a new one can be forged.

LIAM
(Bows his head.)
I understand,
