SETTING: The Queen’s solar, high in the citadel. Late night. Rain lashes against a tall, arched window.

AT RISE: A massive oak table dominates the center of the room, covered by a sprawling parchment map weighed down by carved wooden soldiers. Candles burn low, casting long, trembling shadows. QUEEN ELEANOR (40s), wearing a simple dark wool dress with no crown, stands over the map. She picks up a wooden knight, considers it, and sets it down.

A frantic, heavy knocking sounds at the door. Before Eleanor can speak, the door bursts open. GARETH (20s) stumbles in. He is drenched, caked in mud from boot to shoulder, and breathless. He drops to one knee, his chest heaving.

ELEANOR
You didn't wait for the steward.

GARETH
Forgive me, Your Grace. I—I rode from the north. Three horses. I killed two of them on the road.

ELEANOR
(Calmly, without looking up from the map)
Then you had better have news worth two good horses, boy. Stand up. You’re bleeding on my floor.

Gareth rises slowly, swaying slightly. He clutches a leather satchel to his chest.

ELEANOR
Oakhaven?

GARETH
Gone, Your Grace.

Eleanor’s hand freezes over the map. She slowly raises her eyes to meet his.

ELEANOR
The pass is impregnable. Lord Vance had two thousand men and the high ground.

GARETH
There was fog, Your Grace. Thick as wool. We couldn't see ten paces. By the time the horns sounded, the Ironcoats were already inside the camp.

ELEANOR
That is impossible. The eastern ridge is sheer rock.

GARETH
They didn't come over the rock. They came through the ravine. Someone showed them the old smuggler’s path.

A heavy silence. The rain beats harder against the glass.

ELEANOR
Lord Vance?

GARETH
Dead. Cut down in his tent before he could buckle his armor. The vanguard broke within the hour. It wasn't a battle, Your Grace. It was a slaughter.

Eleanor steps away from the table. She walks toward the fire, keeping her back to him.

ELEANOR
And my son?

Gareth swallows hard. His hands tremble as he reaches into his tunic.

ELEANOR
(Sharply)
Speak, boy. Did Julian make it to the river?

GARETH
He... he wouldn't go, Your Grace.

Eleanor turns slowly.

GARETH
When the line collapsed, the men panicked. They were trampling each other in the mud. The Prince took the royal standard and planted it at the bridge. He rallied forty men—just forty—and held the crossing so the rest of us could swim the ford.

ELEANOR
(Quietly)
He was always too stubborn for his own good.

GARETH
He bought us an hour, Your Grace. He saved hundreds of lives.

Gareth steps forward, holding out a mud-stained silver signet ring. Eleanor stares at it. For a brief, terrible moment, the monarch vanishes, leaving only a mother. Her breath catches. She does not move.

GARETH
I saw him fall. An arrow took him in the throat. The Ironcoats didn't take him alive. I swear it. I pulled this from his hand before they overran the bridge.

Eleanor slowly extends her hand. Gareth places the ring in her palm. Her fingers curl tightly around it, knuckles turning white.

GARETH
I am sorry, Your Grace. I am so, so sorry.

Eleanor closes her eyes. One single, silent second passes. When she opens them, her face is carved from granite.

ELEANOR
How many reached the southern bank?

GARETH
(Startled by her tone)
Perhaps four hundred, Your Grace. Wounded. Mostly without weapons.

ELEANOR
And the Ironcoats?

GARETH
Three thousand strong. They’re already marching south. With the pass open, they’ll be at the capital gates in four days. Maybe three.

Eleanor walks back to the oak table. She looks down at the map. Gently, deliberately, she places Julian’s silver ring directly over the capital city.

ELEANOR
Go down to the kitchens. Tell the cook to give you hot broth and a clean tunic. Sleep for two hours.

GARETH
Your Grace?

ELEANOR
At dawn, you take a fresh horse and ride for the coast. Find Admiral Corvus. Tell him the Prince is dead, the northern army is ash, and I am calling every ship home. Tell him to burn the docks behind him and march his marines to the citadel.

GARETH
Yes, Your Grace.

ELEANOR
Go.

Gareth bows deeply, backing out of the room. The heavy oak door clicks shut behind him.

Eleanor stands alone in the flickering candlelight. Slowly, her composure cracks. She grips the edge of the table to keep from falling, bowing her head over the map as a single, silent sob shakes her shoulders.

LIGHTS FADE TO BLACK.
