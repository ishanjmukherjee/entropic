(A desk. Two chairs. A red folder lies in the centre of the desk. MARA sits behind it. DAVID sits opposite, still wearing his overcoat.)

MARA
You can take that off.

DAVID
I’m fine.

MARA
The heating’s been up since seven.

DAVID
I run cold.

MARA
Right. Well. (She looks down at a single sheet of paper, not the red folder.) It’s a fairly straightforward brief. Compliance, mostly. Oversight. Making sure the left hand knows what the right hand is—

DAVID
I know what the brief is.

MARA
Good. Because the listing was a bit—

DAVID
It was very clear.

MARA
Was it? I thought HR muddied it. They usually do.

DAVID
No. I understood it.

(A beat. MARA shifts the red folder two inches to her left.)

MARA
Says here you left Henderson’s in March.

DAVID
February.

MARA
Says March.

DAVID
The payroll ran till March. I stopped going in on the fourteenth.

MARA
Valentine’s Day.

DAVID
Tuesday.

MARA
And since then?

DAVID
Waiting.

MARA
For?

DAVID
The right room.

MARA
(A tight smile.)
Well. We have a very specific culture here, David. Very flat. Very open. If someone has a problem, they knock on the door. They don’t let it fester.

DAVID
Who had the desk before?

MARA
We’re expanding the team.

DAVID
By the window. The corner one.

MARA
That’s—we’re reorganising the floor.

DAVID
Martin’s desk.

MARA
Martin moved to Zurich.

DAVID
Did he.

MARA
Last month. Lovely flat, apparently. Overlooks the lake.

DAVID
He hates water.

MARA
Does he? You know Martin?

DAVID
We’ve met.

MARA
Small world.

DAVID
Very small.

(MARA pulls the red folder another inch toward herself. Her fingertips rest on the edge of it.)

MARA
Tell me about a time you had to manage a difficult stakeholder.

DAVID
I don’t keep copies of anything.

MARA
Sorry?

DAVID
At home. On my phone. I don’t like clutter.

MARA
That’s—I was asking about stakeholder management.

DAVID
I’m answering.

MARA
Right.

DAVID
People think they want a paper trail. They don’t. They want someone who remembers what was said and forgets who said it.

MARA
We’re a regulated firm, David. We live by paper trails.

DAVID
Of course.

MARA
Everything gets logged. Everything gets signed.

DAVID
Even the red ones?

(MARA’s hand stops moving. She looks at him.)

MARA
I think we might be looking for a slightly different temperament.

DAVID
You haven’t asked me about my references.

MARA
I don’t need to.

DAVID
Henderson’s will give me a glowing one. If you call the right number.

MARA
We have a standard process.

DAVID
Call the main switchboard and you’ll get Carol. Carol doesn’t know anything. Call the direct line on the second page and you’ll get Graham.

MARA
I don’t have a second page.

DAVID
(He nods toward the red folder.)
You do.

MARA
(She stands.)
Thank you for coming in. Truly. It’s been—

DAVID
I haven’t finished.

MARA
I have a ten-thirty.

DAVID
It’s ten past.

MARA
It’s a prep call.

DAVID
Sit down, Mara.

(She doesn’t sit. She looks at the door, then back at him.)

MARA
There isn’t a role for you here.

DAVID
There’s a very good role for me here.

MARA
The salary band is half what you were on. You’d be bored in a week.

DAVID
I don’t get bored.

MARA
You’d be reporting to someone ten years younger than you.

DAVID
I’m very easy to manage.

MARA
You’re not.

DAVID
You don’t know that.

MARA
Martin told me.

(Silence. MARA realises what she’s said. She sits back down, slowly.)

DAVID
Did he call from the lake?

MARA
What do you want?

DAVID
The desk by the window.

MARA
It’s gone.

DAVID
The salary band you mentioned. Plus ten.

MARA
I don’t have sign-off on that.

DAVID
You do if you use the other budget.

MARA
There isn’t another budget.

DAVID
Mara.

(She stares at him. Then she puts her palm flat on the red folder and slides it across the desk. It stops halfway between them.)

MARA
If I give you this, you’re in.

DAVID
I know.

MARA
Nine to six. Every day. You sit at the desk, you do the compliance, you sign the logs.

DAVID
Yes.

MARA
And you don’t—

DAVID
I don’t.

(Neither of them touches the folder. DAVID slowly unbuttons his overcoat, slips it off his shoulders, and drapes it neatly over the back of his chair. He sits back down, folding his hands on the desk, just inches from the red folder.)

DAVID
Where do we start?

(Blackout.)
