A throne room. Sparse. Stone walls. A single narrow window. Morning light falls across an empty throne.

QUEEN ELEANOR sits not on the throne but on its lowest step, examining a map spread across her knees. She is fifty, visibly tired, dressed in plain grey wool.

A knocking. Enter MESSENGER GILES, young, mud-caked, favouring his left leg. He halts ten paces from her, uncertain of protocol.

ELEANOR
(without looking up)
You're from the Darres Pass. I can smell the sulphur from the guns. Come closer, boy. I don't bite subjects who've walked through cannon fire to find me.

GILES
Your Majesty—

ELEANOR
(finally looking)
Your captain? Alive?

GILES
Yes, Majesty.

ELEANOR
Good. He's useful. Your name?

GILES
Giles. Keeper Bradford's son, from the mill village.

ELEANOR
(a ghost of recognition)
Bradford. He milled my father's grain. You have his forehead.

(She folds the map deliberately. Beat.)
The Pass held?

GILES
Majesty... I am to deliver this directly. Captain Wrain insisted. Before the others come. Before the council.

He produces a leather tube, extends it. She makes no move.

ELEANOR
Read it.

GILES
Majesty, it's sealed with your—

ELEANOR
Read it. My eyes are tired. Maps at dawn. Letters at dawn. I'm tired of dawn, Giles. Read me what your captain sealed in such hurry.

Giles breaks the seal with shaking hands, unrollss the letter. Silence.

GILES
(mechanical, rushing)
"The field was taken at the sixth hour. The Prince commanded the right flank against formed cavalry. By the eighth, the flank was turned. By the ninth, the Prince was unhorsed. By the tenth, we held the field but not... not the living."

Beat.

ELEANOR
The full sentence, boy.

GILES
"...not the living Prince."

Silence. Eleanor rises, moves to the window. Her hand finds the stone frame.

ELEANOR
(even, practical)
My son. Harold. Twenty-three years. Last month he wrote me about a horse. A courser, chestnut, vicious temper. He was breaking it to gentle. For a lady he admired, daughter of some merchant in the camp. He thought this romantic. I thought it expensive.

(Turning)
What else? There is always else. Captains pad the blow.

GILES
Majesty—

ELEANOR
Giles. I have buried a husband to fever, a daughter to childbirth, two sisters to this family's appetites for thrones. I have presided over harvests that killed villages, signed death warrants for cousins, watched a plague take my confessor while I lived to tally the souls. Your captain sent you early to spare me humiliation. I am beyond humiliation. What else?

GILES
(breaking)
They couldn't recover him, Majesty. The guns. After the guns. The cavalry withdrawal over ground they'd held. M-my brother was with the guns. He didn't know. Until after.

ELEANOR
Then Harold does not come home.

GILES
Captain Wrain mounted a party. Under white flags. Their officer received them. They traded—there was drinking, Majesty, I was there, I heard them—they traded stories of the exchange, how your son fell, how he called for his horse at the last, some nonsense about a dog he'd had as a—

ELEANOR
(sharp)
Brutus. Brindle. Killed by a tutor for soiling tapestries.

Giles
(lost)
They drank to him, Majesty. Their officer. Our captain. Enemies. They drank to him. Then they said... we cannot give him up. The cavalry. The ground. You understand. Already... there are things moving there. Natural things. Of summer. And they said, perhaps a token. But the guns, Majesty. There was nothing of him that—

He stops. Realizes.

ELEANOR

That I might bury. Yes. I see.

She moves from the window, not toward Giles, toward the throne. She does not mount it.

ELEANOR
Your captain thinks me alone here. He did not hear you knock. The council waits in the antechamber, Giles. Six men who have wanted this throne since my husband's death. Four who would marry me now, or their sons, to secure it. Three who have already written to the enemy, I am certain, offering terms. All of whom will enter when they hear, with condolences prepared, with strategies for succession, with documents I must be too grief-stricken to read.

(To the door, loud)
Enter then! I hear you breathing, my lords!

Scuffling. Silence. Then the door opens. LORD PROTECTOR VARNE enters first, others behind, faces arranged in appropriate sorrow.

VARNE
Your Majesty, we came as soon as—

ELEANOR
You came as soon as you heard a boy weeping. How efficient grief has made you, Varne.

She mounts the throne now. Deliberate. Slow. Arranges her grey wool.

ELEANOR
My lords. This crown has seen three hundred years of bastards, regicides, plagues, and miracles. It does not stop for my sorrow. It does not, I suspect, notice my sorrow.

(To Giles, who remains frozen)
Boy. Giles. You will dine in my chamber tonight. You will tell me what my son spoke of, in the hours before. What he ate. Whether he seemed afraid. The name of this merchant's daughter and whether I should find her and give her the goddamn horse.

VARNE
Majesty, surely this soldier is needed for—

ELEANOR
Needed by whom, Varne? My army is dead or victorious, I am not yet certain which concerns me more, and this boy walked through guns to bring me truth when you walked through pine面板 to bring me opportunity.

(To Giles, softer)
You will stay alive, Giles Bradford. For me. Now. Step behind the throne. Touch that wall. Feel how cold it is. Good. That cold is older than your grief, older than mine. Remember it.

Giles moves. The lords shuffle.

ELEANOR
My lords. I require two days. The kingdom requires two days. You will issue no statements. You will sign no documents. You will return to your estates or you will sit in my cells, where I assure you the walls are equally cold and the company more honest. Two days. Then we discuss which of you I marry, which I execute, and which I merely impoverish.

They hesitate.
