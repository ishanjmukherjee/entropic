A small, windowless conference room. A table, two chairs, a dying ficus. A water pitcher with no cups. MARGARET OKAFOR, 30s, sits upright, a leather folder squared perfectly in front of her. The door opens. GERALD PIKE, 50s, enters holding a coffee mug that reads WORLD'S OKAYEST MANAGER. He does not sit.

GERALD: You're the two o'clock.

MARGARET: (standing, hand extended) Margaret Okafor. Thank you so much for—

GERALD: (not shaking it) Sit, sit. We're informal here. (He sits. She sits.) So. Tell me about yourself.

MARGARET: Of course. I've spent eight years in logistics coordination, most recently at Halvorsen Freight, where I reduced shipping delays by thirty-one percent—

GERALD: Do you like birds?

(Pause.)

MARGARET: I'm sorry?

GERALD: Birds. Generally.

MARGARET: I... don't have strong feelings about birds.

GERALD: (writing something down) Interesting.

MARGARET: Is that relevant to the role?

GERALD: Everything's relevant to the role, Margaret. That's what the role is. (He sips coffee.) Greatest weakness.

MARGARET: (she's rehearsed this) I'd say I'm a perfectionist. I sometimes hold myself to standards that—

GERALD: No.

MARGARET: No?

GERALD: That's not your greatest weakness. That's a humblebrag in a cardigan. Try again.

(Long beat. MARGARET glances at the door.)

MARGARET: I... sometimes struggle to delegate.

GERALD: Warmer.

MARGARET: I get anxious when I'm not in control.

GERALD: Warmer.

MARGARET: (it slips out) I once cried in a Costco because they moved the paper towels.

GERALD: (slapping the table) There it is. Thank you. Now we're talking. (He writes furiously.)

MARGARET: I'd like that off the record.

GERALD: There's no record. The record is in my heart. (Beat.) Why do you want to work here?

MARGARET: Pemberton Industries has a strong reputation for—

GERALD: We don't.

MARGARET: —for growth, and I was impressed by—

GERALD: We had a lawsuit in March. A man fell into the shredder. Not all of him. But enough.

MARGARET: (closing her folder, slowly) Mr. Pike, I'm sorry, is this a stress interview? Because I've read about those, and I think I'd do better if I knew—

GERALD: Today's my last day.

(Silence.)

MARGARET: Oh.

GERALD: They told me Monday. Twenty-two years. They gave me a sheet cake that said "Best Wishes, Gerald" and the "Gerald" was spelled with a J. (He stares into his mug.) Then they said, "Oh, and would you mind doing one last interview? For your replacement."

MARGARET: (very quietly) For your...

GERALD: You're interviewing for my job, Margaret.

(MARGARET looks at the door. Then at GERALD. Then at the ficus, as if it might help.)

MARGARET: I didn't know that.

GERALD: Nobody knows anything here. That's the culture. It's on the website. (He leans back.) So I figured, why not ask the questions I actually care about. Do you like birds. Who are you when the paper towels move.

MARGARET: Why birds?

GERALD: There's a hawk. On the parking structure. Every morning for six years I've watched it from the third floor. Nobody else looks up. I wanted to know if the next person would look up.

(Pause. MARGARET considers this. She opens her folder again, takes out a pen.)

MARGARET: What's the hawk's name?

GERALD: (surprised) Doris.

MARGARET: (writing) Doris. (Beat.) Mr. Pike, can I ask you something?

GERALD: It's irregular.

MARGARET: You said a man fell into the shredder.

GERALD: Partially.

MARGARET: Was that before or after they put in the safety guard?

GERALD: Before. I'm the one who put in the safety guard. I filed the request four times.

MARGARET: And the shipping delays. Your department's numbers. Did they go down after the reorganization in 2019?

GERALD: (narrowing his eyes) How do you know about 2019?

MARGARET: I research. It's part of the anxiety. (Beat.) They went down because of you, didn't they?

GERALD: (slowly) ...Yes.

MARGARET: And they're firing you anyway.

GERALD: "Restructuring."

(MARGARET stands. Squares her folder. Tucks it under her arm.)

MARGARET: I don't think I want this job.

GERALD: You haven't been offered it.

MARGARET: I'm pre-declining. (She walks to the door, stops.) Halvorsen Freight is hiring a regional director. It's a terrible company. But nobody's fallen into anything.

GERALD: Are you... recruiting me?

MARGARET: I'm saying there's a pigeon on their roof. You'd hate it. (Beat.) But somebody should look up.

(She exits. GERALD sits alone. He looks at the ficus. He looks at his mug. Slowly, he picks up her abandoned pen and writes something down.)

GERALD: (to himself) Likes birds.

(Lights fade.)

END OF SCENE
