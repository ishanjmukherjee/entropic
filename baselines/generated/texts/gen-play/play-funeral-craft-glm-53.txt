(Later. The church hall has emptied. A folding table with caterer's urns, stacked cups, one plate of sandwiches. MARTIN, fifties, folders chairs. JON, late forties, at the far end, coat on, a folded umbrella in his hand.)

MARTIN
You're going.

JON
They've finished.

MARTIN
The vicar wanted a word.

JON
She can ring me.

MARTIN
She asked me for your number.

JON
Give her yours.

(MARTIN stacks another chair. Stops.)

MARTIN
Sandwiches?

JON
I ate.

MARTIN
There's a plate.

JON
I saw it.

(MARTIN crosses to the table. Moves the plate an inch toward JON. Leaves it there.)

JON
That urn's still hot.

MARTIN
Is it.

JON
You want me to have a sandwich and a cup of tea and then what.

MARTIN
Then nothing.

JON
Right.

(JON picks up the umbrella. Doesn't move.)

MARTIN
That's his.

JON
What?

MARTIN
The umbrella. His.

JON
It was by the door at the home. They'd have binned it.

MARTIN
You took it from the home.

JON
I took it from the door.

MARTIN
He had it forty years. He'd have wanted—

JON
You don't know what he wanted.

MARTIN
I was there, Jon.

JON
So was I. At the end.

MARTIN
Two weeks at the end.

JON
Long enough to watch him die, was it. Were you in the room? No. So.

(Silence. MARTIN rights a fallen chair. Sits on it. Backwards.)

MARTIN
The vicar said a nice thing. In there.

JON
I heard it.

MARTIN
About the allotment.

JON
I heard it, Martin.

MARTIN
He kept going down there. After. Even when he couldn't—

JON
Changing the subject.

MARTIN
I'm telling you something.

JON
About the allotment.

MARTIN
About Dad.

JON
You want to tell me about Dad. Now. In a church hall.

MARTIN
Better than the phone.

JON
You never rang.

MARTIN
You changed your number.

JON
I moved house.

MARTIN
You moved house and changed your number.

(JON puts the umbrella down on the table, on top of the sandwiches. Picks up a cup. Pours tea. Doesn't drink it. MARTIN watches the cup.)

MARTIN
There's milk.

JON
Black's fine.

MARTIN
It's not, for you. You take three sugars. You always—

JON
People change.

MARTIN
Do they.

JON
Yes.

MARTIN
Name one.

(JON drinks the tea. Grimaces. Puts the cup down.)

JON
The will.

MARTIN
What.

JON
When's the will.

MARTIN
You came for the will.

JON
I'm asking.

MARTIN
Thursday. The solicitor's. I sent you the—

JON
Nothing came.

MARTIN
To that address?

JON
I'd have given you a forwarding—

MARTIN
You'd have. Would you.

JON
This is why.

MARTIN
What's why.

JON
This. This exact thing.

MARTIN
I asked you a question in that letter. One question. You could have rung. Written two words.

JON
Which letter.

MARTIN
You know which.

JON
Fifteen years back.

MARTIN
Fourteen.

JON
Fourteen.

MARTIN
And you never answered.

JON
I'm not answering it now.

MARTIN
I didn't ask again.

JON
No.

MARTIN
I'm not asking now either.

JON
Good.

MARTIN
Good.

(He collects the teacup. Sets it near the urn. JON takes the umbrella off the plate. Holds it. Reaches into his coat and puts something on the table: a key, an old mortice key, on a loop of string.)

JON
The shed.

MARTIN
What about it.

JON
Allotment. The shed. He kept it on a nail by the— you know where.

MARTIN
I know where.

JON
I haven't been down there. Since. I couldn't open it without—

(He stops. Moves the key an inch toward MARTIN. The same gesture, the sandwich plate.)

JON
There might be stuff. In it. You'd want first look.

MARTIN
Why me.

JON
Because you're the one who goes down there.

MARTIN
Who says I go down there.

JON
The vicar said. In there. Nice thing number two. She got it from you.

(Pause. MARTIN's hand goes out to the key. Stops.)

MARTIN
The tomatoes he grew. That last summer. You remember?

JON
I wasn't—

MARTIN
You remember.

JON
I remember the smell.

MARTIN
He made me carry them home on the bus. All the way to yours, actually. He made me. Bunches of them, in a box on my knees, on the bus.

JON
To mine.

MARTIN
To yours.

(Silence. JON picks up the umbrella. MARTIN picks up the key. Neither looks at the other. JON pockets it—no. JON holds it out.)

JON
Keep it.

MARTIN
It's yours.

JON
It's not mine I'm offering.

(MARTIN takes it. JON takes the umbrella and goes. MARTIN stands quite still, the key in his fist. He sets it back on the table beside the plate. He picks up the plate of sandwiches and puts it on the chair where he'd been sitting, facing the urn. He sits across from it.)

(Blackout.)
