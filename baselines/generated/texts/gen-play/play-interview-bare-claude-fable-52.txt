THE POSITION

A small office. A desk, two chairs, a dying ficus. On the desk sits a cardboard box, half-packed. WALTER PRUITT, fifties, in a tie slightly too tight, sits behind the desk. DEV OKAFOR, twenties, in a suit slightly too new, sits across from him, holding a folder.

WALTER: So. Dev. Thank you for coming in.

DEV: Thank you for having me. I'm very excited about the opportunity.

WALTER: I'm sure you are. (He looks at the résumé.) Regional Logistics Coordinator. Tell me, what draws you to the role?

DEV: Well, I've always been passionate about supply chains.

WALTER: Nobody has always been passionate about supply chains.

DEV: (Beat.) I became passionate. Gradually.

WALTER: That's how it gets you. (He makes a note.) And what do you know about the position?

DEV: The listing said it oversees distribution for the tri-county area, manages a team of six...

WALTER: Seven. There's a Gary. Nobody lists Gary. Go on.

DEV: And it reports directly to the VP of Operations.

WALTER: Linda.

DEV: Linda. Yes.

WALTER: Have you met Linda?

DEV: She did my phone screen. She seemed lovely.

WALTER: (Quietly.) She does seem that.

He takes a framed photo from the desk, looks at it, and places it in the box. DEV watches.

DEV: Are you... moving offices?

WALTER: In a sense. (Brightly.) Next question! Describe a time you faced a challenge at work.

DEV: Sure. At my last job, we had a shipment of four hundred office chairs sent to the wrong...

WALTER: Mine is that eleven days ago, Linda asked me to "help with a transition." I said of course. I'm a team player, it's on my mug. (He holds up a mug that says TEAM PLAYER and puts it in the box.) I asked what was transitioning. She said, "The position."

DEV: (Slowly.) Which position?

WALTER: Regional Logistics Coordinator.

Silence. DEV looks at the box, then at the nameplate on the desk. He turns it around and reads it.

DEV: Oh no.

WALTER: Nineteen years.

DEV: Mr. Pruitt, I had no idea. They told me the role was vacant.

WALTER: It will be. At five. (He checks his watch.) She thought it would be "efficient" if I conducted the interviews. Since I know the job best.

DEV: That's monstrous.

WALTER: It's logistics.

DEV stands, gathering his folder.

DEV: I should go. I can't sit here and...

WALTER: Sit down.

DEV: I really don't think...

WALTER: You're the fourth one today. The first had never heard of a pallet. The second called me "bro." The third cried, which I thought was my job. (Beat.) You knew about the tri-county area. Sit down.

DEV sits.

WALTER: Where do you see yourself in five years?

DEV: (Carefully.) Honestly? Here, I'd hoped. Doing this well.

WALTER: That's what I said. In this chair. To a man named Horace, who had a box. (He laughs, and it goes on a little too long.) I thought he was moving offices.

DEV: Mr. Pruitt, can I ask you something?

WALTER: That's not how this works.

DEV: Where do you see yourself in five years?

WALTER opens his mouth. Nothing comes out. He picks up the ficus, considers the box, and sets it back down.

WALTER: Nobody's asked me that since Horace.

DEV: What did you say then?

WALTER: I said, "Here." (Beat.) I wanted to build boats, once. Small ones. I have a garage full of cedar I've been saving for a slow quarter.

DEV: Has there been a slow quarter?

WALTER: Not in nineteen years.

DEV: There's one starting at five.

WALTER looks at him for a long moment, then picks up his pen and writes on the résumé.

DEV: What are you writing?

WALTER: "Asks good questions. Do not hire."

DEV: What? Why?

WALTER: Because you're twenty-six and you became passionate gradually. (He slides the résumé back across the desk.) Go and find out what your cedar is. I'll tell Linda you called me "bro."

DEV takes the résumé, stands, and offers his hand. WALTER shakes it.

DEV: Who gets the job?

WALTER: (Putting the ficus in the box.) Gary. It's time somebody listed him.

DEV exits. WALTER lifts the box, looks around the office, and turns the nameplate face down. Lights fade.

END OF SCENE
