(A church hall after the service. Trestle table, a tea urn, plates of sandwiches under cling film. PAUL, in a good coat, is peeling the cling film back. DENNIS enters carrying a stack of folding chairs. He sets them down. On the table, by the urn, a single Yale key on a ring with a plastic fob.)

DENNIS
You're still here.

PAUL
Egg or ham?

DENNIS
They're all egg. Ham went first.

PAUL
Course it did.

(He takes one. Doesn't eat it.)

DENNIS
Train's at six.

PAUL
Twenty past.

DENNIS
Twenty past. Right.

(DENNIS unfolds a chair, then folds it again.)

PAUL
Good turnout.

DENNIS
Bowls club. They'd come for anyone.

PAUL
Mrs Ellery cried.

DENNIS
Mrs Ellery cries at the weather.

PAUL
She said he talked about me.

DENNIS
Did she.

PAUL
That's what she said.

DENNIS
She's eighty-four.

PAUL
So she's wrong.

DENNIS
I didn't say she was wrong.

(Pause. DENNIS picks up the key, looks at it, puts it down nearer PAUL.)

DENNIS
Boiler's playing up.

PAUL
At the house?

DENNIS
Needs bleeding. The radiators. Upstairs ones.

PAUL
You know how to bleed a radiator.

DENNIS
I'm in Leeds Thursday.

PAUL
So get a man in.

DENNIS
A man.

PAUL
A plumber, Den.

DENNIS
Someone ought to be in the house. That's all.

(PAUL looks at the key. Doesn't touch it.)

PAUL
Was he in pain?

DENNIS
They had him on the stuff.

PAUL
That's not what I asked.

DENNIS
He did the crossword. Right up till Tuesday.

PAUL
The cryptic?

DENNIS
The quick one.

PAUL
He hated the quick one.

DENNIS
Yeah, well.

(PAUL finally eats the sandwich. DENNIS watches him.)

DENNIS
You'll want some of his things.

PAUL
What things.

DENNIS
I don't know. The books.

PAUL
I've got books.

DENNIS
The clock, then.

PAUL
Mum's clock.

DENNIS
It was in his room.

PAUL
It was always in his room.

DENNIS
So you'd know where it is.

(Beat.)

PAUL
Did he say anything?

DENNIS
About what?

PAUL
Anything. At the end.

DENNIS
He asked for the window open.

PAUL
And you opened it.

DENNIS
It was February.

PAUL
So you didn't.

DENNIS
I opened it a bit.

(PAUL laughs, once, despite himself. DENNIS nearly does.)

DENNIS
You could stay tonight. In your room. Bed's made up.

PAUL
Since when?

DENNIS
Since Monday.

PAUL
You made it up on Monday.

DENNIS
Somebody had to.

PAUL
For me.

DENNIS
For whoever.

(Pause.)

PAUL
Mrs Ellery said he kept my postcards. On the fridge.

DENNIS
The magnets fell off. Years back.

PAUL
So they're not on the fridge.

DENNIS
I didn't say they weren't anywhere.

PAUL
Where are they?

DENNIS
Come and look.

(Silence. PAUL turns to the urn, tries the tap. Nothing.)

PAUL
It's empty.

DENNIS
It's off. You have to —

(He reaches past PAUL and flicks a switch at the back. They stand close. Neither moves away immediately.)

PAUL
Twenty past six.

DENNIS
There's one at nine.

PAUL
Is there.

DENNIS
And in the morning.

PAUL
You've checked.

DENNIS
I was looking anyway.

PAUL
For Leeds.

DENNIS
For Leeds.

(PAUL takes his coat off the back of a chair, lays it over his arm.)

PAUL
Did he ask for me, Den?

(DENNIS picks up the stack of chairs.)

DENNIS
These go back in the cupboard.

PAUL
Dennis.

DENNIS
Before the Brownies at seven. They're very particular.

(He carries the chairs off. PAUL is alone. He looks at the key for a long moment. He puts his coat on. He picks up the key, weighs it in his palm, and sets it back down exactly where it was.

DENNIS returns, sees the key on the table. Sees PAUL in his coat.)

DENNIS
Right, then.

PAUL
Right.

(PAUL goes to the door. Stops.)

PAUL
Which room's the clock in?

DENNIS
His.

PAUL
I know. I mean — where in it.

(DENNIS picks up the key. Holds it out. PAUL doesn't come back for it. DENNIS doesn't lower his arm.

The urn begins, faintly, to tick as it heats.

Blackout.)
