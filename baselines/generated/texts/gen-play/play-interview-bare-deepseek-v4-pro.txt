Setting: A stark, white interview room. A single metal table. Two chairs. A large, frosted glass window occupies the back wall.

Characters:
- MR. HARROW (50s), a man carved from granite and quiet disdain. Wears an immaculate suit.
- LEO (20s), acutely intelligent, a human oscillating between charm and exposed nerve. Wears a suit that’s slightly too big, as if borrowed or inherited.

(At rise: MR. HARROW sits motionless, a tablet flat on the table before him. LEO is mid-answer, leaning forward with a carefully calibrated smile.)

LEO: …which resulted in a seventeen percent increase in cross-departmental synergy. My professor called it a radical re-contextualization of the sales funnel. But I prefer to think of it as simply… listening to the data. The data doesn’t scream, Mr. Harrow. It whispers. You just have to be quiet enough to hear it.

(A pause. MR. HARROW does not react. He doesn’t even blink.)

MR. HARROW: A whisper.

LEO: Exactly.

MR. HARROW: And what, pray tell, does your whispered data say about this chair?

(He taps the chair next to him. LEO’s smile flickers, then holds.)

LEO: The chair? It’s a chair.

MR. HARROW: Is it? Or is it a collection of atoms, vibrating in a quantum flux, held in the temporary shape of a chair by a shared social delusion we mistake for reality?

(LEO stares. He lets out a single, involuntary laugh of pure adrenaline.)

LEO: Okay. Great. A stress test. I love this. (He shifts gear, his voice becoming less polished, more engaged.) You want to see if I can deconstruct a premise under pressure. Alright. That chair is a tool. Its data whispers “sit.” It also whispers “power,” because you’re on one side of the table and I’m on the other. The table, by the way, is whispering “moat.”

MR. HARROW: The table is just a table. The chair is just a chair. That’s your first mistake. You project. You hear whispers where there is only silence. A dangerous quality in risk assessment.

LEO: My apologies. I was trying to be engaging. Let me be direct. My qualifications for this compliance role are—

MR. HARROW: Let’s discuss your qualifications. Specifically, the two-year gap. Here. Between “Freelance Digital Strategy” and your internship at Brightbox.

(Harrow points at the tablet with one manicured finger. The air leaves LEO’s body. He doesn’t look at the tablet.)

LEO: I was caring for a family member.

MR. HARROW: (A slow, deliberate blink) You were caring for a family member.

LEO: My mother.

MR. HARROW: Your mother. Yes. We do thorough vetting here, Mr. Finch. Your mother passed away eleven months into that two-year gap. The death certificate is a matter of public record.

(Silence. LEO’s face is a mask, frozen.)

LEO: It took seven months to arrange the funeral. (A beat). There were complications with the estate. They whisper, too, the dead. You’d be surprised. You have to be very quiet to hear them.

MR. HARROW: What did you really do for that year, after she died?

LEO: (Voice suddenly raw, the mask slipping) Nothing. I did nothing. I sat in a chair. Not that chair. A worse one. A chair that was just a chair. And I was just a collection of atoms. No social delusion. No shared reality. Just… a man in a room. Is that what this is? Is that the correct answer? Are we done with this part?

MR. HARROW: (Leans forward, his voice dropping to a conspiratorial murmur) No. We’re just beginning. You see, Mr. Finch, you’re not here to fill a compliance role.

LEO: I’m not?

MR. HARROW: No. There’s an anomaly. A pattern in your data. Six months ago, you closed your bank account. You let your phone contract expire. You sublet your apartment. You effectively vanished from the digital record, only to re-emerge two weeks ago, applying for this job with a resume that is a masterpiece of plausible invention. My question is… who are you pretending to be now? And for whom? Because the man who walked into this room no longer exists on any system. You are a dead man whispering.

(LEO goes completely still. His eyes, previously nervous, now hold a terrifying clarity. He looks at Harrow as if seeing him for the first time.)

LEO: (Quietly, without inflection) A dead man whispering. That’s good.

(He slowly, carefully removes his suit jacket. He folds it once, twice, then places it gently on the table, as if preparing for a messy task.)

LEO: You found me. I knew someone would eventually. I’d hoped for more time, but… here we are.

(MR. HARROW’s granite composure develops a hairline crack. He pulls back almost imperceptibly.)

MR. HARROW: Security is thirty seconds away.

LEO: Security. (A small, sad smile). Mr. Harrow, the data isn’t whispering anymore. It’s screaming. And you don’t even know what room you’ve walked into.

(LEO reaches into his pocket. Harrow flinches. LEO slowly pulls out a small, smooth stone. He places it in the precise center of the table.)

LEO: That’s not a stone. That’s a failure. Yours. The interview is over.

(Lights snap to black. The sound of a chair scraping violently against the floor, then silence.)
