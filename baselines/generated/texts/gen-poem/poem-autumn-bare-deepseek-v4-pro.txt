The trees let fall their gilded stores,
A carpet spread on earthen floors,
The scent of woodsmoke, sharp and thin,
As cooler days at last begin.

The sun, a pale and weaker gold,
Tells tales of summer that are old,
And shadows stretch a longer shape
Across the hill and through the grape.

A rustle stirs the brittle corn,
A single, clear, and lonely horn
From some far field where geese converse,
Preparing to rehearse the universe.

Apples drop with sodden thud,
Fermenting in the grass and mud,
A sweet decay, a heady wine,
A promise of the closing vine.

So let the branches turn to bone,
The world prepare its hushed, gray tone,
For in this withering, deep and vast,
Is beauty that was built to last.
