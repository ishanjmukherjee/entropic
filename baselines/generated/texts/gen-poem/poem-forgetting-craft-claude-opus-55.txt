She winds his watch at five and sets it wrong.
Down the hill, a laker eases in with ore;
the bridge at Ashtabula rings its bell too long.

She lays out forks for three. She salts the strong
black coffee he took sweet. A knock. The door
is someone's son. She winds the watch. It's wrong.

He shows her how the crown pulls out, a prong
to turn eleven clicks. She counts to four.
The bridge at Ashtabula rings its bell too long.

She asks if his father's working still among
the night crew at the Huletts. "Till before
dark," he says. She winds the watch and sets it wrong.

She bangs the dog's dish with a spoon, a gong
for supper, though there's been no dog since '94.
The bridge at Ashtabula rings its bell too long.

He goes. She drops the watch in with the tongs
to soak, and dries her hands, and locks the door.
Tomorrow she'll wind it at five and set it wrong.
The bridge at Ashtabula rings its bell too long.
