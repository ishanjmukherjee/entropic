The alley behind Krauser's Mill, 3 a.m.:
a possum carries off a blackened pear.
I stand at the loading dock, drink cold coffee,
listen to the belt idling inside, no one
feeding it cardboard. A man has hanged himself
in the room I rent. I learned this from the landlady,
not the paper. She spoke through her screen door,
held a parakeet against her chest. I walk now
past the one dumpster that still smolders,
to the creek where teenagers shove shopping carts.
The water carries a foam I can't identify.
I have not slept in four days. My fingers
hold the rail for proof of weight. A train
passes, and the rail begins to warm.
