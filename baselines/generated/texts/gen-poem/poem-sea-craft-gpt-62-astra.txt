At six in Whitby the sea comes up the Esk
and lifts the cobles off the mud.
A man on the fish quay lowers a bucket
until its lip tips under.
He hauls it up against the wall;
the handle squeals in its two holes.

“I've got your teeth,” a woman calls.
She holds up a mustard jar.
He puts the bucket down to take it.

Across the deck he throws the water.
Scales run to the scupper,
then a strip of skin too wide to pass.
He takes it between finger and thumb
and slaps it overboard.

The tide has covered the last iron rung.
A gull steps off the ferry ramp
onto its own two yellow feet in the water.
