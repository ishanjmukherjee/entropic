The sea remembers what the sky forgets,
each wave a breath the ocean takes in sleep.
It carries salt and secrets in its depths,
and cradles all the ships it means to keep.

By morning light it shimmers, green and gold,
a restless mirror for the wandering sun;
by night it turns to ink, both deep and cold,
its darkness holding stars that have come undone.

I walk the shore and listen to its speech—
a language older than the first man's word,
of tides that rise and fall beyond our reach,
and storms that howl at everything they've stirred.

The sea gives nothing back but what itborrowed:

I'll patch that line—

The sea gives nothing back but what it took,
yet still we come, drawn to its wide blue door,
to watch the water write upon the rock
and read the waves forever, evermore.
