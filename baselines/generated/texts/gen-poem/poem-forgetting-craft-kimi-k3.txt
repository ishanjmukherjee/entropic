The keys are missing from the kitchen hook at dawn,
the bus goes by, the umbrella stays behind,
she checks the hall, the drawer, the washing gone.

A man asks twice for tickets on the train,
his paper ticket folded out of mind.
The keys are missing. From the kitchen hook, a line

of dust marks where the metal used to hang.
At eight the light falls level through the blind;
she checks the hall. The drawer, the washing line

a shed skin on the balcony, is blank.
The neighbor calls his dog a different name.
The keys are missing from the kitchen hook, at dawn,

but frost still keeps the shape of where they lay.
Inside his coat, the ticket sings for gain;
she checks the hall, the drawer. The washing gone

returns as water silvering the drain.
The keys are missing from the kitchen. Hook at dawn.
She checks the hall. The drawer. The washing. Gone.
