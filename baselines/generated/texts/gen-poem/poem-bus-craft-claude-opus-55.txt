The 61C kneels at Forbes and Murray
at twenty to two, and a woman in green scrubs
climbs on with a ten-pound sack of onions,
pays in quarters, sets the sack in the seat by the window
and keeps one hand on it.
The driver has his radio low,
a ballgame from somewhere it is still evening,
the crowd coming and going like weather.
Two boys in the back pass a single earbud
between them, and the heater ticks
under the seats. At every stop the doors fold open
on nobody. A man in a blue parka
pulls the cord at each block, then sits back
and tells the window, Not this one.
Past the cemetery fence, past the dark Giant Eagle,
the woman works one onion loose through the mesh,
splits the skin with her thumbnail,
and bites into it like an apple.
Nobody looks. The boys trade the earbud.
Someone on the radio steals second.
Her eyes run and she keeps chewing,
the sack slumped against the glass,
and on the ribbed floor a curl of brown skin
lifts a little each time the doors open
and settles when they close.
