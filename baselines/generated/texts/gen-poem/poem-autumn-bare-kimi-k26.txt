The field shivers gold, then bronze, then rust.
Wind writes a thin script through the osier,
and the orchard reduces its light
to these last globes, heavy and cider-sweet.

Leaves unfasten without grief,
tesselating the path, the pond, the roof—
every vessel filling with the same
quiet erasure.

I too am learning
to loosen from the stem,
to listen for the particular
click of frost threading
the morning's black seams.

Evening arrives sooner,
carrying its small cargo of stars.
Smoke braids above the valley
where someone, still burning,
has not yet learned
the whole of surrender.

The geese pass, aligning
their cold music south.
I stand in the aperture
of the dying year,
my pockets full of seed,
my mouth full of wind,
not knowing if I am
the one who remains
or the one who departs.
