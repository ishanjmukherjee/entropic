Maps of quiet labor, etched in skin,
With calloused palms and knuckles scarred by time.
They hold the weight of all that he has been,
The steady grit, the grease, the stubborn grime.

They’ve swung the hammer, turned the rusted wrench,
And built the shelter where we safely slept.
They’ve worked until the daylight had to quench,
And guarded every promise that he kept.

Yet rough as bark, they knew a gentler art—
To steady wobbly wheels along the street,
To mend a broken toy, a heavy heart,
And guide my small and stumbling, restless feet.

Now silver veins trace rivers on the back,
A little slower, weathered by the years,
Yet still they hold the strength I sometimes lack,
And still they know the way to dry my tears.
