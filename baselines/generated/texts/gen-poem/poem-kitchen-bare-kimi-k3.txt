The room remembers every spaciousness
of flour settling on the wooden boards,
how morning light steepens in the kettle’s throat
and the cold linoleum answers bare feet
with a shock that sharpens into routine.

Olive oil shifts in its glass,
green-gold, thick with afternoon,
while the refrigerator hums its electric lullaby
to the eggs, the last bruised pear,
the jar of something preserved in September
that waits like a secret behind the milk.

Here, the knife separates green from stalk,
onion from paper skin—
small violences that serve as sacraments,
the garlic crushed until it releases
its ghost into the heated pan
where it dances, browning,
becoming something the body craves
before the mind has named it hunger.

The oven breathes like a sleeping animal,
warming the palms of everyone
who passes through this architecture of steam
and clatter, where conversations stick
like honey to the sides of glasses
and the clock above the doorway
licks its lips and purrs.

Nothing here is theoretical.
Salt is salt, fire is hot, water boils
when it is ready, not when it is watched.
Bread rises because it is alive
and has smelled the yeast of its own potential.
Even the burnt edges have a flavor
that teaches the tongue acceptance.

And at midnight, when the dishwasher
settles into its final rhythm
and the countertops gleam like moonlit fields,
the room stands open, a yawning promise—
that tomorrow someone will come here
with empty hands and a willing heart,
and leave carrying warmth
in the bowl of their body.
