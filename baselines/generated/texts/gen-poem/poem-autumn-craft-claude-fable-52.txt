Four-forty at Canal Park, and the lift bridge
is going up for the Paul R. Tregurtha,
a thousand feet of ore boat
sliding between the piers, slow as a wall.

My father's thermos sits on the bench beside me,
green Stanley, the dent in its shoulder
from the day it fell off the roof of the Buick.
I unscrew the cup. The coffee is his recipe,
which is to say too much of it, and boiled.

Up the hill the maples along Skyline have let go
all at once, the way a crowd leaves a hockey game,
and the gutters on Superior Street run red and yellow
down to the storm drains.

A boy on the pier counts the deckhands out loud.
He gets to four. One of them waves a glove.
The boat answers the bridge: one long, two short.

Last week I found a wasp asleep inside the cup,
folded into the thread of the lid,
and carried it out to the porch rail on a spoon.

Now the gulls lift off the breakwater light.
The bridge comes down. Cars start their engines.
The steam goes sideways off the cup
toward Wisconsin, and a leaf, an ash leaf,
has stuck itself to the wet ring on the slat.
