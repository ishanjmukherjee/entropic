At six-fifteen the streetlights on Marine Drive
click off, one by one, toward the river.
You set the steel thermos on the fender
and unscrew the cap. Steam rises
into the fog coming off the pilings in Astoria.
I hand you the foil-wrapped egg.
Your knuckles are split from the salt,
the creases packed with grease
that pumice soap never takes out.
A gull drops a clam onto the gravel
ten feet from your boots, misses the crack,
and hauls it up again.
You have a scar shaped like a comma
behind your left ear from a nail gun in Eugene,
and you still turn your head that way
whenever a horn blows on the water.
You pour the first cup and hold it out to me
before you drink from the mouth.
The truck idles, rattling the mirror
on the passenger side.
When you climb into the cab,
you reach across the bench
and pull the choke half an inch,
leaving a thumbprint of coffee
on the chrome.
