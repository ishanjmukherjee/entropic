5:40, the Greyhound depot on West Michigan Street, Duluth.
The vending machine keeps my dollar and gives nothing back.
You unscrew the thermos your father carried to the ore docks,
green Stanley, dented at the shoulder,
and pour into the cap because there is one cap.
We pass it. The coffee tastes of the soup
it held on Sunday.
Behind the glass the clerk reads out the times
for Hinckley, Minneapolis, to a room of four.
Yours is the 6:05. I am not going anywhere;
I drove you here and will drive back
past the lift bridge with the heat on your side still up.
You say, the furnace filter is the sixteen by twenty.
You say, don't let Carol take the ladder.
I say I have never once known where we keep the ladder.
A man asleep across three chairs
laughs at something inside the sleep.
Your thumb finds the split seam in my glove
and works it wider.
When the driver calls it you stand,
put the cap in my hand, still wet,
and take the thermos with you.
Through the door the bus lights come on,
one row, then the next.
On the bench, a ring where the cap sat,
drying from the outside in.
