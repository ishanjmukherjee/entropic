At five a.m. the fog erases Barrow Road,
a milk crate full of empties on the curb.
I have to check the stove before I know

I checked it twice already. A truck’s low load
grinds past the window, diesel and adverb.
At five a.m. the fog erases Barrow Road

and the cat lifts her head as if she’s been told
someone is coming. Nobody comes. The verb
I have to check the stove before I know

turns out to be the wrong verb. There’s a fold
of skin above the knuckle where a burn once served
as a small reminder. At five a.m., Barrow Road

holds its breath, and in that pause the mold-
flecked ceiling listens with me for the unheard
click I have to check the stove for. Before I know

my legs are carrying me, I’m at the stove. The cold
dial registers off. The cat blinks at the curb
where five a.m. eats the end of Barrow Road.
I check the stove again before I know.
