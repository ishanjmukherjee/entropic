At four on Boston Common a man folds a chessboard
with the pieces still on it, and has to open it again.
A rook rolls under the bench.
His opponent stops it with her shoe.

The elms have dropped enough leaves
to hide the squares somebody chalked on the path.
A boy hops anyway, both feet down
where he remembers eight.

“My hen laid that one in the laundry.”
The woman has an egg in her coat pocket.
She takes it out to reach the bag for the pieces
and the man holds out his hand.

He sits with the egg while she counts pawns.
A bus kneels on Tremont Street.
Wind pushes a row of elm leaves
against the boy’s bare ankle.

The woman turns the bag inside out.
In the crease at the bottom, a black pawn,
a shirt button threaded with blue.
