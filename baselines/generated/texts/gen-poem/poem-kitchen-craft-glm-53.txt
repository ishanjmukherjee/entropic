The kettle ticks as it cools,
past six, the window gone dark
over Fourth Street where the bus
releases one passenger, then none.

My mother's knife, the small one
with the cracked wooden handle,
still cuts the onion on the board
she bought before I was born.
The onion comes apart in rings.
One lands on the floor and I leave it.

The fridge hums, stops, hums.
A magnet holds the dentist's card
from an appointment four years old.
On the stove, oil shifts
and settles in the pan
like something deciding.

I rinse the rice until the water
runs off clear, and the radio man
says rain for Thursday, then
reads the scores. The cat
pushes her head into my shin.
Her bowl is already empty—
she has learned to ask early.

Steam climbs the window
and the streetlight doubles in it.
I salt the water twice,
happy, and put the lid on.
