My father's hands could tell a peach was ripe
without a thumbprint on the skin.
They tried each buckle on my skates,
then set me wobbling on the pond.
I thought the trick was holding on.
He knew the trick was when to stop.

This winter, at his kitchen table,
I thread a needle while he waits.
He takes it back and mends his cuff,
each stitch a little longer now.
I leave my hands beside my cup
until he asks me for the scissors.
