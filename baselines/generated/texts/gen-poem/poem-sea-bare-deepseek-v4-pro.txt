The sea is not a single thing, but many—
A cradle rocking ancient, sunken stone,
A begging bowl receiving every penny
Of rain, and spending nothing of its own.

It is a wrinkled map of all the weather,
A muscle flexing underneath the moon,
A book the wind can’t read, that nonetheless will gather
A single, shattered alphabet of foam.

By day, a beaten shield of tin and silver,
By night, a restless, black-and-bellied beast
That breathes against the continents and shivers
The shoreline, grain by grain, into the east.

It mumbles in the hollow of a conch shell,
It climbs the air and sharpens into wings,
It is the last, unfenced and common gospel
Of which no human mouth can truly sing.

It does not know its depth, it does not measure
The ribs of ships it keeps, the swallowed prayers.
It only knows this endless, pressing pressure—
To rise and fall,
and rise and fall,
and wear.
