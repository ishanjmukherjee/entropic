The clock hands crawl; the ceiling holds my stare,
And every sheep I count has slipped the fold.
The pillow turns, but finds no comfort there,
One side too warm, the other side too cold.

The house exhales its settling creaks and sighs,
A faucet marks the seconds, drop by drop,
While thoughts I buried under daylight rise
And circle like a wheel that will not stop.

I tally debts, old words I can't unsay,
Tomorrow's labors looming, list by list,
Each hour I lose subtracted from the day
That waits beyond the window's graying mist.

Then, just as dawn spills pale across the deep,
The birds begin, and I at last could sleep.
