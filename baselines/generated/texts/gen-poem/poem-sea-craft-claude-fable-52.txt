Ten to six at Cromer, the tide halfway down the shingle.
A tractor backs the boat off its trailer into the surf
and the man at the wheel does not look behind him.

On the gangway someone has left a crab pot
with its door tied open, blue twine, a reef knot,
and inside it a child's sandal.

The water is the colour of the tractor's exhaust.
It comes in under the pier and knocks at the piles,
one, then a gap, then two together.

A woman in a hi-vis coat counts floats into a fish box.
She says to nobody, forty-one, and then,
he never did like eggs.

The boat clears the third wave and turns north-east
for the pots off Overstrand.
Its engine can still be heard after the hull has gone into the grey.

The tractor stands in water to its axles.
Gulls walk up the trailer as if it were a street.

In the pot, the sandal's buckle
has started to take the sun,
a square of it, the size of a stamp.
