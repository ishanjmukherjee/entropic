Thumbs like mismatched doorknobs,
the left one from a house north of Medicine Hat
where the screen door never latched.
On the back of that hand, a pale hook of scar
from a barbed-wire fence he held for a brother.
The right thumbnail is permanently ridged
from a hammer that glanced off a spike in December.
One knuckle won’t straighten.

At four o’clock he comes in
carrying a basin of early tomatoes
and sets it on the kitchen floor.
His fingers sort them blind, grading by weight.
A wasp on the windowsill grooms its front legs
and the refrigerator cycles on.
He tells my mother to leave the skins on for sauce.

When he talks his hands stall
in the air above the table
as if holding a tiller steady in a crosswind
that never reaches the kitchen.
Later, tying a blood knot in new tippet,
he borrows light from the window over the sink
and the line goes quiet against his palm.
The pulse in the hollow of his wrist
moves the feather on the hook.
