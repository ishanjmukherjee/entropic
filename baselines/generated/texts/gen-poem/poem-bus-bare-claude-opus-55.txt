The last bus hums along the avenue,
a lit aquarium drifting through the dark,
its windows framing strangers, two by two,
each seat a small and temporary ark.

A nurse in scrubs is dozing by the door,
her badge still clipped, her shoes untied for ease.
A boy with headphones taps against the floor
a rhythm only he and midnight please.

The driver knows each pothole by its name,
each corner where the streetlight flickers out.
He's carried lovers, drunks, the old, the lame,
and never asked what any were about.

The doors exhale. A woman steps outside
into the rain with groceries in her arms.
The bus moves on, the city open wide,
its sleeping blocks, its distant car alarms.

And no one speaks, and no one needs to speak.
The engine sings its low and steady hymn.
We ride together, tired and mild and meek,
toward homes we'll reach when all the stars go dim.

At the last stop, the driver dims the light,
the empty seats still holding warmth like breath.
The bus, a lantern swinging through the night,
returns, as all things must, from little deaths.
