The clock strikes three, then four, then five—still wake,
While darkness presses heavy on my eyes.
I count the breaths I cannot seem to take,
And listen to a silence that denies
The mercy of the sleep my body craves.
Each thought, unbidden, turns and turns again,
Like restless waves that never find their graves
But break, and break, against the windowpane.
What shadowed thing keeps vigil with my mind?
What hidden thread, pulled taut, refuses snap?
I am abandoned by the peace I pine,
A stranger in my own familiar trap.
Yet morning will arrive, indifferent, pale—
And I, hollow-eyed, will rise, and live, and fail.
