My father's hands were maps of weathered land,
with rivers running blue beneath the skin,
a scar across one knuckle, pale and thin,
from some old work I'd never understand.

They smelled of sawdust, coffee, motor oil.
They fixed the bike chain, steadied the first ride,
then let go without telling me, and I'd
gone half a block before I felt the toil

of balance was my own. They thumped ripe melons,
shuffled cards, turned pages, tied my tie,
and once, just once, I saw them wipe his eye.

Now mine have grown the same rough constellations.
I catch them resting, folded, on my knee,
and find him there, still holding on to me.
