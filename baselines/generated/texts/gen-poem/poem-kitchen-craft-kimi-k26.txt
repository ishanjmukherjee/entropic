Provincetown, 6 a.m.

The coffeemaker gargles its last water.
I stand at the window watching a man
walk two leashed terriers past the house.
He does not look up.

The linoleum has a crack near the stove
where a cast-iron trivet fell last winter.
I leave it. My neighbor's rooster insists
the day is elsewhere.

On the counter, a cantaloupe
waits for the knife. I quarter it,
scoop seeds into the disposal,
remember the hardware store closed
last month for good, its nails
now sold to strangers.

The oldest drawer sticks.
Inside: a rubber band around napkins,
a P-touch label maker, dead batteries,
a photograph of someone standing
in this same kitchen, wearing
an apron I threw out years ago.

The man returns without the dogs.

I save the cantaloupe rind
for my sister's rabbit. The sun
finds the faucet's nickel stain,
dims, goes on.
