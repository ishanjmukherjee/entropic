At five-forty the kitchen bulb hums over the formica.
My father sets a 9/16-inch wrench beside the salt shaker
and unscrews the lid of his Stanley thermos.
The grease in the creases of his knuckles
does not come out with Lava soap.

Outside, the gravel in the alley is frozen to the mud.
A freighter clears the Duluth lift bridge,
its horn rattling the storm window against the sash.
He wraps electrical tape twice
around the split in his right thumb.

Once, at the county fair, he won a goldfish
by dropping a dime onto a floating saucer
and carried the bag home in his shirt pocket
so the water would stay warm.

He pours coffee from the carafe,
caps the thermos, and drops the wrench into his coat.
When he pulls the back door shut behind him,
a smudge of motor oil stays on the white paint
just above the brass handle.
