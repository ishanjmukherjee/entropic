Down Greenwich Street the halogen is leaking
through warehouse windows onto a stack of tires.
A tugboat’s diesel mutters all the way over.
I am counting the holes in the ceiling tile.
Thirteen, plus half a tile. The clock says three
and then it says a little after.
The tiles
have no opinion. Inside the wall, a pipe
gulps once and stops.
A month ago at lunch
I found a magpie skull near a basketball court,
bleached to a chalk-print on the asphalt, paper-
light, eye socket still holding a shadow.
That shadow’s here now, riding the ceiling’s grain;
the truck that backed up beeping at four-fifteen
stalled in my chest and will not start again.
