At twenty past five the boats at Dungeness
are still up on the shingle, leaning on their keels,
each with a rusted cable running
to a winch in a tin shed.

My father sets his thermos on the drum,
unscrews the cup, and pours.
The steam goes sideways, toward France.

From a hut with its door propped on a brick
the radio reads the shipping forecast,
Dover, Wight, south-west five or six,
in the voice of a man who has never been wet.

Behind us the power station keeps its lights on
for nobody. The tide is far out
and coming in the way a dog comes back
when it's called and not ready,
by the long route, stopping at every stone.

He hands me the cup. The rim
takes the skin off my lip, the way it did
when I was nine and stood here in his coat.
He doesn't ask for it back.

At the waterline a work glove, orange,
palm up among the weed,
and the first wave fills it
and lifts each finger, one by one.
