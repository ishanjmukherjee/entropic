The amber light falls soft and low,
Where summer's blaze has dimmed to gold,
The weary winds begin to blow
And whisper stories growing old.

The maple drops its crimson crown
Upon the frost-embroidered ground,
While shadows lengthen, drifting down
In silence, without any sound.

The geese are calling from the height,
Their arrows pointed far from here,
And evening comes a little more near,
A little earlier than last night.

The harvest moon, so round and clear,
Watches the fields let go their grain,
And in the coolness of the year,
We feel both sorrow and its gain—

For all things fall, and yet they rise
In different forms, at different times,
And beauty lives in goodbyes
As surely as in morning's chimes.
