Steel sighs at the corner stop,
its windows glowing amber-gold,
a lantern rolling through the dark
on streets the midnight has holds.

Inside, a scatter of quiet souls—
a nurse with tired eyes,
a boy with headphones drowning out
the city's lullabies.

The driver's hands, steady and old,
guide the beast through rain-slicked streets,
past shuttered shops and sleeping homes
where the porch light rarely greets.

Headlights carve a fleeting path
through puddles mirroring the moon,
each stop a breath, a pause, a door
that opens and closes too soon.

When the last rider steps away
and the engine's hum grows still,
the bus rolls empty to the barn,
keeping time the city will.
