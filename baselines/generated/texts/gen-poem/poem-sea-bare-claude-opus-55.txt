The sea keeps no ledger of the ships it takes,
no list of names, no stone for those who sank;
it folds them under like a bed one makes
each morning, smoothing every wrinkled plank.

It speaks in one long vowel to the shore,
a breath drawn in, a breath let slowly go,
the same word it was saying long before
there was a mouth to answer it below.

At dawn it wears the color of a pearl,
at noon a blue too bright to look upon,
at dusk it takes the sun and lets it curl
to embers, and then swallows it, and then it's gone.

And still the children run to meet its edge,
and still the old men sit and watch it turn,
as if, between the breakers and the ledge,
there were some patient thing it meant to learn,

or teach: that nothing held is ever kept,
that every wave arrives to pull away,
that all the salt of every tear we've wept
is home again, and moving, and at play.
