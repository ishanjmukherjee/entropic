At a quarter to five on Fourth Street in Duluth
you are already in the kitchen,
running the tap until it comes hot
so the thermos won't crack when the coffee goes in.
I hear the lid grind on its bad thread
before I hear you. It has never closed right
since the February it rolled under the truck
at the ore docks. You wrap the neck
in electrical tape and change the tape
on the first of every month.
Down the hill the lift bridge sounds twice
for a laker nobody we know is on.
You set my gloves on the register to warm
and the left one, as always, slides off.
In 1987 my father told me never to marry a man
who whistles indoors. You are whistling
four notes from the gas station radio,
wrong, the same wrong way as yesterday.
Steam fogs the window over the sink.
The coffee climbs brown inside the glass liner.
When I come down you are holding the thermos
against your chest with both hands,
the way you'd hold a bird you found in the road
while you decide whether it will live.
