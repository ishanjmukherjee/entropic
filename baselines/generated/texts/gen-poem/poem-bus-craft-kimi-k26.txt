The 16 halts at Kedzie and Devon at 2 AM.

A man sleeps against the window, his phone
still playing a video, sound off, the screen
painting his cheek in blue checkers.
No one sits beside him. The driver
yawns into her fist at the light.

A woman counts dollar bills, licking her thumb,
once, twice, the same bill. Outside, the dental clinic
is dark except for a scrolling LED sign
promising smiles in Spanish, then English,
then Polish, then dark again.

Three teenagers share earbuds, one wire dangling
to the floor where a straw wrapper breathes
each time the doors fold open. No one boards.
The wrapper stills. Someone has taped
a photograph of a child to the wall above the glove
advertisement, the edges curling, no name written.

At the back a man peels an orange in one long
spiral, the oil catching the streetlamp through the glass.
He eats each wedge slowly, dropping the peel
onto the floor beside his boot. The scent
reaches the woman with the bills. She looks up,
laughs once, returns to counting.

The bus turns west on Peterson. Somewhere
a dog barks at the air brakes. The sleeping man's
phone dies. His face goes out.
The orange man finishes, wipes his fingers
on his jeans, sits with his hands open
in his lap, palms up, as if waiting
for rain, or nothing, or the next thing.
