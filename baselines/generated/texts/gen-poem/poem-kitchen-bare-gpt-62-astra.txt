The kitchen keeps its own small hours:
the kettle’s click, the cooling bread,
a stripe of sun that takes all morning
to cross the table’s wooden grain.

My father pares a single apple,
turning the fruit toward his thumb.
The peel falls whole upon the counter.
We used to clap. Today I do.

He gives me half without a word.
The kettle starts its song again.
Outside, the day has filled with errands.
Inside, we eat down to the seeds.
