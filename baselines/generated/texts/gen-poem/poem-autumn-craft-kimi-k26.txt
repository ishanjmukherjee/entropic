Somewhere between Auxerre and Vézelay, the bus stops. The hour is four.

A man gets off with a cardboard box tied with twine. Inside, something moves.

I stay. The road narrows to a lane between walnut trees.
The leaves are the color of rust on a blade left in rainwater.

A woman in a field bends to lift the last pumpkins.
Her rubber boots make no sound in the wet ground.

I counted fourteen crows on the wire this morning.
Now there are fifteen.

The man left the box at the intersection.
A dog watches from the ditch, not eating what spills.

My hands smell of copper from the latch on the gate.
The woman stands, wipes her forehead with the inside of her wrist.

It is not yet dark.
I can see owls begin in the branches, though they make no sound.

The dog moves. Something is finished in the tall grass.
A truck passes, Geneva plates, carrying apples in open crates.

The woman walks to a house with blue shutters, unlatches the door.
She does not look back at the field.

From the box, nothing.
Or nothing I can hear from where I stand.

The crows drop one by one.
There is a moon, not full, rising over a toolshed with a tin roof.

I find a splinter in my thumb from the bus seat.
I taste it, wood and oil and something else.
