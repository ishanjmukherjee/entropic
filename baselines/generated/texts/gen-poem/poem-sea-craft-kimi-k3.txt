At the Aran Island pier, five a.m. softens
the church bell’s iron to wool.
A drowned doorknob, bright with barnacles,
heaves through the swell beside the currach.
Seaweed hisses against the stone steps,
dark ribbons of kelp knotted in a hand.
The boatman spits into the water;
from the oily circle rises the smell of pennies.
He says the peat smoke in his coat
has begun talking to the gulls.
Nobody believes him, but the gulls
keep landing closer, heads tilted
like old women at a kitchen door.
He cuts the engine. Slap of bilge,
the cormorant’s sudden arrow.
My pocket fills with salt
where last night’s candle leaked.
Far out, the ferry drills a hole
in the day’s pale cloth.
Under the stern, a jellyfish pulses—
tiny, translucent lung
searching for music it lost.
