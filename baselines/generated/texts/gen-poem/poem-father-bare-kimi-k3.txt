These hands have weathered sixty winters’ bite,
A geography of callus, scar, and stain—
Grease mapping knuckles, thorn marks white
At thumb’s edge, topography of pain
And persistence. Once, they feared to hold
The porcelain doll I was, wary of their rough,
Their blunt authority; now, grown old
And somewhat softened, they are enough
To hoist the grandchild, swing her slow
Through autumn’s dying light, or steady me
With one palm’s press against my shoulder’s hollow.
I study how the veins swell, blue as sea
Or rivers on old maps, how fingers curl
Around the coffee cup that trembles slight—
How much of life is grasping at the world
With tools that crack but somehow hold us tight
Through splintered boards and fevered nights.
They taught that love is labor, not the heart
But what the hand performs; that comfort starts
With taking things apart to mend their parts.
