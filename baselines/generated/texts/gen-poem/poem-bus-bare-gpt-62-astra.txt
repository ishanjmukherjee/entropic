At midnight the bus kneels down
for a nurse with a sack of pears.
One pear rolls under a seat.
A boy stops it with his shoe.

“Good catch,” she says, and for a block
they ride with the same small smile.
Outside, the laundromat windows
turn blue, then black, behind them.

At her corner the driver waits
until she gets her key to turn.
Then the doors fold up the cold
and carry the boy toward morning.
