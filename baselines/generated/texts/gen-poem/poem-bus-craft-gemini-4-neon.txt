At 1:15 the 49 swings south onto Western
and the rear axle thumps over the rail spur.
Fluorescents buzz above the ad strip—
an injury lawyer, a trade school, a missing dog
whose collar is red.
Nobody sits in the back.
By the wheel well, a woman in scrubs
sets a plastic bag between her sneakers:
three limes and a roll of electrical tape.
The speaker calls out Augusta. The brakes grab.
A man boards carrying a bicycle wheel
with no bicycle, pays in dimes,
takes the seat behind the driver.
You tell him I'm not coming back for the couch,
someone says into a phone,
then laughs once, like a cough.
Outside, the laundromat at Chicago Avenue
tumbles its dryers for an empty room.
The driver shifts his cap, reaches down,
and bites into an unpeeled cucumber.
At the red light the wipers scrape
a dry windshield, leaving two arcs of dust
across the glass.
