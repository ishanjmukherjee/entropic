The parking meters blink their empty green eyes
along Congress Street where the city workers
have already swept the Thursday market stalls
into canvas bags and left the stems

At seven the air holds the cold
that the brick buildings kept from October
and you carry the wooden ladder
across the empty lot to the plane tree

Your jacket pockets hold the tang
of hardware store nails and the fish sandwich
you bought from the truck parked by the synagogue
where the cantor practices standing on one foot

The ladder vibrates when you drop the wrench
and above you the branches let go
three yellow leaves that spiral down
to rest inside the empty cash box

You think about your father’s hands
how they still smell of turpentine
though he has not painted since the factory closed
and now he carves ducks for children

The cold enters through your boots
while the streetlights buzz awake
and a woman pushes a stroller
full of pumpkin vines across the asphalt

You tighten the bolt on the bracket
and taste the salt on the wind
that has come down from the north
to lace the sidewalk with the first thin ice
