The 49 kneels at Western and Lawrence, 2:40,
and the doors fold open on nobody.
The driver waits anyway, counts to five
by tapping the wheel.

Three seats back a man holds a rice cooker
still in its box, the receipt taped to the lid.
He has it pinned to his lap with both forearms.
At Montrose a woman gets on in a hairnet
and pays in dimes. The machine takes each one
with a click like a swallowed pill.

Nobody pulls the cord for Irving Park.
Lane Tech's field goes by, the goalposts,
the chain-link, a shopping cart on the fifty,
and the man says to the box, not loud,
first in Tagalog, then in English,
you'll like her.

The woman in the hairnet has taken her shoes off.
Her socks do not match: one is a child's,
with a rubber giraffe on the sole.

Past Belmont the driver brakes hard for a red
and his thermos comes off the dash,
rolls down the aisle past all the empty seats,
unscrewed, drawing its thin line of tea
toward the back door.
