At six in the morning in Duluth
my father puts his thumb through my mitten.
We both look at the tip of it.
A fleck of mustard lives under the nail.

He takes the darning egg from the drawer.
It has a face on the back, in ballpoint,
with one ear bigger than the other.
He puts the face inside the mitten.

Twice he wets the yarn and cuts it.
The third time I thread the needle.
He lays the strands across the hole
as far apart as the teeth of his comb,
then works the point across them.

Outside, a plow backs up in three short notes.
He turns the mitten inside out
and cuts the knot close with his pocketknife.
On the table between his hands
the wooden ear with a nick across it.
