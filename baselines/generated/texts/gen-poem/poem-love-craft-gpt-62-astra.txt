At six the shutters go up on the fish stalls in Rialto.
You set your hearing aid in my palm
and pull the wool cap over both ears.
A gull leaves a chicken foot on the weighing pan.
Nobody sees it but us.

The man with the hose goes on hosing the tiles.
You point to the little door on the hearing aid.
My thumbnail fits where yours used to.
The battery rolls into a crease in my palm
and sits there with a grain of salt.

From the paper wheel in your coat pocket
I press out a fresh one, peel off the tab.
While we wait you put your hands in my pockets.
A porter wheels six crates between us and the canal.
“Mind your heels,” he says to my heels.

I lift the edge of your cap and fit the hook.
You turn at the slap of the hose on the tiles.
On the pan beside the stack of weights
three yellow toes curled round a chip of ice.
