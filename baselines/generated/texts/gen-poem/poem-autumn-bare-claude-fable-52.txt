The light comes slanted now, and thin,
a gold that's lost its summer heat;
the maples let their burning in
and spill it, ember-red, at my feet.

A wind arrives with something to say,
rattling the dry stalks of the corn,
and geese in their ragged alphabet
write southward on the smoky morn.

The orchard gives its last sweet weight,
windfalls bruising in the grass,
while spiders string the garden gate
with silver for the frost to glass.

And evening settles early, deep,
with woodsmoke drifting, slow and blue—
the earth is leaning toward its sleep,
and asks the same of me and you.
