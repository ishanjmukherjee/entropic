Before I knew the arithmetic of loss,
or the slow erosion of a name,
I found the map of my becoming
folded in the atlas of your frame.

Your hands, two anchors in the deepening dusk,
your voice, the timber of a waking bell—
I learned the language of a quieter trust,
a syntax where no syllables befell.

Not flame that feeds on air and dies from hunger,
but the low, persistent burning of the earth—
the patient geology of wonder
that knows no famine, only dearth
when you are absent from the morning’s breaking,
when I must build the daylight on my own.
All other suns are imitations, faking
the warmth I have exclusively known.

If time should carve our statues out of silence,
and leave our names for wind to overwrite,
I’ll find you in the particles and islands,
the single, indivisible, shared light.
