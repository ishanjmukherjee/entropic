At 3 a.m. I walk to the kitchen and open the fridge
not for food but for the light the small bulb still holds.
I look at the eggs in their cardboard their quiet speckled weight
and close the door and open it again to watch the light come on.
The kettle has not boiled the cups hang unmoving
the house holds its breath like something that has forgotten itself.
I've counted the cracks in the ceiling above my bed
they number eleven though one might be a shadow.
I take an orange from the bowl and roll it in my palm
the pores catch my thumb like punctuation I cannot read.
Somewhere a car passes and the window briefly glows
then goes back to the color of a tooth.
I put the orange back knowing in an hour the alarm
will ring and the eggs will still be there.
