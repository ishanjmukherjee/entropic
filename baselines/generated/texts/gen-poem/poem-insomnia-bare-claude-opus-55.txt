The clock has swallowed midnight whole, and still
I lie awake and count the ceiling's cracks;
The house exhales; the radiators fill
With ticking ghosts that knock along their tracks.
My mind, a moth, keeps battering the glass
Of every word I wish I'd never said,
And all tomorrow's errands, as they pass,
Parade like soldiers through my aching head.
I turn the pillow to its cooler side
As if relief were something I could flip;
I bargain with the dark, I try to hide
Inside the hush between each faucet drip.
  At last the window grays, the birds begin,
  And now, too late, I feel the tide roll in.
