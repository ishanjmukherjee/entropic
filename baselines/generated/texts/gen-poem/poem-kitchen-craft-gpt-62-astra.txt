At six in the kitchen on Atwells Avenue
my father sets the flour tin in the sink.
He works the lid off with a spoon.
Inside, a bus ticket curls around a tooth.

He puts the ticket and the tooth in a saucer
and goes back to scraping the lid.
Who painted this shut, he says,
with the green paint still on his cuff.

Downstairs the baker rolls up his grate.
A truck backs in. Someone keeps saying
a little more, a little more, whoa.

I hold the tin while my father taps
the dent out from inside with the spoon.
It takes two taps. He gives it ten.
Then he runs his thumb around the rim
and fits the lid without pressing it down.

On the saucer the ticket has opened a little,
one blue corner propped on the tooth.
