The sea keeps no calendar, only the tide,
a slow gray breathing against the stone,
hauling its silver in, letting it slide
back to the deep where the light is unknown.

It carries the names of the ships it has held,
the salt of old storms, the hush after rain,
and every shore that its patience has felled
it gives to another as sand, grain by grain.

At dawn it is glass, at dusk it is fire,
at midnight a field where the moon lays its road;
it asks nothing of us, not grief nor desire,
yet takes what we bring it and lightens the load.

Stand at its edge and the world falls away:
one long horizon, one voice, and the spray.
