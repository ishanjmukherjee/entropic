I didn't know that love could be so small:
the way you leave the kettle half-filled for me,
your coat that slumps beside mine in the hall,
the crossword squares you leave for me to see.

I thought it came in thunder, or in flame,
in letters sealed and sent across the sea,
but mostly it's the way you say my name
when you're half-asleep and reaching out for me.

It's toast gone cold because we talked too long,
your laughter breaking open in the car,
the hum you make, not knowing it's a song,
the way you always know just where things are.

So keep your grand cathedrals and your vows
carved in marble, stubborn as the stone.
I'll take this ordinary here and now,
this kitchen light, this life I call my own.

And if the years should bend us, slow us down,
and silver every strand upon your head,
I'll love you in the quiet of this town
the way I love you now: in what's unsaid.
