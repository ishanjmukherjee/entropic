Heat comes off the iron grill of the floor register
and the cat is a bolster dropped on it.
At the back of the house the kitchen holds
the last of the 2 a.m. dark that Bangor
forgets by morning. The bulb over the sink
is not on. A dishcloth, half-folded, makes
a cairn by the cold tap. In the stillness
a refrigerator cycles its coolant through copper
and a moth, pale as a cuticle, revises its course
along the ceiling. It will not find the window.
The boy comes down for water. His feet sound
separately on each tread. He does not trouble the switch.
By the glow of the clock radio he fills a glass
and drinks until his throat moves a shadow.
Somewhere else in the building a cough is a dry stick.
When he sets the glass on the counter
it sounds one ceramic note. The fly-spots
on the shade of the standing lamp are not words
but a scatter of ink that I read and read.
