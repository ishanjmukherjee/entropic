At three-fourteen the ore boat clears the lift bridge
in Duluth, its engines shaking the glass
of the storm window. I twist the brass valve
on the radiator until my thumb turns gray.
The pipe knocks twice, then settles into a hiss.
Across the hall, a toilet flushes, refills,
and stops. My heel has worn a trough
in the bottom sheet where I keep kicking.
My father died in a room with no windows
and a thermostat bolted inside a plastic box.
I get up, run the kitchen tap, and drink
until my teeth ache. On the linoleum,
a moth drags one wing toward the baseboard,
stopping each time the fridge kicks on.
