At 3:40 the radiator knocks twice
and an ore boat answers from the ship canal,
one long, two short. The lift bridge goes up for it.
From the bed I count the deck lights, nine of them,
sliding behind the Hendersons' chimney.
The water glass on the sill has grown a skin of dust.
I have turned the pillow four times. The clock
lies face-down under a sock, so I take the hour
from the bridge. My mother kept her teeth
in a glass like this one, and spoke to them.
Downstairs the refrigerator starts, then quits.
A plow climbs Lake Avenue with its blade raised,
scraping nothing, and its amber light crosses
the ceiling, the dresser, the back of my hand.
