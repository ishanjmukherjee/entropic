Morning comes in through the window over the sink,
laying its pale hands on the counter's worn edge,
where a ring from last night's cup still holds
the shape of a conversation.

The kettle begins its low complaint,
then rises, insistent, into song.
Steam fogs the glass. A spoon
rings once against the rim, a small bell.

Here the onions have made everyone weep
for no reason worth naming,
and bread has risen under a dish towel
like something quietly deciding to live.

The drawer that sticks, the burner that tilts,
the chair nobody claims but everyone uses:
all of it leans toward the stove's blue flame,
that steady, flickering heart.

And when the dishes are stacked and dripping,
and the light goes amber, then gone,
the room keeps its warmth a while longer,
the way a hand remembers what it held.
