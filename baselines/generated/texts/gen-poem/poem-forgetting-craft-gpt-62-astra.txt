At four in Hull I take the teapot down.
The spout has lost a chip against the sink.
One willow on the lid has lost its crown.

I set two cups out, one of them turned down.
The gas ring catches on the second clink.
At four in Hull I take the teapot down.

Your sugar in its paper has gone brown.
I turn it till I see the café ink.
One willow on the lid has lost its crown.

A man came here to weigh your dressing gown.
He wrote its weight beside the words moth pink.
At four in Hull he took the teapot down.

He asked which kiln, which year, which county town.
He wet his thumb. The blue came off like ink.
One willow on the lid had lost its crown.

I pour until the tea runs off the clown
you painted on the cup you used to drink.
At four in Hull I set the teapot down,
one willow on the lid without its crown.
