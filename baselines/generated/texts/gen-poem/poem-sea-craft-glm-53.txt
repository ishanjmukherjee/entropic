At low tide the flats at Morecambe shine
like a wet roof. A man walks out
past the old lifeguard station, bucket
in one hand, digs for cockles
with his heel where the sand
splits around his boot.

Four o'clock. The light has quit
pretending. It lies flat on the water
and the water takes it
without comment. Out near the channel
a cormorant holds its wings open
like laundry on a line,
drying in a wind nobody feels.

The woman on the promenade bench
peels an orange with her thumbs.
Her dog watches the peel
fall between the slats.
She reads the same page
for a long time.

Gulls argue over something dead
near the ferry slip. Their noise
carries better than the sea does,
which today is only a shuffle,
a turning of pages,

and at the far edge, where the sand
goes back to being water,
a single figure stands so still
he could be planted there,
until he isn't,
until he bends and lifts
something small and shining
into his coat.
