The names go first, and then the faces fade.
I knew a street once; now I'm turned around.
I keep the little light the evening made.

My mother's kitchen, yellow, unafraid,
the radio turned low, the kettle's sound.
The names go first, and then the faces fade.

I set the table out of habit, laid
a place for someone nowhere to be found.
I keep the little light the evening made.

The letters in the drawer are creased and frayed,
their loops and crosses blurring, ink half-drowned.
The names go first, and then the faces fade.

I am not grieving. I am not afraid.
The mind lets go the way rain finds the ground.
I keep the little light the evening made.

And if you come to visit, half-dismayed
to find me smiling, gentle, and unbound,
the names go first, and then the faces fade.
I keep the little light the evening made.
