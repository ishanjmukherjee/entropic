At four in Hull I turn the pillow over.
The seam has left a ladder on my cheek.
A lorry takes the corner by the baths;
its tailgate bangs, then bangs a second time.
I fit my ear into the hollow again.
Behind the wall a man says, “Not the goose.”
A cistern fills. I hold the pillow down
until the feathers stop against my palms.
Then something starts to tick inside the case.
I sit up, peel the cotton from one end
and work a fingernail along the piping.
A beetle grips the pad of my thumb.
I tip it on the sill beside the latch:
six bent pins beneath a grain of coffee.
