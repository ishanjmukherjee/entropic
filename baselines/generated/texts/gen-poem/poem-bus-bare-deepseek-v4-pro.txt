The long day’s fever has finally broken,
its heat a memory the shadows hold.
The engine’s hum, a low and lulling token,
a story in the cooling dark retold.

Fluorescent lights, a pale and sterile river,
wash over empty seats of hardened cloth,
and make the scratches on the window shiver,
a secret language spelling out a loss.

The doors sigh open at a vacant corner,
admitting drafts and one forgotten soul,
a tired-eyed and solitary mourner
disembarking from a vanished role.

Then, gathering itself with diesel breathing,
it lumbers on, a beacon in the gloom,
a slow, terrestrial and heavy-leaving
 Leviathan that owns the empty room.

Past shuttered shops and traffic lights that hover,
unblinking yellow eyes above the lane,
it carries its own light from stop to cover,
and gently rocks against the sleeping pain.

A mobile island briefly at the curbside,
a sanctuary for the ones who ride
through the long dark, content to let the outside
unspool its silent film, nowhere to hide.
