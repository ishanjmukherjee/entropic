At Penzance the fish van parks by the harbour wall.
The driver winds down overtime
and three gulls drop into the tarmac.

I stand at the lock gate at three,
my hands on iron, the tide slapping gravel.
A twelve-bolt hinge holds back nothing.

Something off Keyboard Island is barking.
It keeps barking. The diesel is idling
in a language nobody speaks.

I pick up a mussel shell with a borehole clean through it,
the whelk's acid job, and rub the rim
against my thumb until it warms.

Now the van starts. The gulls lift.
The driver does not look at the water.
The water does not look at him.

A blue bib of diesel where the hull kissed the jetty.
A man coiling hose on a trawler named Boy James.
The barking stops. Something else begins,

or was always continuing under it,
one notch below hearing, the slack rope
trembling on the bollard. I set down the shell.

The numbers on the lock wall: depth, date, initials.
A wasp has drowned in a pint of rainwater.
Two hundred miles east my daughter builds a city

from cereal boxes. She does not know
I am watching the wasp dispute the surface
with no one, winning no one, entering the measure

the tide makes of this hour,
already closing over its yellow and black,
already keeping the ribbon of its legs.
