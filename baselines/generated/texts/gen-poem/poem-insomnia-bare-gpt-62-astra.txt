The clock strikes three. I hear the little spring
Draw back its hammer from the single bell.
By day I never hear the wretched thing;
By night I know its aftershiver well.
I turn my pillow to the cooler side
And shut my eyes against the window square.
A motor passes like a falling tide
And leaves the bedroom floating on the air.
Then, just as I forget to wish for sleep,
A blackbird tries a note and lets it fall.
I wait to hear if he intends to keep
That one clear note, or fashion it a call.
He sings again. The window fills with gray.
I close my eyes and miss the break of day.
