Steam sighs against the window's cold,
a kettle's slow exhalation.
The cutting board bears its scars—
onion, garlic, the ghost of rosemary.

Copper pans hang like harvest moons,
dulled by decades of flame.
The refrigerator hums its single note,
keeping time.

Flour ghosts the counter's edge,
fingerprints of bread still rising somewhere.
The oven light: a small sun
watching dough become crust.

Here the spoons rest in their drawer,
wood worn to the grain of countless hands.
Here the faucet drips its mathematics,
counting the nights no one came.

At 3 AM the toaster ticks,
cooling from no one knows what.
The kitchen stands without witness—
floor tiles holding the weight of standing still,

the avocado's blackened stem,
the milk three days uncertain,
the single bulb over the stove
that sometimes works, sometimes doesn't,

and still someone will enter
barefoot, searching,
refrigerator door thrown wide
against the dark,

its pale stage light catching
only this hunger,
this ordinary miracle
of wanting.
