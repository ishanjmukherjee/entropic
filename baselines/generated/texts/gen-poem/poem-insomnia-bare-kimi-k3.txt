The clock unspools its numbers, one by one,
And shadows stretch like fingers on the wall;
The night, once soft, now weighs a heavy ton—
A tide that neither ebbs nor starts to fall.

I count the stars, the sheep, the hours too
That slip through grasping hands like silver sand;
The darkness holds no dreams, no ship, no crew,
Just silence vast, and thoughts I can’t command.

Oh, Sleep, cruel lover, distant and aloof,
You grant your gifts to children and the just,
But leave me here, awake, without reproof,
To search your face among the ash and dust.

Now dawn approaches with its pale distress,
And finds me still adrift in emptiness.
