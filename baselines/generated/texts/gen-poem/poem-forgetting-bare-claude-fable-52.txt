The names slip first, like water through the hand,
then faces thin to weather and decay;
I hold what's left and cannot understand.

A house, a street, a shoreline's shifting sand,
I knew them once, and knew them yesterday.
The names slip first, like water through the hand.

Somewhere a kitchen, somewhere a brass band,
a woman laughing, turning half away;
I hold what's left and cannot understand.

The mind's a map of half-remembered land
whose roads dissolve in fog and go to gray.
The names slip first, like water through the hand.

I reach for words; they scatter where I stand,
small birds that will not settle, will not stay.
I hold what's left and cannot understand.

No loss arrives the way that it was planned.
It takes the night, and then it takes the day.
The names slip first, like water through the hand;
I hold what's left and cannot understand.
