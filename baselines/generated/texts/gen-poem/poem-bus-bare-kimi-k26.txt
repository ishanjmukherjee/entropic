Neon streaks across its flanks,
a caterpillar of steel and glass,
carrying the city's last wishes home.

Empty seats hold the ghosts
of nine-to-five, the perfume
of someone else's morning.

The driver wears his route
like a monk's devotion—
these stops, his litany,
these doors, his benediction.

At each corner, souls rise
from shadow, feed their coins
into the hunger of the box,
submit to the fluorescent shrine.

Through tunnel and bridge
the bus inhales exhaust,
exhales the warm fug
of humanity still persisting.

A man sleeps against the window,
his cheek flattening against
the cold, entire cities
passing through his dreaming.

Dawn will find this vessel
curled in its depot, breathing
diesel, growing clean again—
but now it swims the dark,
a whale with windows,
minor god of the in-between,
gathering all who have nowhere
to be but moving.
