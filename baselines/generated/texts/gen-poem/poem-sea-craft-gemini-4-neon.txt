At five-fifteen the causeway lamps still burn
above the riprap at Nahant.
The tide has dropped four feet since midnight, leaving
a collar of bladderwrack along the granite
and a zinc anode wedged between two stones,
its center pitted where the salt ate through
to save some trawler's hull.
I turn it in my palm. It weighs as much
as a pear, and leaves a film of grey
across my thumb. Out past the bell buoy
a diesel engine coughs, catches, settles
into a thrum. Two men in oilskins
cross the gravel lot, one saying she never
paid the roofer, not a cent, the other
spitting into the weeds.
The Atlantic does not look like water
at this hour; it looks like the lid of a stove
someone forgot to wipe.
A gull drops a mussel from thirty feet
onto the asphalt, misses the crack,
and circles back to try again.
I set the zinc back where I found it.
Far out, the trawler's masthead light
shrinks to a pinhead, then goes behind
the swell.
