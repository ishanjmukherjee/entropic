I did not know the morning had a sound
until you hummed it, barefoot, half awake,
the kettle ticking as the light came round
to find your shoulder and the steam's slow wake.

I had been fluent in a careful life,
its measured rooms, its doors I kept ajar,
and then you laughed, a small and silver knife
that cut me free of everything too far.

Now ordinary things have learned your name:
the second cup, the coat hung by the stair,
the window where the rain repeats its claim,
the dent your sleeping leaves upon the air.

If I am asked what love is, I will say
it is the way you make a place of day.
