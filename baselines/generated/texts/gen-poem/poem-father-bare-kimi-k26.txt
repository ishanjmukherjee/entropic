Calloused maps of who he was,
the lines deep-trenched with what he did:
dawn grease, fence posts, a daughter's
tricycle trued and trued again.

Winter split them at the knuckles.
He'd wince, not stop, the wood
stacked higher than my childhood.

Those same hands, gentle
as moths at my fevered neck,
or holding a fish just released.

Now when I lift anything
heavy, something in them
remembers him. The weight
not less. Only known.
