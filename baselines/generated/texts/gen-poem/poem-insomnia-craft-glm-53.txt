The two a.m. freight shakes the glass of this
rented room above the Greenville yard.
Outside, a switchman's lantern crossing track—
I count the couplings slamming, car by car,
like sheep that no one anywhere has counted.

The clock my father wound still barks at twelve,
lies to me twice, and I believe it twice.
The radiator knocks its one cold knuckle.
Downstairs the landlord's dog wants out, wants in,
wants out. I know the dog. The dog is right.

At three I peel the orange on the sill,
and all the room takes notice of the smell
the way I used to notice, years ago,
a phone that rang at any hour for me—

and now I eat it, section after section,
watching the switchman walk his lantern home.
