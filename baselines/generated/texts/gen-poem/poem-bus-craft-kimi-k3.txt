At 2:13 the Merrick line idles under the overpass on 17th,
its doors sighing open for a woman with a mop bucket
and a man carrying a hive frame wrapped in burlap.
Inside: tangerine lights, a heater ticking,
the scarred floor where bleach has dulled the blue.
The driver taps the farebox with his ring,
then nods to nobody in the mirror.
A receipt from the laundromat sticks to the window—
three queen-size, one pillowcase—
and the radio says the ports will close at morning.
Steam rises from a Styrofoam cup,
a child sleeps against a hose of language,
the map above the seats missing one cracked county.
When the brakes take, standing bodies lean,
then let go of what they almost had to say.
Rubber, mirrors, money changing temperature in a fist,
the woman lifts her bucket, the man his bees.
Outside the street stays wet and counted: hydrant, mailbox, dark line
where the building holds its own back teeth.
