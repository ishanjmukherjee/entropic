The bowl she kept by the stove at four a.m.
I have it now, chipped where her thumb wore in.
The kettle ticks. The tap stutters, then runs.

Her name goes first. Then the street. Bridge and pier
parse like a map that rain has swollen thin.
The kettle ticks. The tap stutters, then runs.

I set two bowls of oatmeal on the table
and count the birds—that habit still is mine—
before the dark lets go of Harbor Drive.

The neighbor's dog is barking at a plow.
I call him by his name and get it wrong.
The kettle ticks. The drip runs down the drapes.

Outside, the trucks have salted Second Street;
a man scrapes ice and curses slow and clean.
I copy him. It helps. It never helps.

I hold the bowl. My thumbs find her worn place.
The mail arrives. The mailman knows my name—
The kettle ticks. I wave. I don't know him.
