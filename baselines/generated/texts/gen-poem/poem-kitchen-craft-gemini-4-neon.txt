At five-fifteen the stove clock reads five-eighteen.
I run the tap until the pipes stop knocking
and fill the enamel kettle. The chip near the spout
shows iron. Outside, a salt truck grinds
in second gear up Fourth Street, its strobe
sweeping the cabinets, then gone.

The radiator ticks. I strike a match,
hold it to the burner until the ring catches.
On the sill, a geranium drops a leaf
onto the Formica. Beside the dish soap,
my father's dentures sit in a jelly glass.

I open the pantry for the oats.
A mouse has chewed the corner of the box,
leaving a crescent of cardboard on the shelf.
The kettle starts its hiss.
Down by the harbor, a foghorn sounds once
through the storm glass.

I pour the water over the grounds,
watch the crust rise and split.
The linoleum buckles near the fridge
where the defrost pan overflowed in August.
Steam climbs the windowpane and blurs the streetlamp.
