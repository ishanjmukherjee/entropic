At ten past four the ore boat clears Two Harbors
and sounds once, long, for nobody on the pier.
I count the cars that shunt along the trestle
by the clank that runs the length of them to here.
The glass of water I poured at midnight stands
on the sill, a film of dust across the top.
Down in the basement the furnace catches, holds,
and quits, a man on the stairs who changes his mind.
My father loaded taconite thirty winters
on graveyard, and slept at noon with a sock across his eyes
while my mother ran the vacuum round the bed.
I lie on his side now. The lake goes gray.
A gull comes down on the hood of the Buick and walks
the length of it, setting its feet in the frost.
