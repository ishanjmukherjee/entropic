Your laughter spills like morning light
through curtains I forgot to close,
and every ordinary night
becomes a place where wildflowers grow.

I know the way your coffee cools,
the songs you hum when you're not aware,
the quiet language of your rules,
the way you brush unruly hair.

Time moves like honey, slow and gold,
whenever you are standing near.
At forty, sixty, growing old,
I'll still find wonders waiting here—

your hand in mine, a borrowed coat,
the door you always leave unlocked,
a love that needs no grander note
than footsteps sharing one sidewalk.
