The bus exhales at the corner, kneeling
to let one tired passenger aboard.
Its windows hold a row of small aquariums,
each face adrift in a private blue.

Fluorescent light hums over empty seats,
over a glove left behind, a folded paper,
the faint perfume of rain on wool.
The driver nods to no one, pulls away.

Outside, the city slides by in pieces:
a laundromat still spinning its last load,
a neon sign that's lost a letter,
a man asleep beneath a shuttered awning.

Someone rings the bell. The sound is gentle,
a single note that asks for almost nothing.
The doors fold open on a quiet street,
and darkness takes a rider gently in.

Then on again, a lantern on its circuit,
stitching one neighborhood into the next,
carrying the late, the lost, the nearly home
through hours that belong to no one else.
