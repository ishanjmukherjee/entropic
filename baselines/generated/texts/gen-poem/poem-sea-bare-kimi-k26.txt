Salt-throated, the horizon swallows the sun
whole each evening, spits it back bone-white
at dawn. Tide marks write their riddles
on the skin of sand, erased before reading.

What the sea keeps:
the helm of a ship named Perseverance,
a pocket watch still ticking in its tomb,
your voice the last time you called my name
from the deck rail, waving.

What the sea returns:
polished stones, the illogic of driftwood,
seaglass dulled to amber and green,
sometimes a body, sometimes
only the jacket, the shoes.

I have learned to translate
the dialect of gulls, to read
the contract of waves—
how they advance, advance,
then break their own promise.

At night the water invents another darkness.
I wade in to my shoulders,
let the undertow write its counter-argument
against my legs. Not drowning.
Listening. The old negotiations:
breath, salt, the cold grammar of buoyancy.

What the sea forgets
it invents again in fog,
in the particular miss of a bell buoy
clanging its one note
where no chart marks danger or depth.

I collect shells that once housed hunger.
Each is a door the animal installed
then outgrew, abandoned.
I put my ear to their hollow
and hear machinery—my own blood,
the lie we tell about oceans
beginning here, so small,
so desperate for the larger darkness.
