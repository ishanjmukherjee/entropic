Some things I lost I cannot name at all—
The way her laugh would catch, then fall like rain,
What door I opened, what I let fall.

Keys, sure. A number. Voices I recall
but not the face, the street, the year of pain.
Some things I lost I cannot name at all.

A necklace? Or the story that it held,
the picnic or the funeral, the train?
What door I opened, what I let fall.

My mother's hands before they turned so pale,
the song she hummed to bring me home again—
some things I lost I cannot name at all.

The years collect what memory would sell:
this room, that stranger's worried, waiting face.
What door I opened, what I let fall.

Now I forget the thing I meant to tell,
the word for Yes, my own address, my name.
Some things I lost I cannot name at all.
What door. I opened. What I let fall.
