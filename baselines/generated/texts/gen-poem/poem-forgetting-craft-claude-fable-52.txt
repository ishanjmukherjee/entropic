She asks again what time the ferry goes.
Four, I tell her. Four. She hunts the chain
of glasses hanging at her chest. It snows

on Stonington, on lobster traps in rows
along the pier, the co-op's yellow crane.
She asks me what time the ferry goes

and who I am to stand there in her clothes.
The coat is mine. Salt trucks grind up Main.
The glasses swing against her chest. It snows

into the dog's dish by the step. Nobody knows
to bring it in. We dug that grave in rain
ten Aprils back. What time the ferry goes

is taped beside the phone. She reads it, slows,
reads it aloud, then reads it once again,
her glasses, found, low on her nose. It snows.

The horn. She lifts her purse, sets it down, stows
one mitten in the breadbox. At the pane
she asks the glass what time the ferry goes.
The tape curls. Wharf lights come on. It snows.
