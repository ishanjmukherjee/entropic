The maples loosen what they held all summer,
a slow red letting-go along the lane,
and every gust becomes a quiet drummer
tapping the gutters with the coming rain.

The light grows thin and amber in the morning,
it lingers low and leaves the garden early;
the geese write out their long and wavering warning
across a sky gone pale and pearly.

The apples soften sweetly in the grass,
the woodsmoke drifts from chimneys down the hill,
and frost lays down its first thin sheet of glass
on puddles where the stars lie bright and still.

So let the year undress and turn to sleep;
there's beauty in the things we cannot keep.
