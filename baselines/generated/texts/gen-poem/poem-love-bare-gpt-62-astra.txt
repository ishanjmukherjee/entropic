You leave the hallway light on when it rains,
though I could find our door by touch alone.
I know the stair that creaks, the sill that leaks,
the little patch of green the moss has grown.

But coming up the hill with soaking shoes,
I see that square of amber through the trees
and think of you at home among small tasks,
one hand still dusted white, turning the keys.

I used to think that love would change the world.
The rain still finds its way beneath the door.
You lift the towel to let me step across,
then lay it down beside my shoes once more.
