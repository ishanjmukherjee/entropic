By four-fifteen the sun drops behind the ridge
above Superior Street and the lake turns to slate.
A freighter idles past the lift bridge,
its hull riding high, empty of taconite.
On the back porch, a blue milk crate holds
seventeen Macintoshes split from the drop.
Wasps work the brown seams.
Inside, the radiator knocks twice, then settles into a hiss.
Down the alley, someone pulls a chainsaw cord,
lets it sputter, pulls again.
A woman shouts for Buster—
not a dog, a man in a wool shirt
who steps from the garage wiping oil from his thumbs.
The maple by the cistern has given up
half its load to the downspout.
I unscrew the hose from the spigot before the freeze
and loop it over a spike in the shed.
Cold water runs across my boot.
At the kitchen window, the kettle fogs the glass.
On the porch, one wasp climbs the rim of the crate,
stops, and folds its wings.
