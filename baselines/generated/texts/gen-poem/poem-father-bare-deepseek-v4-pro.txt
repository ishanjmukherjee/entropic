These hands that once could span my newborn back,
A trembling cradle, careful, swift to hold
A weight of worry, or a colic’s crack,
Now rest with stories that have all been told.
The knuckles are like weathered, knotted oak,
The veins a map of rivers running slow,
They wear the permanent and gritty smoke
Of bonfires built a thousand moons ago.

A crescent scar from where the hammer slipped,
A thumbnail bruised an old, forgotten hue,
The calloused pads from where a rope was gripped
To anchor what the frantic wind blew through.
These hands could fix a chain on any bike,
Or build a world from scraps of wood and glue,
They were the final word, the one to strike
A bargain with the broken, make it new.

They taught me how to tie a double knot,
And held the seat and ran and let it go,
They pointed to the single star we sought
And showed me what a seed needs to grow.
And even in their stillness, I can trace
The ghost of every gesture, every plight,
The strength that held our whole, unsteady space,
Then gently turned the hallway out the light.
