The kettle clicks off before it boils.
Someone has left the radio low,
a voice from Duluth selling mattresses
to the burner, the sink, the empty stool.

I wipe crumbs from the cutting board—
rye, seeded, gone dark at the edges—
and find a bottle cap under the knife block.
It says try again. The light through the window

has the color of weak tea.
On the counter, a bowl of lemons
from the tree that survived the frost,
their skins cold, their ends still green.

The faucet drips. I count four seconds,
then lose count. A truck downshifts
on the hill. My hands smell of garlic
and the pencil I sharpened this morning.

There is a hairline crack in the blue plate
I mean to throw out and never do.
The oven clock reads 6:47,
but the oven is off, and the kitchen

is filling with the first stars,
which look nothing like stars
through the screen door—
only small holes where the light gets in.
