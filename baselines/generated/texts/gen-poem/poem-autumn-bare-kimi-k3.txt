The apples thicken on the bending bough,
The light falls slant across the empty field,
And something in the air is saying now—
A word the summer knew but kept concealed.

Amber, rust, the slow burn of the maples,
The crisp snap of morning on the lawn,
The geese practice their farewells in staples
Of black ink against the drawing dawn.

We gather in the scattered golden leavings,
Rake the year into a rustling pile,
And stand amid the beautiful bereavings,
Learning how to love the meanwhile.

The pumpkins glow like lanterns on the stoop,
The dusk arrives too early, smelling sweet
Of smoke and cider, pressing us to stoop
And warm our hands where heat and season meet.

Then frost, then rest. The garden bows its head.
The roots go dreaming in the dark below.
Autumn is not a mourning for the dead—
It is the earth letting go.
