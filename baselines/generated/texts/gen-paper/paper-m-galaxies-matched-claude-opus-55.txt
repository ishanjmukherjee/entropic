Submillimeter galaxies (SMGs), first uncovered in deep blank-field surveys with the Submillimetre Common-User Bolometer Array (SCUBA) on the James Clerk Maxwell Telescope (Smail, Ivison & Blain 1997; Hughes et al. 1998; Barger et al. 1998), are among the most extreme star-forming systems in the Universe. With infrared luminosities exceeding 10^12 L_⊙ and inferred star formation rates of hundreds to thousands of solar masses per year, these dust-obscured galaxies contribute a substantial fraction of the cosmic star formation rate density at 1 ≲ z ≲ 4 (Blain et al. 2002; Casey, Narayanan & Cooray 2014). Their high gas masses, compact dust continuum sizes, and redshift distribution peaking near z ≈ 2.5 (Chapman et al. 2005; Simpson et al. 2014; Dudzevičiūtė et al. 2020) have made them leading candidates for the progenitors of the massive, compact quiescent galaxies observed at z ≈ 2 and, ultimately, of the giant elliptical galaxies that dominate the cores of present-day galaxy clusters (Lilly et al. 1999; Toft et al. 2014). If this evolutionary link holds, SMGs should reside preferentially in the densest regions of the high-redshift cosmic web. In that case, they could serve as efficient signposts of forming clusters and of the megaparsec-scale structures that surround them.

Several lines of evidence support this picture. Clustering analyses of single-dish-selected SMGs imply typical host dark matter halo masses of ~10^12–10^13 h^−1 M_⊙ (Blain et al. 2004; Hickox et al. 2012; Wilkinson et al. 2017; Chen et al. 2016). Halos of this mass are expected to evolve into group- and cluster-scale systems by z = 0. A growing number of spectroscopically confirmed protoclusters also host multiple SMGs or dusty star-forming galaxies. Examples include GN20 in GOODS-North (Daddi et al. 2009), AzTEC-3 in COSMOS (Capak et al. 2011), the SSA22 protocluster core (Umehata et al. 2015), and the remarkably dense SPT2349−56 and Distant Red Core systems at z ≈ 4 (Miller et al. 2018; Oteo et al. 2018). These discoveries have encouraged the view that intense, dust-obscured starbursts are a characteristic phase of galaxy assembly in overdense environments.

However, the generality of this association remains contested. Many of the most striking SMG-rich protoclusters were found through targeted follow-up of already exceptional sources, which introduces selection biases that are difficult to quantify. Clustering measurements are themselves sensitive to blending in coarse-resolution single-dish maps, to uncertain redshift distributions, and to the multiplicity of submillimeter sources revealed by interferometric imaging (Karim et al. 2013; Hodge et al. 2013; Stach et al. 2018). On the theoretical side, simulations suggest that SMGs are imperfect tracers of the most massive protoclusters. Their short duty cycles and the stochastic nature of starburst triggering mean that many massive structures may host no detectable SMG at a given epoch, while a significant fraction of SMGs may reside in comparatively modest environments (Miller et al. 2015; Casey 2016). Observationally, statistical studies that place SMGs within the surrounding galaxy density field have yielded mixed conclusions. Some report a mild preference for overdense regions, while others find SMG environments that are broadly consistent with those of field galaxies of similar stellar mass (e.g., Chapman et al. 2009; Smolčić et al. 2017; Cornish et al. 2024). A robust, statistically complete assessment of whether SMGs systematically trace megaparsec-scale large-scale structures is therefore still lacking.

The Cosmic Evolution Survey (COSMOS; Scoville et al. 2007) field offers a unique opportunity to address this question. Its ~2 deg^2 area provides a representative cosmic volume. Its deep multiwavelength imaging and extensive spectroscopic campaigns yield precise photometric redshifts for over a million galaxies (Laigle et al. 2016; Weaver et al. 2022). These data have enabled reconstructions of the galaxy density field and cosmic web out to z ≈ 5 (Scoville et al. 2013; Darvish et al. 2015, 2017). In parallel, wide-area SCUBA-2 mapping (Simpson et al. 2019) and extensive ALMA follow-up and archival programs (Liu et al. 2019; Simpson et al. 2020) have produced large samples of submillimeter sources with arcsecond-precision positions. These positions remove the counterpart ambiguity that plagued earlier single-dish studies and allow reliable multiwavelength identifications and redshift estimates.

In this paper, we exploit these resources to carry out a systematic overdensity analysis of 449 SMGs in COSMOS. Using the latest photometric redshift catalog, we reconstruct the three-dimensional galaxy density field in redshift slices matched to the redshift uncertainties of the SMGs and of the surrounding galaxy population. We then quantify the local environment of each SMG on scales ranging from hundreds of kiloparsecs to several megaparsecs. To isolate any environmental signal intrinsic to the submillimeter-bright phase, we compare the SMG environments against carefully constructed control samples matched in redshift and stellar mass. We further examine how environmental overdensity depends on submillimeter flux density, redshift, and other physical properties. We also assess how often SMGs are associated with known or candidate protoclusters and other large-scale structures in the field.

The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 describes the data and sample selection, and Section 3 presents our methodology for estimating overdensities and constructing control samples. Section 4 presents our results, which we discuss in the context of SMG evolution and protocluster formation in Section 5. We summarize our conclusions in Section 6. Throughout, we adopt a flat ΛCDM cosmology with H_0 = 70 km s^−1 Mpc^−1, Ω_m = 0.3, and Ω_Λ = 0.7, and a Chabrier (2003) initial mass function.
